## 1. Printed width: 0.84 → 0.81\textwidth, labels measured at 8.10 pt

The instruction was to try the width lever first. It was tried, and it is a real
but small lever; the table below is measured, not estimated.

`figures/qcomem_pipeline_r47.pdf` is **317.0 × 172.692 pt** (matplotlib
`bbox_inches="tight"`), authored with every label at a nominal **8.0 pt**.
`\textwidth` is 5.5 true in = 396 pt. A label therefore renders at
`8.0 × (width × 396) / 317`:

| width | printed graphic | predicted label | measured label |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.78\textwidth | 308.88 × 168.27 pt | **7.80 pt** — below the floor | not shipped |
| 0.80\textwidth | 316.80 × 172.58 pt | **7.995 pt** — fractionally below 8 | 8.00 pt |
| **0.81\textwidth** | **320.76 × 174.74 pt** | **8.095 pt** | **8.10 pt (all 46 label words)** |
| 0.84\textwidth (R47/R48) | 332.64 × 181.21 pt | 8.395 pt | 8.40 pt |

Measured from the rendered PDF with `pdftotext -bbox` on page 3: **all 46 label
words in Figure 1 are 8.10 pt**, against body text at 8.91 pt. The only smaller
glyphs inside the figure are the sub/superscripts in $h_j^D$ and $h_j^q$ at
6.60 pt (8 words); R47 recorded the same exception class at 6.2–7.1 pt and it is
the same behaviour as body-text math. No label is below 8 pt, so **no asset was
regenerated** — the R47 files are shipped unmodified, and R47's larger-base-font
fallback was not needed.

0.78 was rejected because it drives the labels to 7.80 pt, which is exactly the
defect R47 fixed. 0.81 is the smallest width in the sanctioned 0.78–0.85 band
that clears 8 pt with margin.

**What the width bought.** Only **6.5 pt** of the saving is graphic height
(181.21 → 174.74 pt). The rest is float packing: measured end-to-end, dropping
0.84 → 0.81 moved the main-text overflow from **365.8 pt to 294.3 pt**, because
at the smaller size the float and the text around it repack on page 3 with far
less unfillable remainder. Widths 0.82–0.85 all measured the same 365.8 pt
overflow; 0.80 measured the same 294.3 pt as 0.81. So 0.81 captures the whole
packing benefit at the larger of the two legible sizes.

**Float placement was also swept**, since packing was worth more than height:
anchors at the end of the Introduction, at Motivation, at Methodology, and at
Sections 4.2/4.3/4.4, each with `[t]`, `[tb]`, `[b]`, `[!t]`, `[!b]`, `[htbp]`.
Every `[t]`-family variant gave the same overflow; `[b]` reduced it to 162.1 pt
but pushed the figure to **page 10**, outside the main text, so it was rejected.
`[t]` after the Introduction was kept: it is the placement nearest the first
reference and it is what R47 used.

## 5. What I judged should not be done

1. **The latency table was not moved out again.** It is the adverse evidence R48
   had just restored to the body; making room for a figure by re-burying it would
   trade one presentation defect for a worse evidence defect. It stays on page 7.
2. **The figure was not shrunk below 8 pt labels.** 0.78\textwidth would have
   rendered the labels at 7.80 pt, reintroducing exactly the defect R47 fixed,
   and it would have bought only 6.5 pt of height over 0.81 anyway.
3. **The figure was not regenerated.** Regeneration was on the table only as the
   fallback for labels below 8 pt, and 0.81\textwidth clears the floor at
   8.10 pt. A compaction pass on the layout was considered and rejected: the
   drawn boxes are already about 8 pt tall around 8 pt text, so a shorter canvas
   would have to shrink either the type or the padding, and the whole realistic
   saving was ~20 pt against a 294 pt bill.
4. **The caption was not trimmed.** Trimming its descriptive first sentence would
   have saved about one line and made the figure harder to read, against a
   reviewer who scored presentation 2 of 4.
5. **No adverse sentence, scope statement, non-claim or limitation was touched**,
   and no number was changed. Everything relocated is recoverable verbatim from
   Appendices A, C, E and G.
6. **Nothing was moved out of the appendix to compensate.** The appendix grew by
   2,483 characters; total page count is unchanged at 41.

The residual cost of this pass, stated plainly: the body's Related Work now
attributes **16** of its citations through Appendix G rather than inline (body
distinct keys 35 → 19; all 39 bibliography entries still cited, BibTeX
`warning$ -- 0`), and
Sec. 4.2's quantizer reimplementation detail now reads from Appendix C. Both are
recoverable in one page-turn, and neither is evidence against the paper. That was
the cheapest 294 pt available that did not touch the latency table, the
dispersion panel, the A2 interval, the A4 correction, or any limitation.
