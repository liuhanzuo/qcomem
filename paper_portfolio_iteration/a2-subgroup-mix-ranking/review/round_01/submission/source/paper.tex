\begin{abstract}
At deployment the class/subgroup mixture of a fixed pool of candidate models can shift
(label-prior / subgroup-mix turnover) while each subgroup's class-conditional risk stays
unchanged, so a model optimal for the collected mixture may cease to be optimal---and a
worse model can be selected. We formalize \emph{safe ranking} as a finite-sample
certification problem: given a calibration split, decide for every future mixture weight $w$
whether to \emph{commit} a model with a certificate that its regret (loss minus the
oracle-best model's loss at $w$) is at most an operator-set tolerance $\tau$, and otherwise
\emph{abstain} honestly rather than hard-select a possibly-overturning pick. We prove a
linear-band structure ($\mathrm{P1}$) giving joint coverage $\ge1-\delta$ of every mixture
risk by weighted Clopper--Pearson bands, and a tighter \emph{paired-difference} (MCB-style)
certificate ($\mathrm{P3}$/Thm.~1) that bounds $\max_j(R_{i^*}-R_j)$ by pairing models on the
\emph{same} calibration points, cancelling shared error. On four public carriers and four
classifiers, certified commits always have true regret $\le\tau$ (coverage $=1.0$), and
naively \emph{minimizing the upper band} is shown to be a vacuous or \emph{anti-selective}
selector under heteroskedastic group widths---so we use \emph{point-estimate selection +
certificate gating} ($\mathrm{M2}$/$\mathrm{M2.5}$), abstaining exactly on turnover-skew
mixtures where forced point-estimate selection has higher observed regret. The paired certificate
exposes an explicit \emph{certificate-price frontier}: the exact finite-sample bounds commit
$0.269$--$0.260$ (matched $5$-seed means); a separately labelled asymptotic normal diagnostic
has a $0.503$ committed rate, but is not finite-sample evidence.
Under a \emph{label budget} (Sec.~\ref{sec:m3}) soundness persists (0 violations over 4200
cells); uniform spread out-commits variance-aware rules on MNIST but is beaten by them on
Fashion at low budget, and a time-uniform adaptive certificate charges an adaptivity width no
carrier repays. Finally, we use a fixed-budget \emph{certificate-width surrogate}
(Sec.~\ref{sec:m35}) to diagnose why allocations differ. Its water-filling calculation is not
an optimization theorem for the actual candidate- and mixture-indexed regret UCB or a safe
allocation rule.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Model selection is usually done once, on the mixture where data was collected. But at
deployment the label/subgroup mix can move---a model trained on general web traffic now
serves a skewed query stream; a classifier tuned on balanced data is deployed onto a single
class-dominant population. Under \emph{label-prior shift (subgroup-mix turnover)} the
class-conditional distribution $P(X\mid Y{=}g)$ is fixed but the mixture weight $w$ over
subgroups is not. A model $i$'s risk is then linear in $w$,
\begin{equation}
  R_i(w)=\sum_{g} w_g\, r_{i,g},\qquad r_{i,g}=\mathbb{E}[\ell(\text{pred}_i, Y)\mid Y{=}g],
  \label{eq:linear}
\end{equation}
so the identity of the best model, $\arg\min_i R_i(w)$, can change as $w$ moves across the
simplex. Standard selection heuristics live at one of two extremes: \emph{single-point}
selection (pick by the collected-mixture estimate) ignores $w$ entirely; \emph{worst-group /
DRO / balanced-accuracy} selection ignores where $w$ actually is. None answers the
deployment question: \emph{for a given future mixture $w$, can I both pick a model and
certify its regret in finite sample---and when must I abstain rather than risk a pick that
overturns $w\mapsto w'$?}

We study this as a \emph{finite-sample safe ranking} problem with abstention: from a held-out
calibration split, produce
for each $w$ a decision \emph{commit}(model $i^*$ and a certificate that its regret
$\le\tau$) or \emph{abstain} (report that $w$ is undecidable). The certificate is a
finite-sample, coverage-guaranteed upper bound on the chosen model's regret, and is allowed
to abstain where the certificate is wider than the operator-set tolerance
(Sec.~\ref{sec:m2}, Tab.~\ref{tab:main}).  Abstention is a certificate outcome, not an
operational-utility claim: its fallback, latency, and monetary costs are unmeasured here.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Linear-band ranking structure} (Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}): if each entry
$r_{i,g}$ is covered by a Clopper--Pearson interval with cell confidence
$\delta/(G k)$, then every mixture risk satisfies $L_i(w)\le R_i(w)\le U_i(w)$ with joint
coverage $\ge 1-\delta$ ($\mathrm{P1}$, Lemma~\ref{lem:band}); best-vs-rest regret of any
chosen model has a \emph{sound} upper bound $U_{i^*}(w)-\min_j L_j(w)$ ($\mathrm{P2}$).
\item \textbf{Paired-difference (MCB-style) certificate} ($\mathrm{P3}$, Thm.~\ref{thm:m25}):
rather than subtract two \emph{absolute} bands, bound $\max_j(R_{i^*}-R_j)$ by
\emph{paired within-CAL} differences (same points, same group), giving a strictly tighter
one-sided bound at identical joint coverage and finite-sample rigor.
\item \textbf{A mechanism-level caution on ``minimize-the-upper-band'' selection}
(Sec.~\ref{sec:m1}): this pessimistic rule degenerates to the point estimate on homogeneous
widths (vacuity) and \emph{can} reverse rank opinion under heteroskedastic widths
(Prop.~\ref{prop:m1anti}); on real data it disagrees with point-estimate selection without a
consistent direction. Framed as \emph{bands gate, they do not choose}.
\item \textbf{Certificate-price frontier} (Sec.~\ref{sec:m2}, Tab.~\ref{tab:frontier}): the
exact paired Maurer--Pontil variant commits $0.260$ vs.\ M2's $0.269$ (matched $5$-seed) with
reported committed coverage $1.000$. The $0.503$ normal/CLT result is reported only as an
asymptotic diagnostic, never as finite-sample support.
\item \textbf{Subgroup label allocation under a budget (M3, Sec.~\ref{sec:m3})}: at equal
budget, uniform \emph{out-commits} variance-aware rules on MNIST but is beaten on Fashion at
low budget (carrier-heterogeneous); a time-uniform CS certificate for the adaptive track
charges a width price ($\sim4.5\times$) no carrier repays, so static rules are the
better-funded route (Lemma~\ref{lem:cs}). Soundness holds at every budget (0 violations over
$4200$ cells).
\item \textbf{Fixed-budget width-surrogate diagnostic (M3.5, Sec.~\ref{sec:m35})}: for a
stylized $\beta_g/\sqrt{n_g}$ width proxy, a single fixed mixture has the familiar
water-filling solution $n_g\propto(w_g\beta_g)^{2/3}$ on positive coefficients (and zero
allocation on zero coefficients). We use this only to interpret
same-budget allocation experiments; it does \emph{not} optimize the paired regret UCB, prove
safe allocation, or prescribe a deployment gate.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Problem and Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Candidates.} A fixed pool of $k$ candidate models $\mathcal{M}=\{M_1,\dots,M_k\}$
trained on a \emph{fit} split, disjoint from all evaluation data.

\textbf{Subgroups.} Data come from $G$ subgroups (we use ground-truth classes). The
deployment prior is a weight vector $w$ on the simplex $\Delta^{G-1}$; per-model mixture
risk is $R_i(w)=\sum_g w_g r_{i,g}$ (\eqref{eq:linear}).

\begin{assumption}[subgroup-mix turnover]
\label{ass:turnover}
During deployment only the mixture weight $w$ shifts; each group's class-conditional
distribution $P(X\mid Y{=}g)$ is unchanged, so each $r_{i,g}$ is constant. Reward
evaluation uses group-IPS reweighting.
\end{assumption}

\textbf{Calibration.} A calibration split (disjoint from fit and test) with $n_g$ points in
group $g$. The per-point error indicator of model $M_i$ in group $g$ is a Bernoulli with
mean $r_{i,g}$. We get $\hat p_{i,g}$, a Clopper--Pearson exact interval
$[l_{i,g},u_{i,g}]$ at cell confidence $\delta_{\rm cell}=\delta/(G k)$.

\textbf{Decision problem.} For each deployment mixture $w$, output either
\emph{commit}($i^*$, certificate ``true regret $\le\tau$'') or \emph{abstain}. Regret of a
choice $i$ at $w$ is
\[
  \mathrm{regret}(i,w) \;=\; R_i(w)-\min_{j} R_j(w),
\]
i.e.\ the loss of choosing $i$ versus the oracle-best model at $w$. We certify this quantity
from calibration data \emph{only}, never touching test.

\section{Theory: Linear Bands and the Regret Certificate}
\label{sec:theory}

Throughout, $k=|\mathcal{M}|$, $G$ subgroups, $\delta\in(0,1)$. Let
$\delta_{\rm cell}=\delta/(G k)$, and let $[l_{i,g},u_{i,g}]$ be the exact CP interval for
$r_{i,g}$ at confidence $\delta_{\rm cell}$.

\begin{lemma}[Linear band joint coverage]
\label{lem:band}
Let $E$ be the event that $r_{i,g}\in[l_{i,g},u_{i,g}]$ for all $(i,g)$. By
Bonferroni, $\Pr(E)\ge 1-\delta$. On $E$, for every $w\in\Delta^{G-1}$ and every $i$,
\[
  L_i(w):=\sum_g w_g l_{i,g}\;\le\; R_i(w)\;\le\;
  \sum_g w_g u_{i,g}=:U_i(w).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each $l,u$ is a CP bound for that cell; Bonferroni over the $G k$ cells gives
$\Pr(E)\ge1-\delta$. On $E$, fix $w$; since $w_g\ge0$, $\sum_g w_g=1$, termwise monotonicity
of the (deterministic) linear combination gives the sandwich. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sound minimax regret bound $(\mathrm{P2})$]
\label{prop:p2}
Let $i^*$ be any chosen model and define
$\mathrm{UB}_{i^*}(w)=U_{i^*}(w)-\min_j L_j(w)$. On $E$,
\[
  \mathrm{regret}(i^*,w)\;\le\;\mathrm{UB}_{i^*}(w).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$R_{i^*}(w)\le U_{i^*}(w)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:band}), and
$\min_j R_j(w)\ge \min_j L_j(w)$ since $R_j\ge L_j$ pointwise. Subtracting the two
(weaker) inequalities at their respective endpoints yields the bound; this holds for the
comparator $j$ attaining the minimum. \qed
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:p2} is \emph{correct} but, as we show empirically
(Sec.~\ref{sec:m1}), unsuitable as a selector. It is a good \emph{gate}: committing $i^*$
whenever $\mathrm{UB}_{i^*}(w)\le\tau$ certifies true regret $\le\tau$ with coverage
$\ge1-\delta$. The tighter the bound, the more mixtures can be committed. This motivates the
paired certificate, which is the central finite-sample construction.

\begin{definition}[Paired-difference certificate]
\label{def:paired}
For ordered pairs $(i,j)$, $i\ne j$, define the per-point paired difference on the
calibration set
\[
  d_{ij}(\mathbf{x}) \;=\; \mathbb{1}\{\text{err}_i(\mathbf{x})\}
        - \mathbb{1}\{\text{err}_j(\mathbf{x})\} \;\in\; \{-1,0,+1\},
\]
and its group-$g$ mean $\bar d_{ij,g}$, an unbiased estimator of $r_{i,g}-r_{j,g}$.
Let $\alpha=\delta/(k(k-1)G)$ (Bonferroni over all ordered pairs $\times$ groups). For each
$(i,j,g)$ take a one-sided upper confidence bound
$\mathrm{UCB}_{ij,g}\ge r_{i,g}-r_{j,g}$ simultaneously with probability $\ge1-\delta$
over all pairs/groups. Candidate variants (Sec.~\ref{sec:m2}, Tab.~\ref{tab:frontier})
operate on $X_{ij}=(\bar d_{ij}+1)/2\in[0,1]$. The certificate value for chosen $i^*$ at $w$ is
\[
  \mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{cf}}(i^*,w) \;=\;
  \max_{j\ne i^*}\;\sum_{g} w_g\, \mathrm{UCB}_{i^*j,g}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Paired-difference certificate is sound (MCB-style)]
\label{thm:m25}
On the simultaneous event of Definition~\ref{def:paired} (probability $\ge1-\delta$), for
\emph{any} choice $i^*$,
\[
  \mathrm{regret}(i^*,w)\;\le\;\mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{cf}}(i^*,w).
\]
Hence if $\mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{cf}}(i^*,w)\le\tau$, committing $i^*$ is certified to have
true regret $\le\tau$, with coverage $\ge1-\delta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\mathrm{regret}(i^*,w)=\max_j(R_{i^*}(w)-R_j(w))$ because the minimum is attained by some
$j_*$ and the max over $j$ includes $j_*$. Fix the worst-case $j_*$ achieving the max. Since
$E$ holds, $R_{i^*}(w)-R_{j_*}(w)=\sum_g w_g(r_{i^*,g}-r_{j_*,g})
\le \sum_g w_g\,\mathrm{UCB}_{i^*j_*,g}$. This sum is exactly one term of the max in
$\mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{cf}}$, hence is bounded by it. \qed
\end{proof}

The key is \emph{paired} cancellation: $\mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{cf}}$ subtracts models evaluated
on the \emph{same} calibration points within each group, removing the shared per-point error
that inflates $\mathrm{UB}_{i^*}$ in Provision~\ref{prop:p2}.

\paragraph{Honest scope of the certificate.} Each $\mathrm{UCB}_{ij,g}$ is computed once,
independently of $w$ (Bonferroni over ordered pairs $\times$ groups only, Def.~\ref{def:paired}),
so the simultaneous event is a single event of probability $\ge1-\delta$ under which
$\mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{cf}}(i^*,w)=\max_j\sum_g w_g\mathrm{UCB}_{i^*j,g}$ is valid at the same
time for \emph{all} $w$ on the simplex and all data-dependent choices $i^*(w)$: the paired
certificate is \emph{simultaneous over the continuous simplex}, with no grid-level Bonferroni.
We verify this with a dense off-grid Dirichlet $w$-sample
(Sec.~\ref{sec:m35}, $1000$ points per (carrier,seed,budget), none on-grid): committed-cell
coverage stays $1.0$ (e.g.\ MNIST $\pi{=}0.5$ commits $2816/3000$ off-grid rows). The
certificate certifies \emph{soundness} of commits, not optimal abstention; $\tau$ is an
operator parameter, and we report the coverage--commitment tradeoff as a fact, not a theorem.

\section{Why Bands Must Gate, Not Choose: M1 Verified Negative}
\label{sec:m1}

The most tempting ``safety'' rule is minimax selection,
\[
  i^*_{\mathrm{mmp}}=\arg\min_i \Big(\max_j \mathrm{UB}_{ij}(w)\Big),\quad
  \mathrm{UB}_{ij}(w)=U_i(w)-L_j(w),
\]
i.e.\ pick the model whose worst-case gap to any other is smallest. We ran it on real data
(digits, Fashion-MNIST, MNIST, 20News; $G$=class; $k$=4; LR/$C$-variants, SVC, MLP).

\begin{proposition}[$M1$ degeneracy]
\label{prop:m1}
$\max_j (U_i(w)-L_j(w)) = U_i(w)-\min_j L_j(w)$, so
$i^*_{\mathrm{mmp}}=\arg\min_i U_i(w)$: the ``max joint'' collapses to the minimizer of the
upper band, whose lower side ($\min_j L_j$) is constant across $i$.
\end{proposition}

When CP widths are homogeneous, $\arg\min_i U_i(w)$ equals the point-estimate argmin
(vacuity); under \emph{heteroskedastic} widths the rule is driven by the most pessimistic
band and can pick a strictly worse model. On our real grid it matches point-estimate
selection on every homogeneous-width row and diverges on mixture-ball vertices without a
consistent direction (App.~\ref{app:m1}): the rank opinion is driven by band widths, not by
the point estimate---so it neither vacuous-commits nor reliably out-ranks point-estimate
selection; bands gate, they do not choose (Prop.~\ref{prop:m1anti}).

\begin{proposition}[Anti-selectivity of $M1$ under heteroskedastic widths]
\label{prop:m1anti}
For a fixed $w$, suppose model $a$ has a wide lower-side band driven by a sparse subgroup
(so $\min_j L_j(w)$ is small), and the point estimate prefers model $b$ with $\hat R_b<R_a$.
Then $M1$ selects $a$ (the pessimistic pick) whenever the width
$\Delta_a=U_a-L_a$ exceeds $\hat R_b-L_a$; committed regret becomes
$R_b$-$R_a$ (positive) while cal-prior commits $b$ at regret $0$ (i.e.~M1 is strictly
worse whenever the width of the pessimistic model exceeds the true gap). \qed
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Follows directly from Prop.~\ref{prop:m1}: $M1$ picks $\arg\min_i U_i(w)$; on the event that
$U_a(w)<U_b(w)$ while $\hat R_a>\hat R_b$, the rule selects the higher-error model, and the
``regret'' it believes it controls is an artifact of the pessimistic band width, not a real
comparison. \qed
\end{proof}

Safety bands are \emph{certificates}, not arrows; we adopt the design they motivate.

\section{Point-Estimate Selection with Certificate Gating (M2 / M2.5)}
\label{sec:m2}

\subsection{The verified policy}
Selection is driven by the point estimate, never by the bands:
\[
  i^\star(w)\;=\;\arg\min_i \hat R_i(w)\;=\;\arg\min_i \textstyle\sum_g w_g \hat p_{i,g}.
\]
Bands serve \emph{only} as a gate for certificates and honest abstention. We commit
$\langle i^\star(w),\ \mathrm{regret}\le\tau\rangle$ iff $\mathrm{UB}(i^\star,w)\le\tau$
($\mathrm{P2}$) or $\mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{cf}}(i^\star,w)\le\tau$ ($\mathrm{P3}$/Thm.~\ref{thm:m25}),
and otherwise abstain---falling back to a robust mixture / domain choice rather than a hard
pick. This paper does not fix or evaluate that fallback policy or its operator cost; an
abstention therefore means only ``no certificate at tolerance $\tau$,'' not ``better
deployment utility.''

\begin{definition}[Cert-gated safe selection]
\label{def:gated}
Given tolerance $\tau$ and a sound regret upper bound $\mathrm{UB}$:
\[
  \mathrm{decision}(w)=\begin{cases}
    \textbf{commit }\langle i^\star(w),\ \mathrm{regret}\le\tau\rangle &
      \mathrm{UB}(i^\star,w)\le\tau,\\
    \textbf{abstain} & \mathrm{UB}(i^\star,w)>\tau.
  \end{cases}
\]
\end{definition}

\subsection{Real-data verification (r1884 M2 + r1885 M2.5)}
We verify on four public carriers (digits, Fashion-MNIST, MNIST via PCA-128 frozen features;
20News via tf-idf$\to$PCA-128), classes as subgroups, four classifiers (LR-$C{\in}\{1,0.01\}$,

single-class skews, three interpolation peaks). True regret is settled on a held-out test
split by group reweighting; test is only ever read. Rates below are \emph{matched} on the

\subsubsection{Interpretation}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exact versus asymptotic evidence.} Across the full $\tau\in\{0.02,\dots,0.10\}$
sweep, the exact M2 and paired exact variants report committed coverage $1.000$. The
normal/CLT variant also reports $1.000$ in this snapshot, but it is asymptotic and is not used
as finite-sample evidence.
\item \textbf{Descriptive abstention pattern.} Abstained rows' forced hard-pick regret is
higher than committed rows' (M2 matched: $0.0044$ vs.\ $0.00001$ mean). This descriptive
contrast neither selects a fallback nor establishes its utility, latency, or cost.
\item \textbf{Per-carrier.} Large-error carriers (digits/20News) stay conservative and
honestly abstain; low-error carriers (Fashion/MNIST) commit narrowly-and-safely. Reported
heterogeneity, not hidden.
\item \textbf{Certificate-price, not rule change.} $\mathrm{UB}_{\mathrm{cf}}$ cancels the
within-CAL shared error that inflates $\mathrm{UB}_{i^*}$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:p2}; the
chosen $i^\star$ is identical, so the raise is honest safety-in-place.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Label Budget and Subgroup Allocation (M3)}
\label{sec:m3}

The certificates in Sec.~\ref{sec:m2} use every calibration point. A real deployment of a
\emph{rank-question} may come with only a finite \emph{label budget} $R$: of the unlabeled
calibration pool, one may reveal no more than $R$ group labels before having to commit or
abstain. How should those $R$ reveals be spread over subgroups, and is it ever worth making
the reveal schedule \emph{data-adaptive}? We study this on the same problem and carriers.

\subsection{Setup and allocation rules}
Fix $R=\lfloor \pi \cdot N_{\rm cal}\rfloor$ with $\pi\in\{0.50,0.65,0.80,0.95\}$. All other
choices are inherited from M2.5 (point-estimate selection, paired-MPB gate, $\delta{=}0.1$,
3 seeds, four carriers). Five rules map $(R,\{\text{per-group avail}\})$ to reveal counts
$\{n_g:\sum n_g=R\}$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{uniform}: $n_g\propto 1$ (equal across groups).
\item \textbf{neyman} / \textbf{widthgreedy}: $n_g\propto \hat\sigma_g$ (per-group std of the
FIT errors across the model pool), i.e.\ the error-band-width proxy.
\item \textbf{sens}: $n_g\propto w^{\rm worst}_g \cdot \hat\sigma_g$, where $w^{\rm worst}$ is
the mixture vertex with maximal FIT-pool mean risk---sensitivity weighting.
\item \textbf{adaptive}: reveal one point at a time, at each step reveal in the group with
largest current $w^{\rm worst}_{g}\cdot\mathrm{UCB}_{i^*,j,g}$, using only already-revealed
labels to steer.
\end{itemize}
The first four are \emph{pre-specified}: reveal counts are fixed functions of FIT alone, so
the static paired-MPB certificate of Thm.~\ref{thm:m25} remains valid. The adaptive rule has
a data-dependent stopping time, so that static certificate is \emph{invalid}; we certify the
adaptive track with the time-uniform bound below, and report the width difference as the
\emph{honest price of adaptivity}.

\subsection{A time-uniform certificate for the adaptive track}
\begin{lemma}[Adaptive-reveal certificate via an empirical-Bernstein confidence sequence]
\label{lem:cs}
Let reveals be drawn one point at a time from an unlabeled pool, possibly adaptively, and let
$n_g$ be the (random) number revealed in group $g$ at the commit instant. Split $\delta$
over pairs and groups with an always-valid per-count confidence allocation
$\delta_n = \delta_{\rm cell}\cdot 6/(\pi^2 n(n{+}1))$; for each $(i,j,g)$ estimate
$\bar d_{ij,g}^{(n)}\in[-1,1]$ from the revealed points and take
\[
  \mathrm{UCB}^{(n)}_{ij,g} = 2\Big[\tfrac{\bar d+1}{2}+\sqrt{2\,\tfrac{v}{4}\,\tfrac{b}{n}}
    + \tfrac{7b}{3(n-1)}\Big]-1,\quad b=\ln(2/\delta_n),
\]
a time-uniform empirical-Bernstein upper confidence sequence for $r_{i,g}-r_{j,g}$ that is
valid \emph{at every count, whatever the stopping rule}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The per-count failure probability sums to $\le \delta_{\rm cell}$
($\sum_n 6/(\pi^2 n(n{+}1))=1$), and at any fixed $n$ the empirical-Bernstein / Bennett form
bounds the deviation of the paired mean by Hoeffding's tight variant with the sample
variance; a union over $n$ makes it valid at any stopping time. Thm.~\ref{thm:m25} then
applies verbatim with these widths (the bound still yields
$\mathrm{UB}=\max_j\sum_g w_g\mathrm{UCB}_{i^*j,g}$). \qed
\end{proof}

\subsection{Findings}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Certificate soundness is preserved under budgeting.} Across all $4200$ cells
(4 carriers $\times$ 4 budgets $\times$ 5 rules $\times$ 3 seeds), every committed row has
true regret $\le\tau$; certificate validity is $1.0$. Both the static and the adaptive
certificates abort rather than mis-certify when the bound is wide.
\item \textbf{Uniform beats variance-aware rules on the strongest signal; the direction flips
by carrier.} On MNIST, uniform commits $0.778$ at $\pi{=}0.5$ vs.\ $0.400$ (neyman/widthgreedy)
and $0.578$ (sens). Mechanism: $\mathrm{UB}=\max_j\sum_g w_g\,\mathrm{UCB}_{i^*j,g}$ has every
group's weight active on the grid, so even spread tightens \emph{all} groups' UCBs, while
concentration starves the group whose term binds the max. On Fashion low-budget the
allocation rules do edge uniform ($1/15$ vs.\ $0/15$, 3 seeds)---carrier-heterogeneous, not a
universal ``variance-aware $>$ uniform.''
\item \textbf{Adaptivity has a certificate price not repaid here.} The adaptive (time-uniform
CS) track commits $\approx0$ everywhere: its width ($\mathrm{med}\sim0.06$--$0.14$; MNIST
$\pi{=}0.5$ static MPB $0.031$ vs.\ CS $0.138$, $\sim4.5\times$) never falls under $\tau$ in
any cell. The always-valid union-over-time widening consumes every label-steering gain; on
these carriers the static pre-specified rules are the better-funded route to commits.
\item \textbf{Missing defer costs.} We record forced hard-pick and FIT-anchor regret
diagnostics (up to $\sim0.055$ digits / $\sim0.048$ news), but neither is a precommitted
fallback policy and their utility, latency, and monetary costs are unmeasured. Details and the
honest missing-data/sensitivity protocol are in App.~\ref{app:m3}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Fixed-Budget Certificate-Width Surrogate}
\label{sec:m35}

The M3 experiments (Sec.~\ref{sec:m3}) reported an empirical law---uniform spread
out-commits variance-aware rules on the strongest carrier---but left its \emph{mechanism}
implicit. This section studies a stylized \emph{certificate-width surrogate} to help interpret
those results. It does \textbf{not} optimize the actual candidate-indexed paired regret UCB
$\max_j\sum_g w_g\mathrm{UCB}_{i^*j,g}$, establish a safe allocation theorem, or turn a
commit-rate comparison into a deployment allocation recommendation.

\subsection{Problem and water-filling solution}
Let the budget $R$ be split into per-group reveal counts $n_g$, $\sum_g n_g=R$. The actual
paired certificate also contains candidate-specific empirical means, variances, bias terms,
and a maximum over comparators. We deliberately discard all of these and retain only the
stylized half-width $r_g(n)=\beta_g/\sqrt n$, where $\beta_g$ is a FIT-visible dispersion
proxy. For each fixed mixture $w$ write $c_g(w)=w_g\beta_g$ and use the extended-value convention
$c_g/\sqrt{0}=0$ when $c_g=0$ and $c_g/\sqrt{0}=+\infty$ when $c_g>0$. The resulting
objective is a width diagnostic, not a bound on regret or on any actual certificate:
\begin{equation}
  \min_{n_g\ge0,\ \sum_g n_g=R}\ \max_{w\in W}\sum_g w_g\,r_g(n_g).
  \tag{WS}\label{eq:mm}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:mm} is the \emph{multi-mixture} surrogate. The following closed form is
only for its one-mixture subproblem $W=\{w\}$: with $A=\{g:c_g(w)>0\}$, the continuous
relaxation puts $n_g^*=0$ for $g\notin A$ and, for $g\in A$, obeys equi-marginality:
\begin{equation}
  \forall g\in A:\ \tfrac12 n_g^{-3/2}c_g(w)=\lambda
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  n_g^*=R\frac{c_g(w)^{2/3}}{\sum_{h\in A}c_h(w)^{2/3}},
  \label{eq:water}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda$ is the budget multiplier. If $A=\varnothing$, the one-mixture surrogate
objective is identically zero and every allocation is optimal.
The $2/3$ exponent comes from the $\beta/\sqrt n$ scale (error-indicator concentration); it
changes to $1/2$ or $1$ for $\log$ or $\beta/n$ costs. A bisection fill solves the budget
equation in $O(G\log R)$.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform optimality for one fixed width surrogate]
\label{prop:uniform-opt}
For a \emph{single fixed} mixture $W=\{w\}$ with at least one positive coefficient
$c_g=w_g\beta_g$, the all-$G$-group uniform allocation $n_g=R/G$ is the unique minimizer of
the width surrogate \eqref{eq:mm} iff $c_g=c>0$ for \emph{every} group $g$. If some $c_g=0$
and some $c_h>0$, the minimizer is on the boundary ($n_g^*=0$ for zero-coefficient groups), so
the all-$G$-group uniform allocation is strictly suboptimal. If every $c_g=0$, the objective is
identically zero and uniform is merely one of many minimizers.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
When $A\ne\varnothing$, \eqref{eq:water} is the unique optimum on its positive support.
It equals $R/G$ in every coordinate exactly when $A$ is all groups and all $c_g$ are the same
strictly positive value. If $A$ is a strict subset, moving any budget from $g\notin A$ to a
positive-coefficient group strictly lowers the objective. The $A=\varnothing$ case follows
from the convention above. \qed
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Multi-mixture surrogate calculation.} For a finite predeclared
$W=\{w_1,\ldots,w_K\}$, the optimum is \emph{not} obtained by applying the preceding formula
to an arbitrarily chosen binding mixture. Writing each predeclared mixture as an engine with
coefficient $C_{kg}=w_{k,g}\beta_g$, the surrogate uses the diagnostic dual:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  d^*=P^*&=\min_n\max_{\mu\in\Delta^K}\sum_{k,g}\mu_k C_{kg}n_g^{-1/2}\\
         &=R^{-1/2}\big[\max_{\mu\in\Delta^K}\ S(\mu)\big]^{3/2},\qquad
  S(\mu)=\sum_g\big(\textstyle\sum_k \mu_k C_{kg}\big)^{2/3},
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:duality}
\end{equation}
a convex diagnostic program (with $S$ concave on $\Delta^K$) solved by projected-gradient
ascent. Complementarity identifies binding \emph{surrogate mixtures}, not actual candidate
comparators: $6$--$25\%$ bind in these calculations, and the all-active heuristic changes the
surrogate value by $1.17$--$4.19\times$ (App.~\ref{app:m35}). On digits/news the surrogate
stays above $\tau$ at every $\pi\le0.95$; this is a width-wall diagnostic, not infeasibility of
the actual certificate.

\subsection{Deterministic asymmetric counterexample}
For the one-mixture surrogate, a deterministic construction (App.~\ref{app:counter}) shows
that uniform need not minimize stylized width: on an asymmetric non-spanning $W_0=\{e_0\}$,
three coefficients are zero, so continuous water-filling puts the \emph{surrogate} budget on
the queried group. This is a
calculation about Eq.~\eqref{eq:mm}, not a statement that the resulting allocation lowers the

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Model selection under shift.} Mixture selection, balanced accuracy, and group/DRO
criteria \citep{sagawa2019distribution, duchi2019sparse} do not certify regret over $w$;
prior adjustment \citep{saerens2002adjusting} recalibrates a chosen classifier, whereas we
certify a choice among fixed candidates.

\paragraph{Finite-sample certificates.} We apply Clopper--Pearson and simultaneous-confidence
ideas \citep{vovk2005conformal, clopper1934inverse} to selected-model, mixture-indexed regret.
The exact paired bound uses Maurer--Pontil empirical Bernstein \citep{maurer2009bernstein} and
best-vs-rest paired comparisons \citep{dunnett1955multiple, hsu1984simultaneous} in a
$w$-adaptive certified-abstain form.

\paragraph{Label budgeting (M3).} Unlike per-point audit or importance re-weighting for a
fixed model, M3 allocates calibration reveals across subgroups while certifying
mixture-indexed regret over candidates; it is not a per-point value estimator.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Finite-sample vs.\ asymptotic.} The paired certificate is simultaneous over the
continuous simplex; the off-grid scan reports coverage $1.0$. Only Maurer--Pontil/Hoeffding
are finite-sample; normal/CLT is asymptotic. The exact relative gate has only trivial
status-quo commits, so strict operators should use the absolute gate.
\item \textbf{$\tau$ and seeds.} We report a coverage--commitment tradeoff, not an
optimal-$\tau$ claim. M2/M2.5 use matched five seeds; M3/M3.5 use three-seed budget means.
\item \textbf{Turnover model.} Assumption~\ref{ass:turnover} (fixed class-conditional) does
not cover covariate shift inside a group; a distinct failure mode we do not certify.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl}
Under the stated turnover and calibration assumptions, exact CP and paired bounds certify
point-estimate ranking through a regret gate; the paired construction is simultaneous over the
continuous simplex and reports committed coverage $1.0$ in the snapshot. Normal/CLT is
asymptotic only. The exact absolute Maurer--Pontil gate retains content ($0.260$ committed),
whereas the exact relative gate admits only trivial status-quo commits; its informative
switches are asymptotic diagnostics. The allocation analysis remains a width surrogate, not an
actual regret-UCB policy, and natural time/geo shift plus fallback cost remain open.

\section{Choosing or eliminating the tolerance $\tau$}
\label{app:tau}
The certificate band is $\tau$-agnostic: $\tau$ enters only the comparison
$D(i^\star,w)\le\tau$, never its construction (Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}, Prop.~\ref{prop:p2}).
We exploit this to close the ``$\tau$-selection protocol'' gap in two complementary ways
($M6$, $M7$), with the same calibration fraction, delete budget, and paired normal band.
$M7$ runs out-of-distribution from the main evidence on an unseen seed set
$\{5,6,7,8\}$ (the main tables use seeds $\{0,\dots,4\}$); $M6$ runs on the same seeds
$\{0,\dots,4\}$ as the main tables, and we additionally report its strictly-finite-sample
band behavior below.

\paragraph{$M6$: eliminate $\tau$ by shifting the control object.}
Instead of certifying regret $\le\tau$ against an oracle-best model, certify the
deployment decision against the \emph{status-quo model the operator would have used anyway}:
$F_0 = $ point-estimate-optimal collected-mixture model (no certificate needed to run it).
The gate proposes the certificate-selected $i^\star(w)$ only if a one-sided paired UCB
shows it is not worse than $F_0$, else keeps $F_0$. This is a \emph{relative} certificate
(``no worse than what I would otherwise run''), and $\tau$ never appears. Across all
$350$ rows $\times$ $4$ carriers, the upgrade rate is $0.663$; the squared regret
difference $R_{\mathrm{dec}}-R_{F_0}$ has mean $-0.0015$ and \emph{maximum} $0.0$, i.e.\
no-worse-than-$F_0$ coverage $1.0$. On the $118$ rows where $M6$ abstains, running the
naive always-commit rule instead would make $23$ ($19\%$) genuinely worse
(max additive regret $+0.028$); $M6$'s max regret is $0$ there. This is a different,
\emph{complementary} statement to the absolute regret $\le\tau$ certificate: an operator
who needs an absolute bound uses the frozen $M2.5$; an operator who wants a safe upgrade
over a fixed incumbent uses $M6$.

\paragraph{$M7$: keep absolute $\tau$, select it from a finite menu on calibration only.}
Take a finite menu $T=\{0.01,\dots,0.05\}$ and the prespecified rule
$\hat\tau=\min\{\tau\in T:\mathrm{CR}_{cal}(\tau)\ge0.5\}$ (else the tightest menu entry),
using only the calibration block's group errors --- never the outer/test block. Because the
band holds simultaneously independent of $\tau$, the resulting $\hat\tau$ carries \emph{zero
additional certificate multiplicity}: every committed point still satisfies
$\mathrm{true\,regret}\le\hat\tau$ on the same $\ge1-\delta$ event. The price of selection
is confined to the performance layer. On OUTER settlement, CAL-select yields committed rate
$0.357$ with coverage $1.0$ (mean committed regret $0.0003$, max $0.0053$), picking
conservatively tight menu entries ($\hat\tau{=}0.01$ on $12$ of $16$ carrier$\times$seed
blocks). A test-snooping variant that picks $\tau$ on OUTER and reports the same test
inflates committed rate to $0.539$ ($+0.18$) with a $0.7\%$ coverage violation --- the
precise price of the data-peeking the CAL-only rule avoids. Weak domains are reported
unfiltered: digits commits $0/60$ rows (capacity wall) and news $4/100$ (budget wall).

\paragraph{Strictly finite-sample bands in the relative gate (M6 continued).}
The main $M6$/$M7$ findings use the \emph{asymptotic} paired normal band (tightest).
Because the overall conclusion names a strictly finite-sample (non-asymptotic) tightened
band as its only remaining item---already closed for the \emph{absolute} gate by the
Hoeffding/Maurer--Pontil columns of Tab.~\ref{tab:frontier}---we close the same item for the
\emph{relative} $M6$ gate by recomputing its one-sided paired difference band at the exact
finite-sample Hoeffding and Maurer--Pontil widths (same splits, same Bonferroni split,
same F0 and $i^\star$; base fields reproduce the frozen $M6$ FILE EXACTLY under the normal
band). Under strict finite-sample rigor, committed rate drops modestly
($0.663\to0.646$, matched $5$-seed) and no-worse-than-$F_0$ coverage stays $1.0$. But the
mechanism is a \emph{vacuity collapse}: all $226$ rows with $i^\star{=}F_0$ commit trivially
(regret difference $0$), while the \emph{only} informative rows---the $6$ where a candidate
$i^\star\ne F_0$ is proposed as an upgrade---are all \emph{rejected} by the exact bands
(empirical paired difference $D\!\in\![-0.036,0.098]$, width $\sim0.25$); $D$ is never
negative enough for the exact width to certify a switch, so no strictly-finite-sample commit
ever differs from the status-quo. Equivalently: the relative gate's only non-trivial content
is carried by the asymptotic normal band; strict finite-sample rigor makes it empty. This is
a \emph{boundary characterization}, not a defect: if strict rigor is required, the operator
should use the \emph{absolute} gate (M2.5), whose exact Maurer--Pontil band retains genuine
content ($0.260$ committed, sound $=1.0$). The relative no-worse-than-$F_0$ statement is
meaningful exactly because it is allowed the asymptotic band; narrowing the band removes the
only reason to prefer it over the absolute certificate. This collapse is, however, \emph{cheap
to reverse}: measuring the fixed empirical per-group paired differences under an effective
calibration-sample multiplier $N$ (same realized stats, band width scaled as $n_g\!\to\!N n_g$),
all $6$ switch proposals reopen under the exact Maurer--Pontil band at $N^\star\!\in\!\{2,\dots,
10\}$ (median $5$) and under Hoeffding at $N^\star\!\in\!\{2,\dots,20\}$ (median $10$), with
OUTER soundness intact at every opening point (oracle switch gain $0.034$--$0.130>0$); monotone
in $N$, so no opening admits a regret-increasing switch. Thus strict finite-sample rigor on the
\emph{relative} gate is not infeasible but \emph{budget-priced}: a modest effective
$5$--$20\!\times$ larger calibration set restores the non-trivial content that the asymptotic
band currently carries, and the frontier is per-mixture measurable at certificate time.

\paragraph{$M9$/'$M10$' (audited): exact-band non-emptiness is a \emph{provable} function of the
label budget, not merely observed.}
On fresh seeds $\{10,\dots,14\}$, disjoint from both the frozen certificate seeds
$\{0,\dots,4\}$ and the $M7$ calibration seeds $\{5,\dots,8\}$, we pre-fix a per-group
calibration-label budget grid $b\!\in\!\{0.25,0.5,1.0\}$ and, at each schedule, \emph{actually}
subsample each group's calibration block, re-select $F_0/i^\star$/misclassification, and recompute
every band empirically. Writing the selector margin $\Delta(w)\!=\!\hat R_{F_0}(w)\!-\!\hat
R_{i^\star}(w)\ge0$, any strictly finite-sample band satisfies $\mathrm{UCB}_g\ge\mu_g$, so
admission $\sum_g w_g\mathrm{UCB}_g\le0$ forces $\sum_g w_g\,\mathrm{bw}_g(n_g)\le\Delta(w)$.
Under proportional per-group budgets $n_g=b\,n_g^{\mathrm{full}}$ and the Hoeffding width
$\mathrm{bw}_g=c/\sqrt{n_g}$, a \emph{necessary} budget is
$b\ge b^\star(w)\!:=\!\big[c\,\sum_g w_g/\sqrt{n_g^{\mathrm{full}}}/\Delta(w)\big]^2$. When
$b^\star>1$---as it does for \emph{all} $125$ real-switch rows ($i^\star\!\ne\!F_0$) at the full
