011                                             A BSTRACT
012
013           We study an analysis model for repeated model rollouts: a count-exchangeable
014           binary pass-count replay in which a task’s N outcomes are available only through
015           its pass count. In that model, we give a mixture-posterior stopper (BAYES-H)
016           whose per-problem conditional flip certificate is exact: given a FIT-frozen prior
017
              H over per-task pass counts, stop as soon as the posterior probability that the
              full-N binary pass-count decision will disagree with the current prefix decision
018
              drops below α. By the tower property the replay-population flip rate of any such
019
              prefix-measurable rule is bounded by α without a union bound over stopping times
020           (adaptive is free). Under independent i.i.d. FIT/CAL task sampling, the popula-
021           tion layer adds an empirical-Bernstein CAL step with Bonferroni control of the
022           pre-declared frozen rule family. On 11,607 math problems with N = 32 rollouts
023           each (OpenMathReasoning), selected and evaluated under a strict FIT/CAL/TEST
024           problem split with a single held-out readout, BAYES-H has an 81% replay rollout-
025           count reduction at realized flip rate 0.025 ≤ α = 0.05 and 73% at α = 0.02,
026           dominating the strongest distribution-free baseline (Hoeffding-fixed-k: 16% at
027           α = 0.05) with a statistically significant paired gap, and the strongest mixture-
028
              aware fixed budget (empirical-Bernstein fixed-k: 47%). An ESC-style window
              heuristic with an 86% replay count reduction exceeds 0.05 (0.058 > 0.05). Un-
029
              der pre-fixed synthetic ordered-drift mechanisms (front-loading, linear trend, ad-
030
              versarial block swap), BAYES-H is the most robust stopper in the reported har-
031           ness. The displayed certificate-margin thresholds are exploratory descriptive sen-
032           sitivity results, not frozen-selection or confirmatory claims. The supplied replay
033           readout replicates on a disjoint second shard; transferring one fitted calibration
034           mixture across shards (TV 0.042) moves its reported quantities by ≤ 0.4 points.
035           This is deterministic fitted-mixture sensitivity, not a population-transfer certifi-
036           cate. Two further model–carrier pairs bracket the mechanism: on OpenR1-Math-
037           220k (DeepSeek-R1, M = 2 per problem) the certificate attains the replay risk–
038           count frontier exactly when the mixture structure allows, and never stops under
039
              the stated replay certificate when it cannot; on RLVE (Qwen3-4B, N = 8 per
              problem, verifiable environments) it lands at the intermediate point the mecha-
040
              nism predicts — 51–58% replay count reduction at α ∈ {0.10, 0.05}, 45–48% at
041
              α ∈ {0.02, 0.01}, certified at every level down to α = 0.01 where no distribution-
042           free replay budget is certifiable. Every reported flip is disagreement between cor-
043           responding binary pass-count replay decisions, and every saving is 1 − k̄/N ; these
044           artifacts do not measure a delivered-answer majority, gold correctness, generated
045           tokens, latency, cancellation, or ordered online deployment.

      We make two structural observations that reframe the problem. First, on real carriers the dominant
      deviation from i.i.d. is not latent within-task correlation but across-task heterogeneity: per-task pass
085
      rates are strongly bimodal (Section 3), and the flip probability a stopper must control is a functional
086
      of the mixture of per-task difficulty, not of a pooled correlation coefficient. Pooled second-moment
087   summaries can be simultaneously over-conservative and uncalibrated (Section 3). Second, once the
088   target is the mixture, the correct object is a per-prefix conditional certificate: the posterior probabil-
089   ity, given the observed prefix, that the full-N majority disagrees. This object is exactly computable
090   (hypergeometric posterior), its fixed-budget expectation EH [fK (k)] is monotone in the prefix length
091   (Lemma 1), and — because the tower property collapses the stopping-time dependence — it certifies
092   any adaptive rule at level α with no union bound (Theorem 1).

120   2    R ELATED WORK
121
122
      Adaptive sampling for voting. ASC (Aggarwal et al., 2023) adapts a per-problem sample count
123   using a lightweight stopping criterion, and ESC (Li et al., 2024) proposes early stopping for self-
124   consistency. We do not establish a finite-sample disagreement-with-full-vote guarantee for either;
125   on our supplied heterogeneous replay carrier, the instantiated ESC-style rule exceeds 0.05 (Table 1).
126   MARS (Chen et al., 2026) is a parallel trace-stopping method that bounds future vote movement;
127   our work is instead a sequential count-exchangeable replay certificate. Best-of-n and budget-forcing
128   methods (Snell et al., 2025) tune compute without stopping certificates.
129   Conformal and anytime inference. Conformal prediction (Vovk et al., 2005) gives marginal cov-
130   erage under exchangeability; confidence sequences (Howard et al., 2021) give anytime-valid bounds
131   for i.i.d. means. Our certificate is neither: it is an exact posterior flip probability under a calibrated
132   discrete prior, valid conditionally per prefix (hence adaptivity is free), with the mixture estimated
133   from calibration problems under an empirical-Bernstein bound (Maurer & Pontil, 2009). The Bon-
134   ferroni control of a small pre-declared rule family is deliberately conservative and transparent.
135   Dependent sampling. Design-effect corrections (Kish, 1965) shrink effective sample size by an
136   intraclass correlation; we show pooled correlation is the wrong functional here (Section 3). Beta-
137   binomial and correlated-Bernoulli models (Skellam, 1948) capture overdispersion but target estima-
138   tion, not stopping certificates for the flip event.
139
140   Feature boundary. Table 2 makes the source-verified distinction compact: the proposed object
141   is a specified count-exchangeable full-vote replay endpoint with a calibrated-mixture posterior flip
142   certificate. It is not a general ordered-online early-stopping, conformal, or anytime-inference guar-
143   antee.

145   3    M ODEL : COUNT- EXCHANGEABLE REPLAY
146
147   A task’s N rollout outcomes are summarized by the pass count K ∈ {0, . . . , N }. Across tasks,
148   K ∼ H, a mixture capturing both task heterogeneity and any within-task correlation: the probability
149   a fresh rollout passes, marginally, is EH [K]/N , and the joint law of the N outcomes is exchangeable
150   within a task given its realized count. Analysis model (replay): the stopper observes a uniform
151   random prefix drawn without replacement from the task’s N outcomes — i.e., the first k draws are
152   exchangeable with the remaining N − k. Section 7 stress-tests departures from this model (ordered
153   drift); our validity claims are stated relative to it, and the carrier limitation (counts only, no order) is
154   made explicit in Section 8.
155   Let MV(k) = 1{xk > k/2} with ties assigned to the negative side, and let MV(N ) = 1{K >
156   N/2} be the full-N binary pass-count replay decision. It is not an aggregation of delivered textual
157   answers or an actual delivered answer-majority: the replay model contains only binary pass/fail
158   outcomes. The flip event of a stopping rule τ is {MV(τ ) ̸= MV(N )}.
159   Remark 1 (Why the mixture, not the correlation). On τ 2 -bench carriers (6 public task suites, 4 roll-
160   outs/task) we measured pooled within-task reward correlation ρ̂ = 0.47–0.59, but a task-demeaned
161   (fixed-effect) estimate gives ρ̂FE ≈ −1/3 ≈ −1/(N − 1), the demeaning artifact floor: essen-
      tially all observed correlation is across-task heterogeneity, not latent within-task dependence. On


                                                           3
162
      OpenMathReasoning the per-task pass-rate variance exceeds the binomial baseline by 34×. The
163   flip probability is driven by the mass of tasks with K ≈ N/2 — a mixture functional — so pooled
164   second-moment discounts are the wrong certificate statistic.

448   8    L IMITATIONS
449
450   (i) The public carrier provides pass counts, not ordered rollouts: all main results are exact only under
451   the count-exchangeable replay model; the synthetic stress mechanisms of Section 7 only character-
452   ize specified departures from that model. The certificate is a replay-model conditional certificate,
453
      not an e-process: validity under arbitrary online stopping times in ordered deployment is outside the
      stated claims. No supplied run compares the stopped answer with gold correctness, or records gener-
454
      ated tokens, latency, cancellation acknowledgement, or post-cancellation work; full-vote agreement
455
      and 1− k̄/N cannot substitute for those endpoints. (ii) The mixture prior is estimated from 4000 cal-
456   ibration problems of the same distribution; adversarial mixture shift beyond the tested transfer range
457   can break the certificate; the exact critical radii of Proposition 1 give deterministic frozen-rule sen-
458   sitivity radii over an assumed fitted-mixture TV ball (τ ∗ = 0.024–0.157 across levels and shards);
459   they do not establish target-population containment. Within that assumed ball the failure bound-
460   ary is sharp — at α = 0.01 on shard 1 the observed cross-shard mismatch 0.042 already exceeds
461   τ ∗ (0.01) = 0.024; and the radii are rule-conditional: τ ∗ (α) is a staircase with jumps at the certifi-
462   cate thresholds (Appendix E), so the level α should be read as choosing a step of that staircase, not
463   a point of a smooth trade-off. Along the estimation axis, Appendix E(e) shows τ ∗ is not monotone
464
      in the prior-fit size m; the decomposition of Appendix E(f) attributes this to the rule the fit induces
      (freezing the prior leaves the non-monotonicity intact; freezing the rule removes the largest dips),
465
      not the prior’s sampling error. Appendix E(g) now records only an oracle K-wise profile-capping
466
      sensitivity: its stored subset is selected separately after conditioning on the final count K. Because
467   that final count is unavailable at a prefix, this diagnostic is neither a common prefix-state rule nor an
468   instance of Theorem 1; it supports no stopping-policy, cost, or robustness-repair claim. Appendix F
469   collects every in-distribution readout and its failure boundary in one matrix. (iii) Lemma 1’s mono-
470   tonicity is proved in closed form (Appendix A) and independently verified exhaustively at N = 32.
471   (iv) Main-table counts come from one model–task pair (Qwen2.5-Math-72B-TIR, math problems);
472   generality across problem pools is supported by the disjoint second-shard replication and cross-shard
473   prior transfer, and generality across models and task families by two further model–carrier pairs —
474   DeepSeek-R1 rollouts on OpenR1-Math-220k (M = 2), on which the certificate attains its replay
475
      risk–count frontier, and Qwen3-4B rollouts on RLVE verifiable environments (N = 8), on which it
      is certified at every tested level and interpolates with the resolvable mixture structure (Section 6). (v)
476
      At k = N , the observed pass count equals K, so the binary pass-count replay decision agrees with
477
      itself and cH (xN , N ) = 0. Terminal stops add zero replay flip for every true count; this endpoint
478   convention is shared by Theorem 1 and the DP in Appendix B.

638   tower argument applies to this bounded stopping time, and

                     P(flip) = E cH (xτ , τ ) = E cH 1{τ < N } + E cH 1{τ = N } .
