011                                               A BSTRACT
012
013            Repeatedly sampling a model and taking a majority vote (self-consistency) is a
014            standard way to spend inference compute, but the vote rarely needs all N samples.
015            We give a mixture-posterior stopper (BAYES-H) whose per-problem conditional
016            flip certificate is exact: given a calibration prior H over per-task pass counts, stop
017            as soon as the posterior probability that the full-N majority will disagree with
018            the current prefix majority drops below α. By the tower property the population
019
               flip rate of any such rule is bounded by α without a union bound over stopping
               times (adaptive is free), and the unknown-mixture layer adds only an empirical-
020
               Bernstein calibration step over problems, with Bonferroni control of the whole
021
               searched rule family. On 11,607 math problems with N = 32 rollouts each (Open-
022            MathReasoning), selected and evaluated under a strict FIT/CAL/TEST problem
023            split with a single held-out readout, BAYES-H has an 81% replay rollout-count
024            reduction at realized flip rate 0.025 ≤ α = 0.05 and 73% at α = 0.02, dominating
025            the strongest distribution-free baseline (Hoeffding-fixed-k: 16% at α = 0.05) with
026            a statistically significant paired gap, and the strongest mixture-aware fixed budget
027            (empirical-Bernstein fixed-k: 47%). An ESC-style window heuristic with an 86%
028            replay count reduction exceeds 0.05 (0.058 > 0.05). Under pre-fixed synthetic
029            ordered-drift mechanisms (front-loading, linear trend, adversarial block swap),
030
               BAYES-H is the most robust stopper in the reported harness, and a simple cer-
               tificate margin restores flip control only over stated mechanism–drift ranges. All
031
               conclusions replicate on a disjoint second shard, and survive transferring the cali-
032
               bration prior across shards (total-variation 0.042) with ≤ 0.4 points of movement.
033            Two further model–carrier pairs bracket the mechanism: on OpenR1-Math-220k
034            (DeepSeek-R1, M = 2 per problem) the certificate attains the replay risk–count
035            frontier exactly when the mixture structure allows, and never stops under the stated
036            replay certificate when it cannot; on RLVE (Qwen3-4B, N = 8 per problem, ver-
037            ifiable environments) it lands at the intermediate point the mechanism predicts —
038            51–58% replay count reduction at α ∈ {0.10, 0.05}, 45–48% at α ∈ {0.02, 0.01},
039            certified at every level down to α = 0.01 where no distribution-free replay budget
040            is certifiable. Every reported flip is disagreement between corresponding binary
041
               pass-count replay decisions, and every saving is 1 − k̄/N ; these artifacts do not
               measure a delivered-answer majority, gold correctness, generated tokens, latency,
042
               cancellation, or ordered online deployment.

046   1   I NTRODUCTION
047
048   Self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023) replaces one sampled answer by a majority vote over N sam-
049   ples. Adaptive variants reduce or reallocate sampling: ASC (Aggarwal et al., 2023) adapts a per-
050   problem count with a lightweight stopping criterion, ESC (Li et al., 2024) proposes early stopping
051   for self-consistency, and MARS (Chen et al., 2026) stops parallel traces using a conservative bound
052   on future vote movement. For a count-only replay analysis, two deployment factors remain outside
053   its evidence: (i) within-task rollouts may be correlated; and (ii) chronological generation order may
      drift, breaking an exchangeability-based certificate.

079
080   We make two structural observations that reframe the problem. First, on real carriers the dominant
081   deviation from i.i.d. is not latent within-task correlation but across-task heterogeneity: per-task pass
082   rates are strongly bimodal (Section 8), and the flip probability a stopper must control is a functional
083   of the mixture of per-task difficulty, not of a pooled correlation coefficient. Pooled second-moment
084   summaries can be simultaneously over-conservative and uncalibrated (Section 8). Second, once the
085   target is the mixture, the correct object is a per-prefix conditional certificate: the posterior probabil-
086   ity, given the observed prefix, that the full-N majority disagrees. This object is exactly computable
087   (hypergeometric posterior), its fixed-budget expectation EH [fK (k)] is monotone in the prefix length
088
      (Lemma 1), and — because the tower property collapses the stopping-time dependence — it certifies
      any adaptive rule at level α with no union bound (Theorem 1).

      Contributions.
091
092   1. Framework. A count-exchangeable replay model in which both within-task correlation and
093      across-task heterogeneity are folded into a mixture H over per-task pass counts; the certificate
094      target is the flip probability under H, unifying the two effects (Section 3).
095   2. Method. BAYES-H: stop when the posterior conditional flip probability cH (x, k) ≤ α. Exact
096      certificates (Lemma 1, Theorem 1); adaptivity is free — no union bound, no e-process tuning
097      (Section 4).

099      get × level for fixed-k; level for BAYES-H) is selected on CAL with simultaneous empirical-

114   2    R ELATED WORK
115
116   Adaptive sampling for voting. ASC (Aggarwal et al., 2023) adapts a per-problem sample count
117
      using a lightweight stopping criterion, and ESC (Li et al., 2024) proposes early stopping for self-
      consistency. We do not establish a finite-sample disagreement-with-full-vote guarantee for either;
118
      on our supplied heterogeneous replay carrier, the instantiated ESC-style rule exceeds 0.05 (Table 1).
119
      MARS (Chen et al., 2026) is a parallel trace-stopping method that bounds future vote movement;
120   our work is instead a sequential count-exchangeable replay certificate. Best-of-n and budget-forcing
121   methods (Snell et al., 2025) tune compute without stopping certificates.
122
123
      Conformal and anytime inference. Conformal prediction (Vovk et al., 2005) gives marginal cov-
      erage under exchangeability; confidence sequences (Howard et al., 2021) give anytime-valid bounds
124
      for i.i.d. means. Our certificate is neither: it is an exact posterior flip probability under a calibrated
125
      discrete prior, valid conditionally per prefix (hence adaptivity is free), with the mixture estimated
126   from calibration problems under an empirical-Bernstein bound (Maurer & Pontil, 2009). The Bon-
127   ferroni control of a small pre-declared rule family is deliberately conservative and transparent.
128
129
      Dependent sampling. Design-effect corrections (Kish, 1965) shrink effective sample size by an
      intraclass correlation; we show pooled correlation is the wrong functional here (Section 8). Beta-
130
      binomial and correlated-Bernoulli models (Skellam, 1948) capture overdispersion but target estima-
131
      tion, not stopping certificates for the flip event.

134   3    M ODEL : COUNT- EXCHANGEABLE REPLAY
135
136   A task’s N rollout outcomes are summarized by the pass count K ∈ {0, . . . , N }. Across tasks,
137   K ∼ H, a mixture capturing both task heterogeneity and any within-task correlation: the probability
138   a fresh rollout passes, marginally, is EH [K]/N , and the joint law of the N outcomes is exchangeable
139   within a task given its realized count. Analysis model (replay): the stopper observes a uniform
140
      random prefix drawn without replacement from the task’s N outcomes — i.e., the first k draws are
      exchangeable with the remaining N − k. Section 7 stress-tests departures from this model (ordered
141
      drift); our validity claims are stated relative to it, and the carrier limitation (counts only, no order) is
142
      made explicit in Section 9.
143
144   Let MV(k) = 1{xk > k/2} with ties assigned to the negative side, and let MV(N ) = 1{K >
145   N/2} be the full-N binary pass-count replay decision. It is not an aggregation of delivered textual
146
      answers or an actual delivered answer-majority: the replay model contains only binary pass/fail
      outcomes. The flip event of a stopping rule τ is {MV(τ ) ̸= MV(N )}.
147
148   Remark 1 (Why the mixture, not the correlation). On τ 2 -bench carriers (6 public task suites, 4 roll-
149
      outs/task) we measured pooled within-task reward correlation ρ̂ = 0.47–0.59, but a task-demeaned
      (fixed-effect) estimate gives ρ̂FE ≈ −1/3 ≈ −1/(N − 1), the demeaning artifact floor: essen-
150
      tially all observed correlation is across-task heterogeneity, not latent within-task dependence. On
151
      OpenMathReasoning the per-task pass-rate variance exceeds the binomial baseline by 34×. The
152   flip probability is driven by the mass of tasks with K ≈ N/2 — a mixture functional — so pooled
153   second-moment discounts are the wrong certificate statistic.

403
404   The replay model assumes prefixes are exchangeable with the held remainder. Because the carrier
405   contains no observed chronology, we apply three pre-fixed synthetic drift mechanisms to its true

408
      draw pass-rate trend pj = K/N + δ(2j/(N −1)−1)), E3 (adversarial block: the last ⌈δN ⌉ draws

410   Findings (full tables in Appendix C): (i) all methods break under E1 by δ = 0.10–0.15 in this
411   harness; the certificate is honest about its premise — E1 violates exchangeability by construction;

414
      breaks at δ = 0.20 (flip 0.104; under E3 WINDOW3 stays valid throughout, its last-window rule
      never reaching the drifting tail); at α = 0.05 under E3 it breaks only at δ = 0.15 (flip 0.052),
415
      while FIXED-EB survives (it stops at k = 17, having seen half the block); (iii) validity under drift
416
      is bought by margin: stopping only when cH ≤ α − γ restores validity at a quantified replay-

      the count-exchangeable replay model; the synthetic stress mechanisms of Section 7 only character-

445   calibration problems of the same distribution; adversarial mixture shift beyond the tested transfer

447   (τ ∗ = 0.024–0.157 across levels and shards) and show the failure boundary is sharp — at α = 0.01
448   on shard 1 the observed cross-shard mismatch 0.042 already exceeds τ ∗ (0.01) = 0.024; and the
449
      radii are rule-conditional: τ ∗ (α) is a staircase with jumps at the certificate thresholds (Appendix D),
      so the level α should be read as choosing a step of that staircase, not a point of a smooth trade-off.
450
      Along the estimation axis, Appendix D(e) shows τ ∗ is not monotone in the prior-fit size m; the de-
451
      composition of Appendix D(f) attributes this to the rule the fit induces (freezing the prior leaves the
452   non-monotonicity intact; freezing the rule removes the largest dips), not the prior’s sampling error.
453   Appendix D(g) turns that attribution into a constructive repair: a per-atom flip budget implemented
454   as a certificate-ordered subset of stopping states provably dominates the induced profile, restores
455   the full simplex (τ ∗ = 1, trivial-validity regime) at 70 of 72 cells for +1%–+11% extra votes, and
456   repairs the coarse-fit breaks below — while two alternatives (prior support truncation, deadline trun-

460
      is proved in closed form (Appendix A) and independently verified exhaustively at N = 32. (iv)

      prior transfer, and generality across models and task families by two further model–carrier pairs —

470   10    C ONCLUSION
471
472   Treating across-task heterogeneity as the certificate object turns early stopping for majority voting
473   into an exact conditional-inference problem under the replay model: adaptivity is free (tower prop-
474   erty), calibration is one empirical-Bernstein step over problems, and the supplied OMR readout has
475   an 81% replay rollout-count reduction at full-vote flip level 0.05, where the strongest distribution-
476   free baseline has 16% and the strongest fixed mixture-aware budget 47%. The same framework
477   admits a synthetic ordered-drift analysis with a margin repair, while natural online order, gold cor-
478   rectness, and actual token/latency/cancellation costs remain open.

615   A.2    T ERMINAL - STOP BOOKKEEPING (T HEOREM 1)
616
617   The stopping rule is τ = min{k ∈ {3, . . . , N } : cH (xk , k) ≤ α}. At a reachable terminal state,
618   all N binary outcomes have been observed, hence xN = K. Equivalently, the hypergeometric
619   likelihood at (xN , N ) has support only on K = xN , so
620                                               cH (xN , N ) = 0.
621   Thus k = N satisfies the same threshold rule rather than being an exceptional forced-stop state. The
622   tower argument applies to this bounded stopping time, and
623                                                                                
                     P(flip) = E cH (xτ , τ ) = E cH 1{τ < N } + E cH 1{τ = N } .
624
625   The first term is at most α P(τ < N ), and the second term is zero. Therefore the integrand is
626   bounded pointwise by α and P(flip) ≤ α. For each fixed true count K, a path reaching N also has
627   xN = K, so its terminal contribution to the DP flip probability g(K; α, H   b m ) is exactly zero. The
628   population layer remains needed for calibration-prior estimation, not to absorb a terminal flip.
629   Remark 6 (The terminal-stop mass is small in practice). On the TEST split, the exact fraction of
630   paths reaching k = N under BAYES-H is 0.59% at α = 0.05 and 1.10% at α = 0.02 (the certificate
631   stops early exactly on the bimodal mass). Their contribution to replay flip is zero; their contribution
