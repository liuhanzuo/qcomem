                                                A BSTRACT
013
014
              We study an analysis model for repeated model rollouts: a count-exchangeable
015           binary pass-count replay in which a task’s N outcomes are available only through
016           its pass count. In that model, we give a mixture-posterior stopper (BAYES-H)
017           whose per-problem conditional flip certificate is exact: given a FIT-frozen prior
018           H over per-task pass counts, stop as soon as the posterior probability that the
019           full-N binary pass-count decision will disagree with the current prefix decision
020           drops below α. By the tower property the replay-population flip rate of any such
021           prefix-measurable rule is bounded by α without a union bound over stopping times
022           (adaptive is free). Under independent i.i.d. FIT/CAL task sampling, the popula-
023
              tion layer adds an empirical-Bernstein CAL step with Bonferroni control of the
              pre-declared frozen rule family. On 11,607 math problems with N = 32 rollouts
024
              each (OpenMathReasoning), selected and evaluated under a strict FIT/CAL/TEST
025
              problem split with a single held-out readout, BAYES-H has an 81% replay rollout-
026           count reduction at realized flip rate 0.025 ≤ α = 0.05 and 73% at α = 0.02,
027           dominating the strongest distribution-free baseline (Hoeffding-fixed-k: 16% at
028           α = 0.05) with a statistically significant paired gap, and the strongest mixture-
029           aware fixed budget (empirical-Bernstein fixed-k: 47%). An ESC-style window
030           heuristic with an 86% replay count reduction exceeds 0.05 (0.058 > 0.05). Un-
031           der pre-fixed synthetic ordered-drift mechanisms (front-loading, linear trend, ad-
032           versarial block swap), BAYES-H is the most robust stopper in the reported har-
033           ness. The displayed certificate-margin thresholds are exploratory descriptive sen-
034
              sitivity results, not frozen-selection or confirmatory claims. The supplied replay
              readout replicates on a disjoint second shard; transferring one fitted calibration
035
              mixture across shards (TV 0.042) moves its reported quantities by ≤ 0.4 points.
036
              This is deterministic fitted-mixture sensitivity, not a population-transfer certifi-
037           cate. Two further model–carrier pairs bracket the mechanism: on OpenR1-Math-
038           220k (DeepSeek-R1, M = 2 per problem) the certificate attains the replay risk–
039           count frontier exactly when the mixture structure allows, and never stops under
040           the stated replay certificate when it cannot; on RLVE (Qwen3-4B, N = 8 per
041           problem, verifiable environments) it lands at the intermediate point the mecha-
042           nism predicts — 51–58% replay count reduction at α ∈ {0.10, 0.05}, 45–48% at
043           α ∈ {0.02, 0.01}, certified at every level down to α = 0.01 where no distribution-
044           free replay budget is certifiable. Every reported flip is disagreement between cor-
045
              responding binary pass-count replay decisions, and every saving is 1 − k̄/N ; these
              artifacts do not measure a delivered-answer majority, gold correctness, generated
046
              tokens, latency, cancellation, or ordered online deployment.

085   We make two structural observations that reframe the problem. First, on real carriers the dominant
086   deviation from i.i.d. is not latent within-task correlation but across-task heterogeneity: per-task pass
087   rates are strongly bimodal (Section 3), and the flip probability a stopper must control is a functional
088
      of the mixture of per-task difficulty, not of a pooled correlation coefficient. Pooled second-moment
      summaries can be simultaneously over-conservative and uncalibrated (Section 3). Second, once the
089
      target is the mixture, the correct object is a per-prefix conditional certificate: the posterior probabil-
090
      ity, given the observed prefix, that the full-N majority disagrees. This object is exactly computable
091   (hypergeometric posterior), its fixed-budget expectation EH [fK (k)] is monotone in the prefix length
092   (Lemma 1), and — because the tower property collapses the stopping-time dependence — it certifies
093   any adaptive rule at level α with no union bound (Theorem 1).

122   2    R ELATED WORK
123
124   Adaptive sampling for voting. ASC (Aggarwal et al., 2023) adapts a per-problem sample count
125   using a lightweight stopping criterion, and ESC (Li et al., 2024) proposes early stopping for self-
126   consistency. We do not establish a finite-sample disagreement-with-full-vote guarantee for either;
127   on our supplied heterogeneous replay carrier, the instantiated ESC-style rule exceeds 0.05 (Table 1).
128   MARS (Chen et al., 2026) is a parallel trace-stopping method that bounds future vote movement;
129   our work is instead a sequential count-exchangeable replay certificate. Best-of-n and budget-forcing
130   methods (Snell et al., 2025) tune compute without stopping certificates.
131   Conformal and anytime inference. Conformal prediction (Vovk et al., 2005) gives marginal cov-
132   erage under exchangeability; confidence sequences (Howard et al., 2021) give anytime-valid bounds
133   for i.i.d. means. Our certificate is neither: it is an exact posterior flip probability under a calibrated
134   discrete prior, valid conditionally per prefix (hence adaptivity is free), with the mixture estimated
135   from calibration problems under an empirical-Bernstein bound (Maurer & Pontil, 2009). The Bon-
136   ferroni control of a small pre-declared rule family is deliberately conservative and transparent.
137   Dependent sampling. Design-effect corrections (Kish, 1965) shrink effective sample size by an
138   intraclass correlation; we show pooled correlation is the wrong functional here (Section 3). Beta-
139   binomial and correlated-Bernoulli models (Skellam, 1948) capture overdispersion but target estima-
140   tion, not stopping certificates for the flip event.
141
142   Feature boundary. Table 2 makes the source-verified distinction compact: the proposed object
143   is a specified count-exchangeable full-vote replay endpoint with a calibrated-mixture posterior flip
144   certificate. It is not a general ordered-online early-stopping, conformal, or anytime-inference guar-
145
      antee.

147   3    M ODEL : COUNT- EXCHANGEABLE REPLAY
148
149   A task’s N rollout outcomes are summarized by the pass count K ∈ {0, . . . , N }. Across tasks,
150   K ∼ H, a mixture capturing both task heterogeneity and any within-task correlation: the probability
151   a fresh rollout passes, marginally, is EH [K]/N , and the joint law of the N outcomes is exchangeable
152   within a task given its realized count. Analysis model (replay): the stopper observes a uniform
153   random prefix drawn without replacement from the task’s N outcomes — i.e., the first k draws are
154   exchangeable with the remaining N − k. Section 7 stress-tests departures from this model (ordered
155
      drift); our validity claims are stated relative to it, and the carrier limitation (counts only, no order) is
      made explicit in Section 8.
156
157   Let MV(k) = 1{xk > k/2} with ties assigned to the negative side, and let MV(N ) = 1{K >
158   N/2} be the full-N binary pass-count replay decision. It is not an aggregation of delivered textual
159   answers or an actual delivered answer-majority: the replay model contains only binary pass/fail
160   outcomes. The flip event of a stopping rule τ is {MV(τ ) ̸= MV(N )}.
161   Remark 1 (Why the mixture, not the correlation). On τ 2 -bench carriers (6 public task suites, 4 roll-
      outs/task) we measured pooled within-task reward correlation ρ̂ = 0.47–0.59, but a task-demeaned


                                                           3
162
      (fixed-effect) estimate gives ρ̂FE ≈ −1/3 ≈ −1/(N − 1), the demeaning artifact floor: essen-
163   tially all observed correlation is across-task heterogeneity, not latent within-task dependence. On
164   OpenMathReasoning the per-task pass-rate variance exceeds the binomial baseline by 34×. The
165   flip probability is driven by the mass of tasks with K ≈ N/2 — a mixture functional — so pooled
166   second-moment discounts are the wrong certificate statistic.

454   8    L IMITATIONS
455
456
457
      (i) The public carrier provides pass counts, not ordered rollouts: all main results are exact only under
458   the count-exchangeable replay model; the synthetic stress mechanisms of Section 7 only character-
459   ize specified departures from that model. The certificate is a replay-model conditional certificate,
460   not an e-process: validity under arbitrary online stopping times in ordered deployment is outside the
461   stated claims. No supplied run compares the stopped answer with gold correctness, or records gener-
462   ated tokens, latency, cancellation acknowledgement, or post-cancellation work; full-vote agreement
463   and 1− k̄/N cannot substitute for those endpoints. (ii) The mixture prior is estimated from 4000 cal-
464   ibration problems of the same distribution; adversarial mixture shift beyond the tested transfer range
465   can break the certificate; the exact critical radii of Proposition 1 give deterministic frozen-rule sen-
466
      sitivity radii over an assumed fitted-mixture TV ball (τ ∗ = 0.024–0.157 across levels and shards);
      they do not establish target-population containment. Within that assumed ball the failure bound-
467
      ary is sharp — at α = 0.01 on shard 1 the observed cross-shard mismatch 0.042 already exceeds
468
      τ ∗ (0.01) = 0.024; and the radii are rule-conditional: τ ∗ (α) is a staircase with jumps at the certifi-
469   cate thresholds (Appendix E), so the level α should be read as choosing a step of that staircase, not
470   a point of a smooth trade-off. Along the estimation axis, Appendix E(e) shows τ ∗ is not monotone
471   in the prior-fit size m; the decomposition of Appendix E(f) attributes this to the rule the fit induces
472   (freezing the prior leaves the non-monotonicity intact; freezing the rule removes the largest dips),
473   not the prior’s sampling error. Appendix E(g) now records only an oracle K-wise profile-capping
474   sensitivity: its stored subset is selected separately after conditioning on the final count K. Because
475   that final count is unavailable at a prefix, this diagnostic is neither a common prefix-state rule nor an
476   instance of Theorem 1; it supports no stopping-policy, cost, or robustness-repair claim. Appendix F
477
      collects every in-distribution readout and its failure boundary in one matrix. (iii) Lemma 1’s mono-
      tonicity is proved in closed form (Appendix A) and independently verified exhaustively at N = 32.
478
      (iv) Main-table counts come from one model–task pair (Qwen2.5-Math-72B-TIR, math problems);
479
      generality across problem pools is supported by the disjoint second-shard replication and cross-shard
480   prior transfer, and generality across models and task families by two further model–carrier pairs —
481   DeepSeek-R1 rollouts on OpenR1-Math-220k (M = 2), on which the certificate attains its replay
482   risk–count frontier, and Qwen3-4B rollouts on RLVE verifiable environments (N = 8), on which it
483   is certified at every tested level and interpolates with the resolvable mixture structure (Section 6). (v)
484   At k = N , the observed pass count equals K, so the binary pass-count replay decision agrees with
485   itself and cH (xN , N ) = 0. Terminal stops add zero replay flip for every true count; this endpoint
      convention is shared by Theorem 1 and the DP in Appendix B.

      9   C ONCLUSION
487
488
      Treating across-task heterogeneity as the certificate object turns early stopping for majority voting
489   into an exact conditional-inference problem under the replay model: adaptivity is free (tower prop-
490   erty), calibration is one empirical-Bernstein step over problems, and the supplied OMR readout has
491   an 81% replay rollout-count reduction at full-vote flip level 0.05, where the strongest distribution-
492   free baseline has 16% and the strongest fixed mixture-aware budget 47%. The same framework
493   admits a synthetic ordered-drift analysis with a margin repair, while natural online order, gold cor-
494   rectness, and actual token/latency/cancellation costs remain open.
