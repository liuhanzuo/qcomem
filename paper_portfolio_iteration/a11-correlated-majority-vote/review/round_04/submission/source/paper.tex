\begin{abstract}
We study a count-exchangeable binary pass-count replay model for repeated model
rollouts, where a task's $N$ binary outcomes are summarized by its pass count.
For the \emph{true} count law $\trueH$, an oracle posterior score
$\oraclecert(x,k)$ is the exact conditional replay-flip probability; the
corresponding oracle stopping theorem follows by the tower property without a
union bound over stopping times. The implemented BAYES-H rule instead freezes
$\widehat H_{\mathrm{FIT}}$ and thresholds the fitted stopping score
$\fitscore(x,k)$. We do \emph{not} identify that plug-in score with the oracle
conditional probability. Its operative high-probability statement is
Theorem~\ref{thm:pop}: under independent i.i.d. FIT/CAL task sampling, a finite
FIT-frozen family is CAL-screened using empirical-Bernstein UCBs with a
Bonferroni allocation, yielding a marginal replay guarantee for a selected
frozen rule; TEST is descriptive.

On the supplied OpenMathReasoning count-replay artifact (11{,}607 problems,
$N=32$; FIT/CAL/TEST $4000/4000/3607$), the CAL-selected fitted-score rule has
a descriptive TEST replay rollout-count reduction of $80.9\%$ at realized
replay flip $0.0249$ for $\alpha=0.05$ and $73.5\%$ at $\alpha=0.02$; the
corresponding FIXED-HOEF/FIXED-EB rows are reported alongside it. An ESC-style
window heuristic has a $0.0577$ replay flip in this TEST readout. The supplied
synthetic drift and all margin-exploration results are explicitly synthetic,
exploratory, and nonconfirmatory: at E3, $\alpha=0.05$, and $\delta=0.15$,
BAYES-H is invalid ($5.2\%$ flip) while FIXED-EB remains valid ($4.7\%$).
The OpenR1 carrier is accounted for as 9{,}374 raw unique problems, of which
8{,}853 have exactly two deduplicated rollouts and are split
$3000/3000/2853$ (FIT/CAL/TEST). Every reported flip is disagreement between
binary pass-count replay decisions, and every saving is $1-\bar k/N$; the
artifacts do not measure a delivered-answer majority, gold correctness,
generated tokens, latency, cancellation, or ordered online deployment.
\end{abstract}

Self-consistency~\citep{wang2023selfconsistency} replaces one sampled answer
by a majority vote over $N$ samples. Adaptive variants reduce or reallocate
sampling: ASC~\citep{aggarwal2023asc} adapts a per-problem count with a
lightweight stopping criterion, ESC~\citep{li2024esc} proposes early stopping
for self-consistency, and MARS~\citep{chen2026mars} stops parallel traces using
a conservative bound on future vote movement. For a count-only replay analysis,
two deployment factors remain outside its evidence: (i) within-task rollouts
may be correlated; and (ii) chronological generation order may drift, breaking
an exchangeability-based certificate.

We make two model-scoped observations. First, in a count-only carrier the
observable and modeled task-level variation is \emph{across-task}
heterogeneity: per-task pass rates are strongly bimodal
(Section~\ref{sec:model}), and the replay flip probability is a functional of
the mixture of per-task counts. Because the artifact retains counts rather
than ordered outcomes, it cannot identify whether within-task correlation is
the dominant deviation from i.i.d., nor establish a pooled-correlation
diagnostic. Second, once the target is the mixture, the
correct \emph{oracle} object is a per-prefix conditional probability under the
true law $\trueH$: the posterior probability, given the observed prefix, that
the full-$N$ majority disagrees. This object is exactly computable
(hypergeometric posterior), its
fixed-budget expectation $\E_H[f_K(k)]$ is monotone in the prefix
length (Lemma~\ref{lem:exact}), and --- because the tower property
collapses the stopping-time
dependence --- it certifies \emph{any} adaptive rule at level $\alpha$ with no
union bound (Theorem~\ref{thm:tower}). The implemented plug-in score formed
with $\widehat H_{\mathrm{FIT}}$ is distinct; its usable guarantee is the
FIT-frozen CAL-screened marginal result of Theorem~\ref{thm:pop}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Framework.} A \emph{binary pass-count replay} model, not an
ordered online or answer-aggregation model, in which both within-task
correlation and across-task heterogeneity are folded into the true mixture
$\trueH$ over per-task pass counts; the target is replay flip probability
(Section~\ref{sec:model}).
\item \textbf{Oracle theorem and fitted implementation.} For true $\trueH$,
$\oraclecert(x,k)$ is an exact conditional replay-flip probability and
Theorem~\ref{thm:tower} is an oracle result. BAYES-H instead thresholds
$\fitscore(x,k)$ after fitting $\widehat H_{\mathrm{FIT}}$; its operative
guarantee is Theorem~\ref{thm:pop}'s CAL-screened marginal replay bound, not
the oracle conditional identity (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
\item \textbf{Legitimate selection.} Under the stated i.i.d.-task and
FIT/CAL-independence assumptions, a FIT-frozen, pre-declared rule family
(budget $\times$ level for fixed-$k$; level for BAYES-H) is selected on CAL
with simultaneous empirical-Bernstein bounds~\citep{maurer2009eb} plus
Bonferroni.  The fixed benchmark TEST split is read once and reported only
descriptively (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).
\item \textbf{Empirical replay readouts.} On 11{,}607 OpenMathReasoning
problems, the one descriptive TEST readout reports $80.9\%$ replay
rollout-count reduction at replay flip $0.0249$ ($\alpha=0.05$), versus
$15.6\%$ for FIXED-HOEF; ESC-style window stopping has $0.0577$ replay flip.
Two additional supplied count-replay carriers (DeepSeek-R1 at $M=2$ and
Qwen3-4B at $N=8$) are setting-specific descriptive comparisons, not a
policy-class comparison or a general allocation law (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\item \textbf{Honest boundaries.} The public carrier gives per-task pass
\emph{counts}, not ordered rollouts; it supports a random-prefix
(count-exchangeable) analysis only as a model assumption and cannot validate
ordered online deployment. We therefore bound the failure domain under three
pre-fixed synthetic ordered-drift mechanisms; every displayed margin result is
synthetic, exploratory, and nonconfirmatory (Section~\ref{sec:drift}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Adaptive sampling for voting.} ASC~\citep{aggarwal2023asc} adapts a
per-problem sample count using a lightweight stopping criterion, and
ESC~\citep{li2024esc} proposes early stopping for self-consistency. We do not
establish a finite-sample disagreement-with-full-vote guarantee for either;
on our supplied heterogeneous replay carrier, the instantiated ESC-style rule
exceeds $0.05$ (Table~\ref{tab:main}). MARS~\citep{chen2026mars} is a
parallel trace-stopping method that bounds future vote movement; our work is
instead a sequential count-exchangeable replay certificate. Best-of-$n$ and budget-forcing
methods~\citep{snell2024scaling} tune compute without stopping certificates.

\textbf{Conformal and anytime inference.} Conformal
prediction~\citep{vovk2005algorithmic} gives marginal coverage under
exchangeability; confidence sequences~\citep{howard2021cs} give anytime-valid
bounds for i.i.d.\ means. Our oracle object is neither: under the true
$\trueH$, $\oraclecert$ is an \emph{exact posterior flip probability}, valid
conditionally per prefix. The implemented fitted score is instead screened
through the finite FIT-frozen CAL empirical-Bernstein/Bonferroni construction
of Theorem~\ref{thm:pop}; it is not claimed conditionally exact per prefix.

\textbf{Bayesian stopping and finite-population inference.} Rossell and
M\"uller~\citep{RossellMuller2013SequentialStopping} and
Novikov~\citep{Novikov2010OptimalSequential} are Bayesian
decision-theoretic sequential-stopping antecedents; neither is a held-out
task-level calibration result for a fitted score under this replay endpoint.
For the sampling model, Waudby-Smith and
Ramdas~\citep{WaudbySmithRamdas2020Confidence} are the closest
finite-population without-replacement precedent: their binary prefix counts
are hypergeometric and their confidence sequences remain frequentist under a
working prior. Our supported novelty is narrower: independent FIT and CAL
tasks freeze and screen a finite family of plug-in fitted scores by exact
task-level replay loss. We do not claim a new general confidence sequence,
Bayesian stopping principle, or optimal stopping result.

\textbf{Dependent sampling.} Design-effect corrections~\citep{kish1965deff}
shrink effective sample size by an intraclass correlation; we show pooled
correlation is the wrong functional here (Section~\ref{sec:model}).
Beta-binomial and correlated-Bernoulli
models~\citep{skellam1948betabinomial} capture overdispersion but target
estimation, not stopping certificates for the flip event.

\paragraph{Feature boundary.} Table~\ref{tab:feature-compare} makes the
source-verified distinction compact: the proposed object is a specified
count-exchangeable full-vote \emph{replay} endpoint with an oracle conditional
object and a distinct fitted-score CAL guarantee. It is not a general
ordered-online early-stopping, conformal, or anytime-inference guarantee.

\section{Model: count-exchangeable replay}
\label{sec:model}

A task's $N$ rollout outcomes are summarized by the pass count
$K\in\{0,\dots,N\}$. Across tasks, $K\sim\trueH$, the \emph{true} mixture
capturing both
task heterogeneity and any within-task correlation: the probability a fresh
rollout passes, marginally, is $\E_{\trueH}[K]/N$, and the joint law of the $N$
outcomes is exchangeable \emph{within} a task given its realized count.
Analysis model (\emph{replay}): the stopper observes a uniform random
prefix drawn without replacement from the task's $N$ outcomes --- i.e., the
first $k$ draws are exchangeable with the remaining $N-k$.
Section~\ref{sec:drift} stress-tests departures from this model (ordered
drift); our validity claims are stated relative to it, and the carrier
limitation (counts only, no order) is made explicit in
Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

Let $\MV(k)=\mathbf{1}\{x_k>k/2\}$ with ties assigned to the negative side,
and let $\MV(N)=\mathbf{1}\{K>N/2\}$ be the full-$N$ \emph{binary
pass-count replay decision}.  It is not an aggregation of delivered textual
answers or an actual delivered answer-majority: the replay model contains only
binary pass/fail outcomes. The \emph{flip event} of a stopping rule $\tau$ is
$\{\MV(\tau)\neq\MV(N)\}$.

\begin{remark}[Why the mixture, not the correlation]
\label{rem:pooled}
On $\tau^2$-bench carriers (6 public task suites, 4 rollouts/task) we measured
pooled within-task reward correlation $\hat\rho=0.47$--$0.59$, but a
task-demeaned (fixed-effect) estimate gives $\hat\rho_{\mathrm{FE}}\approx-1/3
\approx-1/(N-1)$, the demeaning artifact floor: essentially \emph{all}
observed correlation is across-task heterogeneity, not latent within-task
dependence. On OpenMathReasoning the per-task pass-rate variance exceeds the
binomial baseline by $34\times$. The flip probability is driven by the mass of
tasks with $K\approx N/2$ --- a mixture functional --- so pooled
second-moment discounts are the wrong certificate statistic.
\end{remark}

\section{Method: the mixture-posterior stopper}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Exact per-task flip probability.}
Given count $K$, the prefix count $x_k$ is hypergeometric
$\HG(N,K,k)$, hence
\begin{equation}
f_K(k) \;:=\; \P\big(\MV(k)\neq\MV(N)\,\big|\,K\big)
= \sum_{x}\HG(N,K,k,x)\,\big|\mathrm{side}(x,k)-\mathrm{side}(K,N)\big|.
\label{eq:exact}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Exactness and monotonicity]
\label{lem:exact}
\eqref{eq:exact} is an identity (no approximation). For every fixed $K$,
$f_K(k)$ is nonincreasing in odd $k$; hence for any mixture $H$,
$\E_H[f_K(k)]$ is nonincreasing and
$k^*(\alpha)=\min\{k:\E_H[f_K(k)]\le\alpha\}$ is well defined and
binary-searchable.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The identity is a definition expansion. Monotonicity follows from a
coupling of the prefixes at $k$ and $k{+}2$ on a common ordered draw.  The
proof first handles the support-boundary case where the downward boundary atom
has probability zero directly; only otherwise does it divide to obtain the
ratio $(K-t)/(N-K-t)\ge0$ for $K>N/2$. The complementary case follows by
pass/fail reflection (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). An exact-rational CPU audit
checks every admissible $(N,K,k)$ for $2\le N\le64$, including
$(32,17,29)$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Oracle conditional certificate (true $\trueH$ only).}
Given the true count law $\trueH$ and an observed prefix $(x,k)$, define the
oracle posterior flip probability
\begin{equation}
\oraclecert(x,k) \;:=\; \P_{\trueH}\big(\mathrm{side}(K,N)\neq\mathrm{side}(x,k)\,\big|\,x,k\big),
\qquad \text{posterior} \propto \trueH[K]\cdot\HG(N,K,k,x).
\label{eq:cond}
\end{equation}
We define the terminal oracle certificate by $\oraclecert(x,N):=0$. At a
posterior-supported terminal state, the hypergeometric likelihood has support
only on $K=x_N$, so this convention equals the conditional flip probability:
$\oraclecert(x_N,N)=0$. It also gives the correct endpoint value for a
zero-prior-mass row of a frozen fitted-score table, because the observed total
count already fixes the binary full-$N$ replay decision.

\begin{theorem}[Oracle conditional identity and adaptive bound]
\label{thm:tower}
Let $\tau$ be any stopping time measurable w.r.t.\ the prefix filtration that
stops only at states with $\oraclecert(x_\tau,\tau)\le\alpha$. The terminal
state $k=N$ satisfies this same rule because $\oraclecert(x_N,N)=0$. Under
the true law $\trueH$,
$\P_{\trueH}(\MV(\tau)\neq\MV(N)) = \E_{\trueH}[\oraclecert(x_\tau,\tau)]\le\alpha$,
with equality in the identity and no union bound over stopping times.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\P_{\trueH}(\flip)=\E_{\trueH}[\P_{\trueH}(\flip\mid\text{prefix path})]$.
For any path stopped at $(x,k)$,
$\P_{\trueH}(\MV(N)\neq\mathrm{side}(x,k)\mid\text{path})=\oraclecert(x,k)$
since the posterior is exactly the conditional law of $K$ under $\trueH$ and $\MV(N)$ is a
function of $K$. At $\tau=N$, $x_N=K$ and hence this conditional probability
is $\oraclecert(x_N,N)=0$; at every earlier stopped state it is at most $\alpha$ by
the rule. Thus the integrand is bounded pointwise by $\alpha$, and taking
expectations gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Fitted implementation (BAYES-H).}
The implementation freezes $\widehat H_{\mathrm{FIT}}$ before CAL and thresholds
the \emph{fitted stopping score}
\[
\fitscore(x,k):=\P_{\widehat H_{\mathrm{FIT}}}
\big(\mathrm{side}(K,N)\neq\mathrm{side}(x,k)\mid x,k\big).
\]
It stops at $\widehat\tau=\min\{k\ge3:\fitscore(x_k,k)\le\alpha\}$.
This score is a plug-in posterior calculation, not a claim that
$\fitscore(x,k)=\P_{\trueH}(\flip\mid x,k)$ unless
$\widehat H_{\mathrm{FIT}}=\trueH$. Its terminal value is set to zero because
$x_N=K$, but that endpoint fact does not repair the non-oracle conditional
identity at earlier prefixes. Once the fitted rule is frozen, its exact
per-task replay-flip probability and expected stopping time under any true
count $K$ are computable by DP over prefix states $(k,x)$
(Appendix~\ref{app:dp}). The operational guarantee for this fitted rule is
Theorem~\ref{thm:pop}, not Theorem~\ref{thm:tower}.

\begin{theorem}[CAL-screened marginal replay guarantee for a FIT-frozen rule family]
\label{thm:pop}
Let FIT and CAL be independent i.i.d. task samples from a common
count-exchangeable replay population with true count law $\trueH$. Conditional
on FIT, freeze $\widehat H_{\mathrm{FIT}}$, a finite pre-declared family
$\mathcal R_{\mathrm{FIT}}$ (including all fitted-score tables), and fix
$r\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{FIT}}$. Let $g_r(K)$ be the exact per-task replay
flip probability of this \emph{fixed fitted rule}, and let
$\bar g_{\calD,r}$, $s^2_{\calD,r}$ be the mean and variance of $g_r(K)$ over
the $m$ CAL tasks.
For a per-rule failure allocation $\delta_r$, with probability
$1-\delta_r$ over CAL conditional on FIT,
\[
\P_{\text{replay},\trueH}(\flip) \;\le\; \bar g_{\calD,r}
+ \sqrt{\tfrac{2 s^2_{\calD,r}\log(4/\delta_r)}{m}}
+ \tfrac{7\log(4/\delta_r)}{3(m-1)},
\]
where the first term is $\bar g_{\calD,r}$.  This is the
empirical-Bernstein inequality~\citep{maurer2009eb} for bounded i.i.d.
per-task flips $g_r\in[0,1]$. Setting $\delta_r=\delta/J$ and applying
Bonferroni gives a simultaneous CAL selection guarantee over $J$ frozen
candidates (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}). This is a marginal replay statement
for the CAL-screened FIT-frozen rule; it does not assert a per-problem oracle
conditional identity for $\fitscore$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{remark}[No Jensen gap]
For a fixed fitted rule, the population replay flip rate is a \emph{linear}
functional of $\trueH$: $\P_{\trueH}(\flip)=\sum_K \trueH[K]\,g(K)$. Hence the
CAL bound applies to a bounded per-task quantity; it does not make the fitted
score an oracle conditional certificate.
\end{remark}
\begin{proposition}[Exact deterministic TV sensitivity of a frozen rule]
\label{prop:tv}
Fix the rule (fitted-score tables frozen from $\widehat H$) and let $g(K)$ be
its exact per-task flip probability. Over the total-variation ball
$B_R=\{q:q\ge0,\sum_K q_K=1,\|q-\widehat H\|_1\le 2R\}$,
\[
V(R)\;:=\;\max_{q\in B_R}\;\textstyle\sum_K q_K\,g(K)
\]
is a capacity-constrained linear program whose optimum is a two-sided greedy:
add mass to atoms in descending $g$ (capped by $q_K\le1$) and drain mass from
atoms in ascending $g$ (capped by $\widehat H_K$). It is computable in
$O(N\log N)$ and is nondecreasing, piecewise linear in $R$; it is not
generally globally affine because donor and recipient capacities create
breakpoints. Any two simplex points have $\ell_1$ distance at most $2$, so
$B_1$ is the full simplex; the linear objective is maximized at a vertex and
therefore $V(1)=\max_K g(K)$. For the positive radii and realized capacities checked in the
supplied LP grids, it is strictly below the $1$-Lipschitz envelope
$\flip(\widehat H)+R$; no universal strict-refinement claim is made. The
\emph{critical radius} $\tau^*(\alpha)=\max\{R:V(R)\le\alpha\}$ is a
deterministic property of the frozen rule and fitted reference mixture. It
becomes a population certificate only after separately proving that an unknown
target mixture lies in $B_R$, which current artifacts do not do.
\end{proposition}
The stored OMR, RLVE, and OpenR1 curves in Appendix~\ref{app:tau} are
deterministic visualizations of this assumed-ball calculation for their
respective fitted rules. They are retained only as sensitivity diagnostics:
they neither establish target-population containment nor justify a
cross-carrier mechanism, a shift-robustness ranking, or a prescription for
choosing $\alpha$.

\begin{remark}[Reported fitted-score family for CAL screening]
\label{rem:nested}
The reported protocol uses only the finite, pre-declared $J=64$ family in
Section~\ref{sec:protocol}: 60 fixed-$k$ candidates and four BAYES-H levels.
Theorem~\ref{thm:pop}'s Bonferroni statement is only for that FIT-frozen
family. A stored breakpoint enumeration over other $\alpha$ values is not a
selection family for the reported table and supplies no additional operational
guarantee.
\end{remark}

\section{Protocol: FIT/CAL/TEST with a legitimate selection family}
\label{sec:protocol}

\textbf{Split and status.} 11{,}607 problems are partitioned once (seeded)
into FIT (4000), CAL (4000), TEST (3607). The population statement in
Theorem~\ref{thm:pop} assumes tasks are i.i.d. draws from one replay
population and that FIT and CAL are independent. FIT estimates
$\widehat H_{\mathrm{FIT}}$ and freezes the BAYES-H fitted-score tables and
the pre-declared family before CAL is inspected. CAL selects rules from that
frozen family:
fixed-$k$ candidates $k\in\{3,5,\dots,31\}\times\alpha\in\{0.10,0.05,0.02,
0.01\}$ (60 candidates) and BAYES-H at the same 4 levels, with simultaneous
empirical-Bernstein UCBs at per-rule $\delta_r=0.05/64$ and Bonferroni over
$J=64$ FIT-frozen candidates. $k^*(\alpha)$ is the smallest budget whose CAL
EB UCB is $\le\alpha$; BAYES-H is accepted at level $\alpha$ iff its CAL EB
UCB is $\le\alpha$. These are marginal replay UCBs for fitted frozen rules,
not per-problem oracle conditional certificates. TEST is read exactly once, is not
used for selection, and is a fixed-benchmark descriptive readout rather than
a second population guarantee. All evaluation uses the exact per-task flip
functions (\eqref{eq:exact} for
fixed-$k$; the DP of Appendix~\ref{app:dp} for BAYES-H), so TEST numbers are
exact replay quantities, not MC estimates; the DP itself is cross-checked
against replay MC (max deviation $0.043$ within $3\sigma_{\rm MC}=0.075$).

\textbf{Baselines.} FULL-$N$; FIXED-HOEF (fixed budget selected with
distribution-free Hoeffding UCBs, a named model-free comparator);
FIXED-EB (fixed budget with the same mixture-aware empirical-Bernstein
machinery as BAYES-H --- isolates the value of adaptivity);
BAYES-UNIF (uniform prior; no fitted-score CAL guarantee when the prior is wrong);
WINDOW3 (ESC-style: stop when 3 consecutive samples agree with the current
majority --- no CAL-screened tuning rule).

$+0.058$ (CI radius $0.029$) at $\alpha=0.10$ and $+0.652\pm0.029$ at
$\alpha=0.05$ --- a $5.2\times$ improvement over the baseline's $15.6\%$
count reduction; at $\alpha=0.02$ the baseline has \emph{no} CAL-screened budget while
BAYES-H still has a $73.5\%$ reduction. Against the named mixture-aware fixed budget
(FIXED-EB), this fitted-score rule reports $+34$ points of replay count reduction at $\alpha=0.05$
($80.9\%$ vs $46.9\%$) and $+70$ points at $\alpha=0.02$ --- the adaptive
gap in this TEST readout grows as $\alpha$ tightens. This descriptive pattern
is consistent with the fitted score stopping earlier on extreme-$K$ problems
than on the $11.6\%$ mid-difficulty mass; it is not an oracle conditional
claim for $\widehat H_{\mathrm{FIT}}$.

\paragraph{Cross-source transfer.} Splitting CAL/TEST across the four AoPS
problem sources (train on three, test on the fourth), the supplied BAYES-H
readouts have replay flip $0.021$--$0.033<\alpha=0.05$ and count reduction
$79$--$83\%$. These are descriptive source-split replay readouts, not a
population-transfer, oracle-conditional, or ordered-online transfer guarantee.

\paragraph{Why the window heuristic fails.} On mid-difficulty problems
($K\approx16$), three consecutive agreements with the current majority is a
\emph{common} event under near-coin-flip draws, not evidence the vote has
settled: WINDOW3 realizes flip $0.058$ at mean $k=4.4$. Its flip rate is
nearly flat in $\alpha$ (it has no CAL-screened tuning rule): it can only be tuned by
window length, and its certified population UCB is $0.061$
(\texttt{adaptive\_sweep\_r468\_result.json}), so it barely misses even the
loosest tested level $\alpha=0.10$ on the full OMR test pool.

\section{Synthetic ordered drift: exploratory stress diagnostic}
\label{sec:drift}

The replay model assumes prefixes are exchangeable with the held remainder.
Because the carrier contains no observed chronology, we apply three
\emph{pre-fixed synthetic} drift mechanisms to its true counts
($\delta\in\{0,0.05,0.10,0.15,0.20\}$, 2000 TEST problems, 500 orders each):
\textbf{E1} (front-load: the first $k$ draws are enriched/depleted in passes
by count $D=\lceil 2\delta\min(K,N{-}K)\rceil$), \textbf{E2} (linear per-draw
pass-rate trend $p_j=K/N+\delta(2j/(N{-}1){-}1)$), \textbf{E3} (adversarial
block: the last $\lceil\delta N\rceil$ draws drift toward the minority side).

Findings (full tables in Appendix~\ref{app:drift}) are a specified synthetic
stress diagnostic, not evidence about natural order. All methods exceed the
target under E1 by $\delta=0.10$--$0.15$ in this harness. The grid also has
non-uniform outcomes: under E2 at $\alpha=0.10$, BAYES-H's replay flip is
$0.083$ at $\delta=0.20$, whereas under E3 at $\alpha=0.05$ and
$\delta=0.15$, BAYES-H is invalid ($0.052$) while FIXED-EB remains valid
($0.047$). Thus the grid supplies no global robustness ranking.

Every margin-exploration result is \emph{synthetic, exploratory, and
nonconfirmatory}. No frozen margin-selection record is bundled. For example,
in the E3 $\alpha=0.05$ stress harness, the explored $\gamma=0.025$ row has
flip $0.0495$ and replay count reduction $77.2\%$ at $\delta=0.15$, versus
$81.0\%$ for the unmargined row; at $\delta=0.20$, even $\gamma=0.03$ is
invalid. Under E2, $\gamma=0.03$ has flip $0.0491$ and count reduction
$71.8\%$ at $\delta=0.20$. These values are neither CAL-selected nor
confirmatory and do not certify an online policy. The drift budget $\delta$
could be estimated only if ordered rollouts were retained; on this count-only
carrier it is assumed.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
(i) The public carrier provides pass \emph{counts}, not ordered rollouts.
The oracle identity in Theorem~\ref{thm:tower} is exact only under the true
count-exchangeable law $\trueH$. The implemented $\fitscore$ is a fitted
stopping score, not that conditional identity; its operational statement is
only Theorem~\ref{thm:pop}'s FIT-frozen CAL-screened marginal replay guarantee.
The synthetic stress mechanisms of Section~\ref{sec:drift} are specified
departures from the replay model, not natural-order evidence. No supplied run
compares the stopped answer with gold correctness or records generated tokens,
latency, cancellation acknowledgement, or post-cancellation work; full-vote
agreement and $1-\bar k/N$ cannot substitute for those endpoints.

(ii) The mixture prior is estimated from the 4000 FIT problems; Theorem~\ref{thm:pop}
assumes CAL is an independent sample from the same replay population. The exact
critical radii of Proposition~\ref{prop:tv} are
deterministic fitted-rule sensitivities over an assumed fitted-mixture TV ball
($\tau^*=0.024$--$0.157$ across the named levels and shards); they do not
establish target-population containment, natural-shift robustness, or a
policy-selection prescription. Appendix~\ref{app:tau}(g) records only an
oracle K-wise profile-capping sensitivity: its subset is selected separately
after conditioning on final count $K$, which is unavailable at a prefix. It
is neither a common prefix-state rule nor an instance of
Theorem~\ref{thm:tower}, and supports no stopping-policy, cost, or repair
claim.

(iii) Lemma~\ref{lem:exact}'s monotonicity is proved in closed form
(Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}) and independently checked exhaustively at
$N=32$. (iv) The additional OMR shard, OpenR1, and RLVE results are supplied
setting-specific replay readouts; they do not establish generality or an
allocation law. OpenR1 in particular distinguishes 9{,}374 raw unique
problems from the 8{,}853 exactly-two-deduplicated-rollout analysis population
and its $3000/3000/2853$ split. (v) At $k=N$, the observed pass count equals
$K$, so the binary pass-count replay decision agrees with itself and both the
oracle score and fitted terminal score are zero. Terminal stops add zero replay
flip for every true count; that endpoint fact does not identify fitted earlier
prefix scores with oracle conditional probabilities.

\section{Conclusion}
Under the count-exchangeable replay model, the true law $\trueH$ yields an
exact oracle conditional identity and tower-property theorem. BAYES-H uses a
different fitted score, whose operational claim is only the FIT-frozen,
Bonferroni CAL-screened marginal replay guarantee of
Theorem~\ref{thm:pop}. The supplied OMR descriptive TEST readout reports
$80.9\%$ replay rollout-count reduction at replay flip $0.0249$ for
$\alpha=0.05$, alongside FIXED-HOEF $15.6\%$ and FIXED-EB $46.9\%$ rows.
The drift and margin results remain synthetic, exploratory, and
nonconfirmatory. Natural online order, gold correctness, and actual
token/latency/cancellation costs remain open.

Figure~\ref{fig:tau} is a deterministic visualization of the stored
fitted-rule sensitivity curves. Within a constant fitted-rule interval,
$g$ and hence $V(R)$ are fixed; rule changes create the displayed jumps.
The listed OpenR1 thresholds are algebraic properties of its frozen,
three-atom fitted rule. The plotted carrier comparisons and alignment
statistics are descriptive diagnostics of these stored curves, not a
general cross-carrier relationship, a target-population statement, or a prescription for
choosing $\alpha$.
The nested-FIT-size and rule/prior-channel calculations supplied with the
snapshot are retained only as fitted-rule sensitivity diagnostics. They do
not estimate a population shift, establish a FIT-size requirement, validate a
margin repair, or yield a policy-selection recommendation. Their numerical
details therefore do not enter the paper's current evidence claims.
\emph{(g) Oracle K-wise profile-capping sensitivity (withdrawn as a policy
claim).} The stored fitted-score-ordered subset calculation selects a separate
set $S_K(c)$ after conditioning on each final count $K$ and then evaluates the
coordinate $g_{S_K}(K)$. It is therefore an \emph{oracle K-wise
profile-capping sensitivity calculation}, not the flip profile of one rule.
The final count $K$ is unobserved at every nonterminal prefix, and the
artifacts provide neither a common state table $S(x,k)$ nor a proof that their
K-indexed memberships agree on shared reachable prefix states. Consequently,
this calculation is not a stopping policy, not prefix-observable, and not an
instance of Theorem~\ref{thm:tower}. It cannot support executable or
deployable repair, policy-level count/cost claims, or claims about extra votes,
universal budgets, 70-of-72 cells, domination, edge laws, crossing sets, or
full-simplex robustness. Those prior numerical interpretations are withdrawn;
no new calculation is reported here. Establishing any policy-level repair
would require one K-independent table $S(x,k)$ built only from observable
prefix information, a prefix-measurability check, and recomputation of every
profile and capacity-aware TV LP from that same table.
