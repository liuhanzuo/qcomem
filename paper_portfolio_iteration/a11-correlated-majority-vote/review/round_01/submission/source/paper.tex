\begin{abstract}
Repeatedly sampling a model and taking a majority vote (self-consistency) is a
standard way to spend inference compute, but the vote rarely needs all $N$
samples. We give a
\emph{mixture-posterior stopper} (BAYES-H) whose per-problem conditional flip
certificate is exact: given a calibration prior $H$ over per-task pass counts,
stop as soon as the posterior probability that the full-$N$ majority will
disagree with the current prefix majority drops below $\alpha$. By the tower
property the population flip rate of any such rule is bounded by $\alpha$
\emph{without} a union bound over stopping times (adaptive is free), and the
unknown-mixture layer adds only an empirical-Bernstein calibration step over
problems, with Bonferroni control of the whole searched rule family.
On 11{,}607 math problems with $N=32$ rollouts each (OpenMathReasoning),
selected and evaluated under a strict FIT/CAL/TEST problem split with a single
held-out readout, BAYES-H has an $81\%$ replay rollout-count reduction at realized flip rate
$0.025\le\alpha=0.05$ and $73\%$ at $\alpha=0.02$, dominating the strongest
distribution-free baseline (Hoeffding-fixed-$k$: $16\%$ at $\alpha=0.05$) with
a statistically significant paired gap, and the strongest mixture-aware fixed
budget (empirical-Bernstein fixed-$k$: $47\%$). An ESC-style window heuristic
with an $86\%$ replay count reduction exceeds $0.05$ ($0.058>0.05$). Under
pre-fixed synthetic ordered-drift mechanisms (front-loading, linear trend,
adversarial block swap), BAYES-H is the most robust stopper in the reported
harness, and a simple certificate margin restores flip control only over
stated mechanism--drift ranges.
All conclusions replicate on a disjoint second shard, and survive transferring
the calibration prior across shards (total-variation $0.042$) with $\le0.4$
points of movement. Two further model--carrier pairs bracket the mechanism:
on OpenR1-Math-220k (DeepSeek-R1, $M=2$ per problem) the certificate attains
the replay risk--count frontier exactly when the mixture structure allows, and
never stops under the stated replay certificate when it cannot; on RLVE
(Qwen3-4B, $N=8$ per problem, verifiable environments) it
lands at the intermediate point the mechanism predicts --- $51$--$58\%$
replay count reduction at $\alpha\in\{0.10,0.05\}$, $45$--$48\%$ at
$\alpha\in\{0.02,0.01\}$, certified at \emph{every} level down to
$\alpha=0.01$ where \emph{no} distribution-free replay budget is certifiable.
Every reported flip is disagreement between corresponding binary pass-count
replay decisions, and every saving is $1-\bar k/N$; these artifacts do not
measure a delivered-answer majority, gold correctness, generated tokens,
latency, cancellation, or ordered online deployment.
\end{abstract}

Self-consistency~\citep{wang2023selfconsistency} replaces one sampled answer
by a majority vote over $N$ samples. Adaptive variants reduce or reallocate
sampling: ASC~\citep{aggarwal2023asc} adapts a per-problem count with a
lightweight stopping criterion, ESC~\citep{li2024esc} proposes early stopping
for self-consistency, and MARS~\citep{chen2026mars} stops parallel traces using
a conservative bound on future vote movement. For a count-only replay analysis,
two deployment factors remain outside its evidence: (i) within-task rollouts
may be correlated; and (ii) chronological generation order may drift, breaking
an exchangeability-based certificate.

We make two structural observations that reframe the problem. First, on real
carriers the dominant deviation from i.i.d.\ is not latent within-task
correlation but \emph{across-task heterogeneity}: per-task pass rates are
strongly bimodal (Section~\ref{sec:mixture}), and the flip probability a
stopper must control is a functional of the \emph{mixture} of per-task
difficulty, not of a pooled correlation coefficient. Pooled second-moment
summaries can be simultaneously over-conservative and uncalibrated
(Section~\ref{sec:mixture}). Second, once the target is the mixture, the
correct object is a \emph{per-prefix conditional certificate}: the posterior
probability, given the observed prefix, that the full-$N$ majority disagrees.
This object is exactly computable (hypergeometric posterior), its
fixed-budget expectation $\E_H[f_K(k)]$ is monotone in the prefix
length (Lemma~\ref{lem:exact}), and --- because the tower property
collapses the stopping-time
dependence --- it certifies \emph{any} adaptive rule at level $\alpha$ with no
union bound (Theorem~\ref{thm:tower}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Framework.} A count-exchangeable replay model in which both
within-task correlation and across-task heterogeneity are folded into a
mixture $H$ over per-task pass counts; the certificate target is the flip
probability under $H$, unifying the two effects (Section~\ref{sec:model}).
\item \textbf{Method.} BAYES-H: stop when the posterior conditional flip
probability $c_H(x,k)\le\alpha$. Exact certificates (Lemma~\ref{lem:exact}, Theorem~\ref{thm:tower}); adaptivity is free --- no union
bound, no e-process tuning (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
\item \textbf{Legitimate selection.} A FIT/CAL/TEST problem-level protocol in
which the rule family (budget $\times$ level for fixed-$k$; level for BAYES-H)
is selected on CAL with simultaneous empirical-Bernstein
bounds~\citep{maurer2009eb} plus Bonferroni, and TEST is read once
(Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).
\item \textbf{Empirical replay result.} On 11{,}607 OpenMathReasoning problems,
$80.9\%$ replay rollout-count reduction at realized flip $0.025$ ($\alpha=0.05$), vs $15.6\%$
for the strongest distribution-free baseline at the same level; ESC-style
window stopping is invalid ($0.058>0.05$). Gains are paired-CI significant
(Section~\ref{sec:experiments}). Two further carriers with different models
(DeepSeek-R1 at $M=2$; Qwen3-4B at $N=8$ on verifiable environments) show the
certificate attains the replay risk--count frontier (stop when the replay
certificate permits, otherwise not)
and interpolates with the resolvable mixture structure, i.e.\ the method is
carrier-aware rather than a generic stop-early trick
(Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\item \textbf{Honest boundaries.} The public carrier gives per-task pass
\emph{counts}, not ordered rollouts; it supports a random-prefix
(count-exchangeable) analysis only as a model assumption and cannot validate
ordered online deployment. We therefore bound the failure domain under three
pre-fixed synthetic ordered-drift mechanisms and quantify a certificate-margin repair
(Section~\ref{sec:drift}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Adaptive sampling for voting.} ASC~\citep{aggarwal2023asc} adapts a
per-problem sample count using a lightweight stopping criterion, and
ESC~\citep{li2024esc} proposes early stopping for self-consistency. We do not
establish a finite-sample disagreement-with-full-vote guarantee for either;
on our supplied heterogeneous replay carrier, the instantiated ESC-style rule
exceeds $0.05$ (Table~\ref{tab:main}). MARS~\citep{chen2026mars} is a
parallel trace-stopping method that bounds future vote movement; our work is
instead a sequential count-exchangeable replay certificate. Best-of-$n$ and budget-forcing
methods~\citep{snell2024scaling} tune compute without stopping certificates.

\textbf{Conformal and anytime inference.} Conformal
prediction~\citep{vovk2005algorithmic} gives marginal coverage under
exchangeability; confidence sequences~\citep{howard2021cs} give anytime-valid
bounds for i.i.d.\ means. Our certificate is neither: it is an \emph{exact
posterior flip probability} under a calibrated discrete prior, valid
conditionally per prefix (hence adaptivity is free), with the mixture
estimated from calibration problems under an empirical-Bernstein
bound~\citep{maurer2009eb}. The Bonferroni control of a small pre-declared
rule family is deliberately conservative and transparent.

\textbf{Dependent sampling.} Design-effect corrections~\citep{kish1965deff}
shrink effective sample size by an intraclass correlation; we show pooled
correlation is the wrong functional here (Section~\ref{sec:mixture}).
Beta-binomial and correlated-Bernoulli
models~\citep{skellam1948betabinomial} capture overdispersion but target
estimation, not stopping certificates for the flip event.

\section{Model: count-exchangeable replay}
\label{sec:model}

A task's $N$ rollout outcomes are summarized by the pass count
$K\in\{0,\dots,N\}$. Across tasks, $K\sim H$, a mixture capturing both
task heterogeneity and any within-task correlation: the probability a fresh
rollout passes, marginally, is $\E_H[K]/N$, and the joint law of the $N$
outcomes is exchangeable \emph{within} a task given its realized count.
Analysis model (\emph{replay}): the stopper observes a uniform random
prefix drawn without replacement from the task's $N$ outcomes --- i.e., the
first $k$ draws are exchangeable with the remaining $N-k$.
Section~\ref{sec:drift} stress-tests departures from this model (ordered
drift); our validity claims are stated relative to it, and the carrier
limitation (counts only, no order) is made explicit in
Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

Let $\MV(k)=\mathbf{1}\{x_k>k/2\}$ with ties assigned to the negative side,
and let $\MV(N)=\mathbf{1}\{K>N/2\}$ be the full-$N$ \emph{binary
pass-count replay decision}.  It is not an aggregation of delivered textual
answers or an actual delivered answer-majority: the replay model contains only
binary pass/fail outcomes. The \emph{flip event} of a stopping rule $\tau$ is
$\{\MV(\tau)\neq\MV(N)\}$.

\begin{remark}[Why the mixture, not the correlation]
\label{rem:pooled}
On $\tau^2$-bench carriers (6 public task suites, 4 rollouts/task) we measured
pooled within-task reward correlation $\hat\rho=0.47$--$0.59$, but a
task-demeaned (fixed-effect) estimate gives $\hat\rho_{\mathrm{FE}}\approx-1/3
\approx-1/(N-1)$, the demeaning artifact floor: essentially \emph{all}
observed correlation is across-task heterogeneity, not latent within-task
dependence. On OpenMathReasoning the per-task pass-rate variance exceeds the
binomial baseline by $34\times$. The flip probability is driven by the mass of
tasks with $K\approx N/2$ --- a mixture functional --- so pooled
second-moment discounts are the wrong certificate statistic.
\end{remark}

\section{Method: the mixture-posterior stopper}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Exact per-task flip probability.}
Given count $K$, the prefix count $x_k$ is hypergeometric
$\HG(N,K,k)$, hence
\begin{equation}
f_K(k) \;:=\; \P\big(\MV(k)\neq\MV(N)\,\big|\,K\big)
= \sum_{x}\HG(N,K,k,x)\,\big|\mathrm{side}(x,k)-\mathrm{side}(K,N)\big|.
\label{eq:exact}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Exactness and monotonicity]
\label{lem:exact}
\eqref{eq:exact} is an identity (no approximation). For every fixed $K$,
$f_K(k)$ is nonincreasing in odd $k$; hence for any mixture $H$,
$\E_H[f_K(k)]$ is nonincreasing and
$k^*(\alpha)=\min\{k:\E_H[f_K(k)]\le\alpha\}$ is well defined and
binary-searchable.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The identity is a definition expansion. Monotonicity follows from a
coupling of the prefixes at $k$ and $k{+}2$ on a common ordered draw:
the difference $f_K(k)-f_K(k{+}2)$ reduces to two boundary atoms whose
closed-form ratio is $(K-t)/(N-K-t)$, nonnegative for $K>N/2$, with the
complementary case $K\le N/2$ handled by pass/fail reflection; the full
proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}. Independently, monotonicity is
verified by exhaustive enumeration over all $33\times15$ $(K,k)$ cells
at $N=32$ (zero violations, tolerance $10^{-12}$).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Conditional certificate.}
Given prior $H$ and an observed prefix $(x,k)$, define the posterior flip
probability
\begin{equation}
\cert(x,k) \;:=\; \P_H\big(\mathrm{side}(K,N)\neq\mathrm{side}(x,k)\,\big|\,x,k\big),
\qquad \text{posterior} \propto H[K]\cdot\HG(N,K,k,x).
\label{eq:cond}
\end{equation}
We define the terminal certificate by $\cert(x,N):=0$. At a
posterior-supported terminal state, the hypergeometric likelihood has support
only on $K=x_N$, so this convention equals the conditional flip probability:
$\cert(x_N,N)=0$. It also gives the correct endpoint value for a
zero-prior-mass row of a frozen certificate table, because the observed total
count already fixes the binary full-$N$ replay decision.

\begin{theorem}[Adaptive is free]
\label{thm:tower}
Let $\tau$ be any stopping time measurable w.r.t.\ the prefix filtration that
stops only at states with $\cert(x_\tau,\tau)\le\alpha$. The terminal state
$k=N$ satisfies this same rule because $\cert(x_N,N)=0$. Then
$\P(\MV(\tau)\neq\MV(N)) = \E[\cert(x_\tau,\tau)] \le \alpha$,
with equality in the identity and no union bound over stopping times.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\P(\flip)=\E[\P(\flip\mid\text{prefix path})]$. For any path stopped at
$(x,k)$, $\P(\MV(N)\neq\mathrm{side}(x,k)\mid\text{path})=\cert(x,k)$ since
the posterior is exactly the conditional law of $K$ and $\MV(N)$ is a
function of $K$. At $\tau=N$, $x_N=K$ and hence this conditional probability
is $\cert(x_N,N)=0$; at every earlier stopped state it is at most $\alpha$ by
the rule. Thus the integrand is bounded pointwise by $\alpha$, and taking
expectations gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\textbf{BAYES-H} is the rule $\tau=\min\{k\ge 3:\cert(x_k,k)\le\alpha\}$ with
$H=\widehat H_m$, the calibration empirical mixture. Its per-task exact flip
probability and expected stopping time under any true count $K$ are computable
by a linear-time DP over prefix states $(k,x)$ (Appendix~\ref{app:dp}),
enabling exact calibration and evaluation without Monte Carlo.

\begin{theorem}[Population layer]
\label{thm:pop}
Let $g(K;\alpha,\widehat H_m)$ be the exact per-task flip probability of
BAYES-H under count $K$, and let
$\bar g_{\calD}$, $s^2_{\calD}$ be its mean and variance over $m$ calibration
problems. With probability $1-\delta$ over the calibration sample,
\[
\P_{\text{replay},H}(\flip) \;\le\; \bar g_{\calD}
+ \sqrt{\tfrac{2 s^2_{\calD}\log(4/\delta)}{m}}
+ \tfrac{7\log(4/\delta)}{3(m-1)},
\]
by the empirical-Bernstein inequality~\citep{maurer2009eb} applied to the
bounded per-task flips $g\in[0,1]$, with $\delta$ split by Bonferroni over the
pre-declared selection family ($J=64$ candidates; Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).
\end{theorem}
\begin{remark}[No Jensen gap]
The population flip rate under replay is a \emph{linear} functional of $H$:
$\P(\flip)=\sum_K H[K]\,g(K)$. Hence estimation error in $\widehat H_m$ enters
only through the empirical mean of a bounded per-task quantity --- there is no
Jensen correction, and empirical Bernstein is essentially tight at $m=4000$.
\end{remark}
\begin{proposition}[Exact TV-robustness certificate]
\label{prop:tv}
Fix the rule (certificate tables frozen from $\widehat H$) and let $g(K)$ be
its exact per-task flip probability. Over the total-variation ball
$B_R=\{q:q\ge0,\sum_K q_K=1,\|q-\widehat H\|_1\le 2R\}$,
\[
V(R)\;:=\;\max_{q\in B_R}\;\textstyle\sum_K q_K\,g(K)
\]
is a linear program whose optimum is a two-sided greedy: add mass to atoms in
descending $g$ (capped by $q_K\le1$) and drain mass from atoms in ascending
$g$ (capped by $\widehat H_K$); $V(R)$ is hence computable in closed form in
$O(N\log N)$, and it strictly refines the $1$-Lipschitz bound
$\flip(\widehat H)+R$ whenever the $g$-profile is non-constant. The
\emph{critical radius} $\tau^*(\alpha)=\max\{R:V(R)\le\alpha\}$ quantifies how
much prior shift the frozen certificate provably tolerates at level $\alpha$.
\end{proposition}
On the OMR carrier the critical radii are
$\tau^*(0.10/0.05/0.02/0.01)=0.144/0.078/0.074/0.052$
(shard~0) and $0.157/0.082/0.076/0.024$ (shard~1)
(\texttt{tv\_robustness\_r481\_result.json}; the closed form matches a direct
simplex LP to $10^{-6}$ on a $6\times4\times2$ grid of radii, levels and
shards). Three consequences: \emph{(i)} at the observed cross-shard mismatch
$R=0.042$, the worst-case flip over the whole ball is $0.039\le\alpha=0.05$
and $0.016\le0.02$, so the cross-shard transfer of
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} is certified \emph{post hoc} on both shards
--- but on shard~1, $\tau^*(0.01)=0.024<0.042$, i.e.\ TV alone does not
certify the transfer at $\alpha=0.01$, which is exactly the level the
transfer experiment does not report; \emph{(ii)} a \emph{prior-shift validity
band} follows directly: certifying the frozen tables at the tightened level
$\alpha-R$ keeps the worst-case flip over $B_R$ below $\alpha$ whenever the
re-certified rule's $V(R)\le\alpha$; at $R=0.05$, $\alpha=0.10$ re-certified
at $0.05$ has worst-case flip $0.042\le0.10$ and still has $80.3\%$ replay
count reduction (vs $83.6\%$ without the band) --- a $3.3$-point price for validity
uniform over a $5$-point TV neighbourhood; \emph{(iii)} at every level a $36$--$47\%$ mass of atoms has
$g(K)=0$ exactly (the certificate stops only on states the prefix already
decides), which is why $V(R)$ grows far slower than the Lipschitz rate
$R\cdot\max_K g(K)$.
\emph{Cross-carrier.} On the RLVE carrier ($N{=}8$; same frozen FIT prior as
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}, no TEST re-read) the same closed form gives
$\tau^*(0.10/0.05/0.02/0.01)=0.199/0.551/0.213/0.449$
(\texttt{tv\_robustness\_rlve\_r482\_result.json}; LP cross-check matches to
$10^{-6}$). The radii are \emph{larger} than on OMR at every level even
though the RLVE prior is no more heterogeneous (het-excess variance
$0.0999$ vs $0.129$): the RLVE prior concentrates $74$--$87\%$ of its mass
on \emph{zero}-$g$ atoms (vs $36$--$47\%$ on OMR), and with only $N{=}8$
atoms the worst-case adversary has few high-$g$ directions in which to
move mass. Across carriers $\tau^*$ co-varies with the \emph{alignment} of
the prior with the zero-$g$ set and is uncorrelated with heterogeneity
(het-excess vs $\tau^*$: Spearman $0.01$; het-excess vs alignment: $0.05$
--- the two candidate drivers are not confounded). The
alignment--$\tau^*$ co-variation is a \emph{between-carrier} law: pooled
Spearman $0.75$ over the 16 carrier$\times$level cells, $1.0$ over the
four carrier means, $0.63$ with the two whole-simplex-plateau cells
removed; \emph{within} OMR it is negative ($-0.95$/$-0.89$ per shard ---
tightening the level moves the zero-g set only at the most-aggressive
end, where mass is added on the lowest-$g$ atoms), positive within RLVE
($0.32$) and OpenR1 ($1.0$) (decomposition audit:
\texttt{spearman\_decomposition\_r486\_result.json};
Appendix~\ref{app:tau}). (Two disclosures: $\tau^*$
bisection uses $R\in[0,1]$ here ($0.551>0.5$ exceeds the $[0,0.5]$ range
sufficient for OMR); $\tau^*$ is non-monotone in $\alpha$ ---
$\tau^*(0.05)>\tau^*(0.10)$ --- because tightening $\alpha$ zeroes $K{=}6$
($g:0.107\!\to\!0$) and shrinks $K{=}5$ ($0.339\!\to\!0.071$), flattening
the $g$-profile's top faster than the budget tightens; internal drops
exist as well (the curve has $7$ rule-change breakpoints on the plotted
range, Figure~\ref{fig:tau}), so $\tau^*$ should not be read as globally
monotone in $\alpha$.)
\emph{Three-atom edge case.} On OpenR1 ($M{=}2$; same frozen FIT prior, no
TEST re-read) the support is three atoms $p\in\{0,\tfrac12,1\}$ and
$g(0)=g(1)=0$ at every level (a decided prefix cannot be overturned by one
more rollout), so the worst case is a pure one-sided pump into the unique
positive-$g$ atom: $V(R)=\flip(\widehat H)+g_{\max}R$ exactly and
$\tau^*(\alpha)=\alpha/g_{\max}-\widehat H(\tfrac12)$ in closed form
(\texttt{tv\_robustness\_openr1\_r483\_result.json}; scan match $10^{-9}$,
LP $10^{-6}$). Since $g_{\max}$ tracks the certificates ($0.0785/0.0460$):
$\tau^*(0.10)=\tau^*(0.05)=0.168$ (halving $\alpha$ past $0.0785$ halves
$g_{\max}$, $0.25\!\to\!0.125$), while $\tau^*(0.02)=\tau^*(0.01)=1$ ---
below the smallest certificate the frozen rule never stops, $V(R)\equiv0$,
no prior shift can break it. Non-monotonicity is thus maximal here
($0.168\!\to\!1$); zero-$g$ mass $76.8\%$ sits between OMR's $36$--$47\%$
and RLVE's $74$--$87\%$, completing the pattern from the low-support side.
\emph{Conservation curve.} Appendix~\ref{app:tau} maps
$\tau^*(\alpha)$ on the dense continuum $\alpha\in[10^{-3},0.2]$ for all
three carriers: the curve is analytically staircase-shaped ---
non-decreasing inside each constant-rule interval, so that every jump of
$\tau^*$ is a strict \emph{drop} exactly at a certificate threshold
(analytic on OpenR1: $\tau^*$ drops by $0.314$ at $\alpha=0.0785$ and by
$0.864$ at $0.0460$). The practical reading is a
staircase interpretation of the certificate level: at a rule-change
breakpoint, a small budget relaxation buys a discrete jump in certified
drift tolerance, so $\alpha$ should be set on a step, not inside one.

\begin{remark}[Continuous-$\alpha$ selection is free for \textsc{Bayes-H}]
\label{rem:nested}
The stopping condition $c_{\widehat H}(x,k)\le\alpha$ does not involve
$\alpha$ on the left-hand side, so the \textsc{Bayes-H} rule family is
\emph{nested} in $\alpha$: as $\alpha$ varies continuously, the rule changes
only at the finitely many breakpoints given by the distinct values of
$c_{\widehat H}(x,k)$ --- at most $\sum_{k=3}^{N}(k+1)$ stoppable states
($555$ at $N{=}32$; $267$ distinct values observed, plus the never-stop
rule). These are three distinct counts: $555$ stoppable states bound the
possible rule changes; only $267$ \emph{distinct} certificate values are
observed on the support, and the rule family has one rule per value
\emph{plus} the never-stop rule ($268$ in total; shrunk to $182$ below
for the tested range). A union bound over this breakpoint family
therefore
certifies the \emph{entire continuum} $\alpha\in(0,0.10]$
simultaneously --- no pre-declared $\alpha$ grid is needed. The family can
be shrunk further with no loss: for the range $\alpha\in(0,0.10]$ only the
$181$ distinct positive certificate values ${\le}0.10$ are
rule-distinguishing (values $>0.10$ never trigger any rule in the range;
zero-certificate states stop under every $\alpha>0$), so the exact
effective family has $182$ rules ($J=6{,}501$ including the FIXED-$k$ part)
instead of the $9{,}339$ obtained by counting all observed breakpoints; the
four reference selections below are unchanged and their UCBs tighten by
${\le}6\times10^{-4}$ (\texttt{prop\_562bound\_r479\_result.json}). On our
calibration pool the enlarged family
($J_{\rm cont}=9{,}339$ vs.\ $J=64$) inflates UCBs only through the
logarithmic factor; the selected replay-count-optimal \textsc{Bayes-H} rule is
bit-for-bit identical at $\alpha\in\{0.10,0.05,0.02\}$ and tightens only at
$\alpha=0.01$ (mean $k$ $10.3\!\to\!11.9$), while the weaker FIXED-EB
baseline loses its $\alpha=0.02$ budget under \emph{either} family size
(\texttt{alpha\_continuous\_r478\_result.json}).
\end{remark}

\section{Protocol: FIT/CAL/TEST with a legitimate selection family}
\label{sec:protocol}

\textbf{Split.} 11{,}607 problems are partitioned once (seeded) into FIT
(4000), CAL (4000), TEST (3607). FIT estimates $\widehat H$ and freezes the
BAYES-H certificate tables. CAL selects rules from the pre-declared family:
fixed-$k$ candidates $k\in\{3,5,\dots,31\}\times\alpha\in\{0.10,0.05,0.02,
0.01\}$ (60 certificates) and BAYES-H at the same 4 levels, with simultaneous
empirical-Bernstein UCBs at per-test $\delta=0.05/64$. $k^*(\alpha)$ is the
smallest budget whose CAL UCB is $\le\alpha$; BAYES-H is accepted at level
$\alpha$ iff its CAL UCB is $\le\alpha$. TEST is read exactly once. All
evaluation uses the exact per-task flip functions (\eqref{eq:exact} for
fixed-$k$; the DP of Appendix~\ref{app:dp} for BAYES-H), so TEST numbers are
exact replay quantities, not MC estimates; the DP itself is cross-checked
against replay MC (max deviation $0.043$ within $3\sigma_{\rm MC}=0.075$).

\textbf{Baselines.} FULL-$N$; FIXED-HOEF (fixed budget selected with
distribution-free Hoeffding UCBs, the strongest model-free competitor);
FIXED-EB (fixed budget with the same mixture-aware empirical-Bernstein
machinery as BAYES-H --- isolates the value of adaptivity);
BAYES-UNIF (uniform prior; not a certificate when the prior is wrong);
WINDOW3 (ESC-style: stop when 3 consecutive samples agree with the current
majority --- no certificate knob).

\paragraph{Fair comparison.} Against the strongest distribution-free baseline

count reduction; at $\alpha=0.02$ the baseline has \emph{no} certifiable budget while
BAYES-H still has a $73.5\%$ reduction. Against the strongest mixture-aware fixed budget
(FIXED-EB), adaptivity adds $+34$ points of replay count reduction at $\alpha=0.05$

problem sources (train on three, test on the fourth), BAYES-H stays valid on

\paragraph{Second-shard replication and prior-mismatch transfer.}
We replicate the entire chain on a disjoint carrier, OpenMathReasoning CoT
shard~1 (11{,}454 problems, same $N{=}32$ pass counts; SHA-256 pinned), whose
mixture structure is near-identical (heterogeneity excess $0.125$ vs $0.129$;
$44.4\%$ extreme-$p$ mass). Two runs share the CAL/TEST problems of shard~1 and
differ only in the prior's provenance. \emph{(i) Within-shard}: the prior is
fit on shard~1's own FIT split; every qualitative replay conclusion reproduces ---
BAYES-H has $80.6\%$ replay count reduction at realized flip $0.025$ ($\alpha=0.05$) and $73.1\%$ at
$0.009$ ($\alpha=0.02$), vs $15.6\%$ and \emph{no certifiable budget} for
FIXED-HOEF. \emph{(ii) Cross-shard prior transfer}: the prior is fit on
shard~0 FIT (total-variation distance to the shard~1 prior $=0.042$) and the
certificate is selected and read on shard~1. All numbers move by $\le0.4$
points (count reduction $80.6\%$/$73.2\%$; flips $0.025$/$0.009$). This is a
small movement in one supplied count-replay transfer, not a demonstration of
robustness to naturally ordered online rollouts (Section~\ref{sec:mixture}).

\paragraph{Second model--carrier pair: OpenR1-Math-220k (DeepSeek-R1, $M=2$).}
To probe the mechanism under a different model and a structurally opposite
carrier, we run the identical chain on OpenR1-Math-220k (MIT; 9{,}374 unique
problems, two deduplicated DeepSeek-R1 rollouts each, HF snapshot
\texttt{e4e141ec}). Under the replay model, the full vote is
the 2-vote majority (ties broken by a pre-drawn fair coin), the only stoppable
prefix is $k=1$, and per-problem risk is analytic. The only identifiable
prior at $M=2$ is coarse (three atoms $p\in\{0,\tfrac12,1\}$, fit by exact
pair-type moment matching): $25\%$ always-fail, $23\%$ coin-flip, $52\%$
always-pass. Results (FIT/CAL/TEST 3000/3000/2853):
\emph{(i)} the conditional certificate attains the replay risk--count \emph{frontier}:
at $\alpha=0.20$ it stops on every problem (flip $0.059\le\alpha$,
$50\%$ replay count reduction); at $\alpha=0.10$ full stopping has the tightest
distribution-free UCB $0.080\le\alpha$ and is certified with the same
realized flip and count reduction; at $\alpha=0.05$ it stops only on the
sub-population whose observed prefix certifies (positive-side prefix),
giving $32\%$ replay count reduction at flip $0.029\le\alpha$; at $\alpha=0.02$ it \emph{never}
stops --- and no legal certificate can, since stopping at $k{=}1$ on any
problem carries posterior risk $>0.02$; \emph{(ii)} the realized flip of
always stopping at $k{=}1$ ($0.059$) matches its analytic value
$\E_H[p(1{-}p)]=0.058$ to Monte-Carlo precision. Replay count reduction is bought by mixture \emph{structure} that a prefix can
resolve: with $M=2$ there is at most one draw of prefix information, and the
certificate extracts exactly the count reduction that structure allows --- $50\%$ at
loose levels, partial at $\alpha=0.05$, honestly nothing at $\alpha=0.02$ ---
versus $81\%$/$73\%$ at $\alpha=0.05/0.02$ on the $N{=}32$ OMR carrier. This
is cross-model replay evidence for the stated mechanism; it does not test
chronological online deployment, gold correctness, or operational cost.

\paragraph{Third model--carrier pair: RLVE (Qwen3-4B, $N=8$).} The two
carriers above close the extremes ($N{=}32$ rich-prefix vs $M{=}2$
near-prefix-free). To probe the intermediate regime under a third model
family and a different task family, we run the identical chain on RLVE
teacher rollouts (Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507, pass@8 on 9{,}000 verifiable
counting/combinatorics environments; Apache-2.0; HF snapshot
\texttt{eaeec946}). Success is the offline Gym-verifier reward being positive
(a small mass of fractional partial credit is counted as failure). The
mixture is again strongly U-shaped ($42\%$ extreme problems, heterogeneity
excess $0.100$ over the binomial baseline). Results (FIT/CAL/TEST
3000/3000/3000): \emph{(i)} BAYES-H has replay count reduction $57.6\%$/$50.9\%$/$48.2\%$/$45.4\%$
at $\alpha=0.10/0.05/0.02/0.01$ with realized flips
$0.031$/$0.010$/$0.005$/$0.002$ --- conservative at every level;
\emph{(ii)} the certificate passes at \emph{every} level down to
$\alpha=0.01$, while \emph{no} distribution-free budget is certifiable below
$\alpha=0.10$ (FIXED-EB and FIXED-Hoeffding both null at
$\alpha\le0.05$) --- at $\alpha=0.10$ the paired replay-count-reduction gap over
FIXED-Hoeffding is $+0.20$ (CI radius $0.03$, significant); \emph{(iii)} the
exact DP prediction matches a 3{,}000-draw replay Monte-Carlo on held-out
problems (flip within $0.004$, mean $k$ within $0.04$). Two descriptive
diagnostics: the recorded sample-id success slope is flat
(pooled slope $-7.8\times10^{-5}$/trial; per-position rates $0.316$--$0.326$),
consistent with near-exchangeable sampling in this carrier; and the replay count reduction
interpolates with resolvable structure exactly as the mechanism predicts
($81\%$ at $N{=}32$ with 15 stoppable budgets, $45$--$58\%$ at $N{=}8$ with
3, $32$--$50\%$ at $M{=}2$ with 1; all in-distribution replay readouts collected in
Appendix~\ref{app:matrix}). The sample-id diagnostic does not audit an online
policy or make the count-replay certificate valid for deployment order.

\paragraph{Why the window heuristic fails.} On mid-difficulty problems
($K\approx16$), three consecutive agreements with the current majority is a
\emph{common} event under near-coin-flip draws, not evidence the vote has
settled: WINDOW3 realizes flip $0.058$ at mean $k=4.4$. Its flip rate is
nearly flat in $\alpha$ (it has no certificate knob): it can only be tuned by
window length, and its certified population UCB is $0.061$
(\texttt{adaptive\_sweep\_r468\_result.json}), so it barely misses even the
loosest tested level $\alpha=0.10$ on the full OMR test pool.

\section{Synthetic ordered drift: failure domain and margin repair}
\label{sec:drift}

The replay model assumes prefixes are exchangeable with the held remainder.
Because the carrier contains no observed chronology, we apply three
\emph{pre-fixed synthetic} drift mechanisms to its true counts
($\delta\in\{0,0.05,0.10,0.15,0.20\}$, 2000 TEST problems, 500 orders each):
\textbf{E1} (front-load: the first $k$ draws are enriched/depleted in passes
by count $D=\lceil 2\delta\min(K,N{-}K)\rceil$), \textbf{E2} (linear per-draw
pass-rate trend $p_j=K/N+\delta(2j/(N{-}1){-}1)$), \textbf{E3} (adversarial
block: the last $\lceil\delta N\rceil$ draws drift toward the minority side).

Findings (full tables in Appendix~\ref{app:drift}):
(i) all methods break under E1 by $\delta=0.10$--$0.15$ in this harness; the certificate is
honest about its premise --- E1 violates exchangeability by construction;
(ii) under the gentler E2/E3, BAYES-H is the \emph{most} robust stopper in this synthetic grid: at
$\alpha=0.10$ it remains valid to $\delta=0.20$ under E2 (flip $0.083$) while
FIXED-$k$ breaks at $\delta=0.15$ and WINDOW3 breaks at $\delta=0.20$
(flip $0.104$; under E3 WINDOW3 stays valid throughout, its last-window
rule never reaching the drifting tail); at $\alpha=0.05$ under E3 it
breaks only at $\delta=0.15$ (flip $0.052$), while FIXED-EB survives (it stops
at $k=17$, having seen half the block); (iii) validity under drift is bought
by margin: stopping only when $\cert\le\alpha-\gamma$ restores validity at a
quantified replay-count cost. Under E3 at $\alpha=0.05$, margin $\gamma=0.025$
covers $\delta\le0.15$ (flip $0.0495$, replay count reduction $77.2\%$ vs $81.0\%$
unmargined in the stress harness; the unmargined exact TEST readout of
Table~\ref{tab:main} is $80.9\%$); covering the extreme $\delta=0.20$
adversarial block requires
$\gamma>0.03$ (count-reduction cost $>7$ points), and under the smoother E2 trend
$\gamma=0.03$ covers the full tested range $\delta\le0.20$ (flip $0.0491$,
count reduction $71.8\%$). The drift budget $\delta$ could be estimated on CAL
only if ordered rollouts are retained; on this count-only carrier it is assumed.
This synthetic analysis neither measures nor certifies natural online order.

\section{The mixture is the object}
\label{sec:mixture}

Three converging lines of evidence motivate the mixture framework.
(a) \emph{Pooled correlation is heterogeneity}: on $\tau^2$-bench, fixed-effect
demeaning collapses $\hat\rho$ from $0.5$ to the $-1/(N{-}1)$ artifact floor
(Remark~\ref{rem:pooled}).
(b) \emph{Ignoring the mixture undercovers}: a synthetic probit cluster study
($N\in\{10,20,40\}$, 2500 tasks/cell) shows i.i.d.-conformal stopping
undercovers under heterogeneous pass rates (agreement $0.983<0.99$ target),
while pooled-$\rho$ corrections are valid but over-conservative.
(c) \emph{The plug-in mixture estimate undercovers too}: certifying with
$\E_{\widehat H}[f_K(k)]$ directly yields bootstrap p95 $0.051>\alpha=0.05$ at
$k=11$; the empirical-Bernstein layer is what restores finite-sample validity.
Point-estimate correctness $\neq$ certificate calibration --- this is the
paper's central methodological point.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
(i) The public carrier provides pass \emph{counts}, not ordered rollouts: all
main results are exact only under the count-exchangeable replay model; the
synthetic stress mechanisms of Section~\ref{sec:drift} only characterize
specified departures from that model. The certificate is a replay-model conditional
certificate, not an e-process: validity under arbitrary online stopping
times in ordered deployment is outside the stated claims. No supplied run
compares the stopped answer with gold correctness, or records generated tokens,
latency, cancellation acknowledgement, or post-cancellation work; full-vote
agreement and $1-\bar k/N$ cannot substitute for those endpoints. (ii) The mixture prior is estimated from 4000
calibration problems of the same distribution; adversarial mixture shift
beyond the tested transfer range can break the certificate; the exact
critical radii of Proposition~\ref{prop:tv} quantify the tolerable shift
($\tau^*=0.024$--$0.157$ across levels and shards) and show the failure
boundary is sharp --- at $\alpha=0.01$ on shard~1 the observed cross-shard
mismatch $0.042$ already exceeds $\tau^*(0.01)=0.024$; and the radii are
\emph{rule-conditional}: $\tau^*(\alpha)$ is a staircase with jumps at the
certificate thresholds (Appendix~\ref{app:tau}), so the level $\alpha$
should be read as choosing a step of that staircase, not a point of a
smooth trade-off. Along the estimation axis, Appendix~\ref{app:tau}(e)
shows $\tau^*$ is not monotone in the prior-fit size $m$; the
decomposition of Appendix~\ref{app:tau}(f) attributes this to the
\emph{rule} the fit induces (freezing the prior leaves the
non-monotonicity intact; freezing the rule removes the largest dips),
not the prior's sampling error. Appendix~\ref{app:tau}(g) turns that
attribution into a constructive repair: a per-atom flip budget
implemented as a certificate-ordered subset of stopping states provably
dominates the induced profile, restores the full simplex
($\tau^*=1$, trivial-validity regime) at $70$ of $72$ cells for
$+1\%$--$+11\%$ extra votes, and repairs the coarse-fit breaks below
--- while two alternatives (prior support truncation, deadline
truncation) provably do not. On OMR shard~1 the coarsest fit
$m{=}125$ breaks the
transferred certificate at $\alpha\le0.02$ while $m\ge250$ --- or the
flip-budget repair at any $m$ --- restores it.
Appendix~\ref{app:matrix} collects every in-distribution readout and its
failure boundary in one matrix.
(iii) Lemma~\ref{lem:exact}'s monotonicity is proved in closed form
(Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}) and independently verified exhaustively at
$N=32$.
(iv) Main-table counts come from one model--task pair
(Qwen2.5-Math-72B-TIR, math problems); generality across problem pools is
supported by the disjoint second-shard replication and cross-shard prior
transfer, and generality across models and task families by two further
model--carrier pairs --- DeepSeek-R1 rollouts on OpenR1-Math-220k ($M=2$),
on which the certificate attains its replay risk--count frontier, and
Qwen3-4B rollouts on RLVE verifiable environments ($N=8$), on which it is
certified at every tested level and interpolates with the resolvable mixture
structure (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
(v) At $k=N$, the observed pass count equals $K$, so the binary pass-count
replay decision agrees with itself and $\cert(x_N,N)=0$. Terminal stops add
zero replay flip for every true count; this endpoint convention is shared by
Theorem~\ref{thm:tower} and the DP in Appendix~\ref{app:dp}.

\section{Conclusion}
Treating across-task heterogeneity as the certificate object turns early
stopping for majority voting into an exact conditional-inference problem under
the replay model: adaptivity is free (tower property), calibration is one
empirical-Bernstein step over problems, and the supplied OMR readout has an
$81\%$ replay rollout-count reduction at full-vote flip level $0.05$, where
the strongest distribution-free baseline has $16\%$ and the strongest fixed
mixture-aware budget $47\%$. The same framework admits a synthetic
ordered-drift analysis with a margin repair, while natural online order, gold
correctness, and actual token/latency/cancellation costs remain open.

\section{The critical-radius conservation curve}
\label{app:tau}
\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{fig_tau_conservation.png}
\caption{Critical radius $\tau^*(\alpha)$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:tv} on the continuum $\alpha\in[10^{-3},0.2]$
(step $5\!\times\!10^{-4}$), computed exactly per level from the frozen
$g$-profiles of the three carriers (OMR both FIT shards; OpenR1 exact in
closed form). Grey tick marks: the four reference levels; red dotted
lines: the OpenR1 certificate thresholds $0.0785/0.0460$ where the frozen
rule changes. Artifact:
\texttt{tv\_conservation\_r484\_result.json}.}
\label{fig:tau}
\end{figure}
Figure~\ref{fig:tau} makes the rule-change structure of the certificate
visible. \emph{(a) Staircase law.} Within any $\alpha$-interval on which
the frozen rule is unchanged, the whole vector $g$ --- hence $V(R)$ ---
is constant, so $\tau^*(\alpha)=\max\{R:V(R)\le\alpha\}$ is
\emph{non-decreasing}; every strict decrease of $\tau^*$ coincides with a
rule-change breakpoint (verified at every grid cell of every carrier;
the rule-change counts on the plotted range are $96/93/7/2$ for OMR
shard~0 / OMR shard~1 / RLVE / OpenR1).
\emph{(b) Analytic breakpoints on OpenR1.} With
$V(R)=\flip(\widehat H)+g_{\max}R$ exactly,
$\tau^*(\alpha)=\alpha/g_{\max}-\widehat H(\tfrac12)$: at
$\alpha=0.07852$ the fail-prefix stop engages and $g_{\max}$ doubles
($0.125\!\to\!0.25$), so $\tau^*$ jumps \emph{down} $0.396\!\to\!0.082$
($\Delta=-0.314$); at $\alpha=0.04598$ the pass-prefix stop disengages
and the frozen rule degenerates to never-stop ($V(R)\equiv0$ for any
prior), so the whole-simplex plateau $\tau^*=1$ holds \emph{below} this
threshold and $\tau^*$ jumps \emph{down} $1\!\to\!0.136$
($\Delta=-0.864$) as $\alpha$ crosses it upward. \emph{(c) Whole-simplex plateaus.}
Wherever the frozen rule's worst case cannot exceed $\alpha$ for
\emph{any} prior ($\max_K g(K)\le\alpha$), $\tau^*=1$: RLVE for
$\alpha<0.002$ and OpenR1 for $\alpha<0.0460$; OMR's rule never stops
only below $10^{-3}$, off the plotted range. \emph{(d) Alignment
co-variation (decomposed).} The zero-$g$ alignment number co-varies with
$\tau^*$ at pooled Spearman $0.75$ across the 16 carrier$\times$level
cells (descriptive; the per-cell alignment is itself $\alpha$-dependent).
The decomposition audit
(\texttt{spearman\_decomposition\_r486\_result.json}) shows this is a
between-carrier law, not a within-carrier one: $1.0$ across the four
carrier means, $0.63$ on the 14 non-plateau cells, $0.44$--$0.90$ under
leave-one-carrier-out; within-carrier it is $-0.95$/$-0.89$ (OMR shards),
$+0.32$ (RLVE), $+1.0$ (OpenR1) --- the OMR reversal is the staircase
mechanism itself (level tightening adds zero-g mass on the lowest-$g$
atoms only). Heterogeneity is not a confounder: het-excess vs $\tau^*$
Spearman $0.01$, het-excess vs alignment $0.05$ (16 cells).
\emph{Practical reading.} The staircase shape is a design signal for
choosing the certificate level: inside a constant-rule interval,
loosening $\alpha$ buys certified drift tolerance gradually; at a
breakpoint it buys (or loses) a discrete jump --- $\alpha$ should be set
on a step, not inside one.
\emph{(e) How much FIT data buys how much robustness.} Rebuilding the
prior from nested prefixes of the frozen FIT order at
$m\in\{\tfrac{1}{32},\dots,1\}\times m_{\mathrm{full}}$ and recomputing
$\tau^*_m(\alpha)$ exactly
(\texttt{prior\_fit\_size\_r491\_result.json}) gives an operational size
rule. On OMR the $\tau^*_m(0.05)$ curves already clear the observed
cross-shard transfer $0.042$ at the smallest tested pool $m{=}125$
(shard~0: $0.062$; shard~1: $0.046$), i.e.\ cross-shard robustness does
\emph{not} require the full $4000$-problem fit. The radius is
\emph{not} monotone in $m$ (OMR shard~1 at $\alpha=0.01$: $0.010$ at
$m{=}125$, up to $0.056$ at $m{=}1000$, back to $0.024$ at $m{=}4000$):
growing the fit moves both the prior (better) and the frozen rule
(larger $g_{\max}$ possible), and the rule channel can dominate ---
$\tau^*_m$ is a property of the fitted \emph{pair} $(\widehat H_m,
\text{rule}_m)$, not of the sample size alone. The closed loop of
Proposition~\ref{prop:tv} holds at every one of the 51 cells with
$\mathrm{TV}(\widehat H_m,\widehat H_{\mathrm{full}})\le\tau^*_m$: the
$m$-rule's exact flip under the full-data prior never exceeds $\alpha$
(0 violations), while at the coarsest OMR shard~1 fit ($m{=}125$, TV
$0.175$, outside the ball) the transferred rule genuinely breaks
(flip $0.0244>0.02$, $0.0165>0.01$) and is repaired from $m{=}250$
onward --- inside/outside the radius predicts validity/failure exactly.
The estimation error itself scales as $\mathrm{TV}_m\approx C/\sqrt m$
(within-carrier spread of $\mathrm{TV}_m\sqrt m$ below $1.5\times$ on OMR;
the RLVE spread reaches $3.5\times$ only because its coarsest prefixes
carry a missing-mode error $\sim\!\sqrt{m_{\mathrm{full}}/m}$ larger).
\emph{(f) Which channel moves $\tau^*_m$: rule, not prior --- with one
caveat.} We decompose the $m$-sensitivity into its two moving parts by
computing, at every $(\text{carrier},m,\alpha)$ cell, the two
counterfactual radii (\texttt{rule\_channel\_r494\_result.json}):
$\tau^{*\,\mathrm{pf}}_m=\tau^*(\widehat H_{\mathrm{full}},g_m)$ freezes
the prior at the full fit and lets only the \emph{rule} vary with $m$,
and $\tau^{*\,\mathrm{rf}}_m=\tau^*(\widehat H_m,g_{\mathrm{full}})$
freezes the rule and lets only the \emph{prior} vary. The recomputed
both-moving curve reproduces the $m$-grid of
\texttt{prior\_fit\_size\_r491\_result.json} bit-exactly (0/72
mismatches). Three findings. \emph{(i) The rule channel drives the
non-monotonicity.} With the prior frozen at $\widehat H_{\mathrm{full}}$,
$\tau^{*\,\mathrm{pf}}_m$ is still non-monotone at OMR shard~1
$\alpha{=}0.01$: $0.000$ at $m{=}125$ rising to $0.060$ at $m{=}1000$
then falling to $0.024$ at $m{=}4000$ --- the same up-then-down shape as
the both-moving curve --- because the refitted rule's $g_{\max}$ itself
varies non-monotonically ($0.563\to0.075\to0.098$) as the coarse prefix
shifts which budgets are certifiable. \emph{(ii) The estimation channel
is benign on average but \emph{not} uniformly.} Holding the rule fixed,
a coarser prior can \emph{shrink} the radius below the full-fit value in
31 of 72 cells (all on OMR shard~1 and RLVE; largest deficit $0.053$ at
RLVE $m{=}93$, $\alpha{=}0.05$). The mechanism is the base-flip term
$\sum_K \widehat H[K]\,g(K)$: a coarse prefix that under-covers the
likely-correct (low-$g$) region and over-weights high-$g$ mass raises the
base flip, and --- because the \emph{frozen full rule} already has large
$g$ exactly on those atoms --- the certificate has less slack to spend on
drift. This is the missing-mode failure of (e) seen through the rule:
the same coarse prefix that carries a $\sim\!\sqrt{m_{\mathrm{full}}/m}$
estimation error also pays it on the worst (largest-$g$) coordinates.
\emph{(iii) The RLVE coarsest dip is pure rule-channel.} The collapse of
the both-moving radius at RLVE $m{=}187$, $\alpha{=}0.05$ ($0.235$, far
below both $m{=}93$ at $0.616$ and $m{=}375$ at $0.534$) survives with
the prior frozen ($\tau^{*\,\mathrm{pf}}_{187}=0.222$) and vanishes with
the rule frozen ($\tau^{*\,\mathrm{rf}}_{187}=0.530$): it is entirely a
$g_{\max}$ event ($0.179$ vs.\ $0.071$ for the full rule), not an
estimation one. \emph{Reading.} The robustness certificate is governed by
the \emph{rule} the fit induces more than by the prior's sampling error:
two fits of the same size can certify very different radii because the
induced rule differs, and the only lever that reliably buys radius is one
that shrinks the induced $g$-profile (e.g.\ the margin repair of
Section~\ref{sec:drift}), not one that merely adds FIT data.
\emph{(g) A constructive repair that buys radius: flip-budget state
subsets.} The reading of (f) --- shrink the induced $g$-profile --- is
here made executable
(\texttt{full\_sweep\_r498e\_result.json}). Fix a per-atom flip budget
$c$. On each atom $K$, order the original rule's stopping states by
their certificate value and keep the longest prefix whose cumulative
flip contribution is at most $c$; states not kept run to $n$. The
realized subset rule satisfies, pointwise on every atom,
$g_S(K)\le\min(g_{\mathrm{orig}}(K),c)$ (domination), its stopping set
is a subset of the original rule's (it never stops earlier, so the
replay-count cost is only extra votes), and its exact radius is
recomputed by the same linear program --- nothing is re-estimated from
data. Sweeping $c\in\{0.30,\dots,0.01\}$ over all 72
carrier$\times m\times\alpha$ cells of (e): recomputation anchors the
grid bit-exactly (0/72 mismatches), domination holds at every atom of
every cell, and at the best cap every cell reaches $\tau^*=1$: for
$70$ of $72$ cells this is a strict gain over the original radius at
$+1\%$ to $+11\%$ mean votes (max $+11.3\%$ at OMR
shard~0 $m{=}500$, $\alpha{=}0.10$; RLVE never exceeds $+3.3\%$),
while the remaining two (RLVE at $\alpha{=}0.02$) are already
whole-simplex under the original rule and the sweep selects no cap.
The $\tau^*=1$-by-cap cells sit in the
\emph{trivial-validity} regime: the capped profile satisfies
$\max_K g_S(K)\le\alpha$ \emph{and} $\mathrm{base}_S\le\alpha$ by
construction, so the certificate survives \emph{any} bounded prior
shift. This is not ``infinite robustness for free'': the bill is paid
by the realized base-flip level $\mathrm{base}_S$ (e.g.\
$0.0054\ll\alpha{=}0.05$ at OMR $m{=}4000$, cap $0.02$) and by the
extra votes, both reported per cell. The pitfall cell of (f) is
repaired exactly: RLVE $m{=}187$, $\alpha{=}0.05$ goes from
$\tau^*=0.235$ to $1.0$ at cap $0.07$ with $+0.5\%$ votes; the coarse
OMR shard~1 $m{=}125$ fits all exceed half their full-fit radius at
every level (e.g.\ $\alpha{=}0.01$: $0.0095\to1.0$). Two negative
results delimit the mechanism. First, \emph{support truncation fails}:
zeroing the low-mass atoms of the prior and renormalizing changes the
induced certificate not at all (bit-identical $g$-profiles, 0
differences in 6 probed cells $\times$ 5 truncation levels;
\texttt{support\_trunc\_repair\_r498\_result.json}) --- the spikes of
(f) live on near-zero-mass boundary atoms that the base term never
visits but the $\ell_1$ adversary targets, and removing them from the
prior does not touch the local likelihood geometry that induces them.
Second, \emph{deadline truncation fails}: capping each atom's stopping
time at $\kappa(K)$ is non-monotone in $\kappa$ (flip $0.014$ at
$\kappa{=}8$ rising to $0.357$ at $\kappa{=}7$ and $0.5$ at
$\kappa{=}5$ on a probed RLVE atom) and can certify \emph{less} than
the original rule (RLVE $m{=}187$, $\alpha{=}0.05$: $0.235\to0.133$;
\texttt{efficiency\_r498c\_result.json}) --- a hard deadline deletes
informative late states wholesale, whereas the certificate-order
subset deletes exactly the states that are expensive to certify.
Together with (f) this closes the loop: moving the prior does nothing,
moving the time axis hurts, moving the \emph{stopping-state set} in
certificate order repairs --- the rule channel is the lever, and the
effective control is a flip budget, not a data budget. The repair is
also not a degenerate retreat to stopping at the first informative
prefix: a fixed $k{=}3$ stopper on OMR shard~1 $m{=}125$ has base flip
$0.093$ ($9\times$ above $\alpha{=}0.01$, $\tau^*=0$) against the
repaired rule's $\mathrm{base}_S=0.0036$.
\emph{Universal budget.} The sweep also pins down a single
carrier- and $m$-independent repair budget
(\texttt{universal\_cap\_r500\_result.json}): at cap $0.01$ ---
the smallest level of the paper's grid --- \emph{every} one of the
$72$ cells reaches $\tau^*=1$, at $+1.0\%$ to $+22.3\%$ mean votes
(OMR; RLVE $\le+5.2\%$) and realized base flip $\le0.0038$. The
boundary is sharp within the scanned grid: at cap $0.015$ exactly
the $18$ cells with $\alpha{=}0.01$ ($6$ per carrier, every $m$)
still fall short of the whole simplex, while all $54$ cells with
$\alpha\ge0.02$ are already repaired. A budget at or below the
smallest level of interest therefore repairs every fit size and
every carrier we tested, and no smaller scanned budget suffices for
the strictest level --- the repair law is a property of the cap
relative to $\alpha$, not of $m$ or the carrier. A fine-scale pass
(\texttt{critical\_cap\_r503\_result.json}; caps at $5\times10^{-4}$
resolution plus bisection to ${\approx}6\times10^{-7}$ on the
full-radius prefix) sharpens this into an exact edge law. Because
domination gives $\max_K g_S(K)\le c$ pointwise, every cap
$c\le\alpha$ certifies the whole simplex mechanically, so the
effective critical cap $c^*=\sup\{c:\tau^*(c)=1\}$ satisfies
$c^*\ge\alpha$ in every cell; the supremum is attained strictly
above $\alpha$ whenever the realized staircase leaves
$\max_K g_S(K)\le\alpha$ beyond $c=\alpha$ --- which happens in all
$72$ cells. The margin above $\alpha$ is carrier-resolved: it stays
${\le}+0.8\%$ at $\alpha\ge0.02$ on OMR (one shard~1 cell excepted,
${+}2.8\%$) and reaches ${+}10.6\%$ at
$\alpha{=}0.01$ on OMR (fine $N{=}32$ certificate geometry). On RLVE
the margin is not just measured but \emph{derived}
(\texttt{edge\_law\_r504\_result.json}): the flip mass of stopping at
state $(k,x)$ on an atom is the hypergeometric reach probability
$\binom{K}{x}\binom{n-K}{k-x}/\binom{n}{k}$, a rational number that
does not depend on the fitted prior; the smallest certificate-greedy
achievable flip sum above $\alpha$ is thus computable from the
certificate table alone, and it equals the scan-measured edge wherever
the scan bracketed one (all $18$ such cells). On RLVE ($N{=}8$) this quantum
sits at $c^*{=}3/28$ at $\alpha{=}0.10$ ($15/14\cdot\alpha$), $1/14$
at $\alpha{=}0.05$ ($10/7\cdot\alpha$), and $1/70$ at
$\alpha{=}0.01$ ($10/7\cdot\alpha$), identical at every one of the six
fit sizes --- the edge is combinatorics of the binding atom, not of the
prior. At $\alpha{=}0.02$ the cell splits by a certificate straddle:
at the four fit sizes whose binding state $(4,1)$ has certificate
${\le}0.02$ the edge is $1/14$ ($25/7\cdot\alpha$, beyond the scanned
band, which had reported only the censored bound $c^*{\ge}2\alpha$),
while at $m\in\{93,750\}$ that state's certificate crosses
$\alpha{=}0.02$ under the coarse-prefix prior, every achievable flip
sum stays ${\le}\alpha$, and the full radius survives \emph{every}
budget ($\tau^*{=}1$ certified at cap $0.5$, $\max_K g_S{=}1/56$). On
OMR ($N{=}32$) no such $O(\alpha)$ gap exists: the per-atom
closed-form edge upper-bounds the budget-constrained scan edge
(equality at $11$ of $48$ cells) and stays within ${+}10.6\%$ of
$\alpha$ in every cell --- the quantization of the repair edge is a
small-$N$ phenomenon. A tightness audit of the bound
(\texttt{edge\_tightness\_r505\_result.json}) separates the $48$ OMR
cells into two regimes by the sign of the certified slack one step below
the closed edge: in $11$ cells the realized $\max_K g_S$ at cap
$c^*_{\mathrm{closed}}{-}\epsilon$ is still ${\le}\alpha$ (the
binding atom's crossing state has not yet entered), so the scan edge and
the closed edge coincide up to the scan's bracket resolution --- the
closed form is tight; in the remaining $37$ cells the realized $\max_K
g_S$ at $c^*_{\mathrm{closed}}{-}\epsilon$ already exceeds $\alpha$
by $2.9\times10^{-5}$ to $3.7\times10^{-3}$, so the
budget-constrained greedy must drop the crossing prefix and
$c^*_{\mathrm{scan}}<c^*_{\mathrm{closed}}$ strictly. The two regimes
are disjoint in sign (nearest slacks $-2.43\times10^{-5}$ and
$+2.91\times10^{-5}$, an inner margin ratio of $1.20\times$ ---
correcting the ${\ge}2.4\times$ printed in v5.19--v5.20), so tightness
is decided by a sign, not a fitted threshold. (This audit also corrected
the equality count reported above in v5.18 from $12$ to $11$: the
twelfth cell, shard~1 $m{=}2000$ $\alpha{=}0.10$, differs by
$8.2\times10^{-5}$ --- two orders of magnitude above the scan's
rounding noise --- and had been absorbed by the verifier's $10^{-4}$
tolerance.) A discriminating audit at sub-artifact resolution
(\texttt{discriminant\_r507\_result.json}) head-to-heads the slack-sign
classifier against the two falsified first-draft conditions (recorded
in \texttt{edge\_tightness\_r505\_result.json}) on all $48$ cells: the
sign rule is exact
($11$~TP~/~$37$~TN, no errors, invariant to the probe offset over
$[5\times10^{-8},10^{-6}]$); the unique-binding-atom biconditional errs
on $22$ cells ($19$~FP~$+3$~FN); and the best fixed margin threshold
achieves only the always-strict constant ($11$ errors). The audit also
maps the tolerance window itself: the $11$-cell tight count is stable on
$[5\times10^{-7},2\times10^{-6}]$, degenerates to $3$ at
$2.5\times10^{-7}$ (the scan edge's six-decimal storage spreads exact
equalities to $\pm4\times10^{-7}$), and would absorb the genuine twelfth
cell only above $8.22\times10^{-5}$ --- the usable discriminating band
is $[5\times10^{-7},8.22\times10^{-5})$, floored by the artifact's own
rounding resolution. Under $\pm5\times10^{-7}$ scan perturbation
(worst-case rounding) the sign rule and the scan verdict agree on all
$48$ cells; at exactly one bisection step ($\pm6\times10^{-7}$) a single
boundary cell flips per side, and no strict cell is ever absorbed. The universal $c\le\min\alpha$ rule is thus the
carrier-independent safe budget, and any budget inside the derived
margin above $\alpha$ is free extra stopping mass, not a risk.
\emph{One geometry} (\texttt{discrete\_geometry\_r506\_result.json}).
All three non-monotone phenomena of this section are one object, the
discrete stopping-state set: $\tau^*(\text{cap})$ is \emph{not} a
monotone function ($323$ of $792$ adjacent-cap pairs on the coarse
$12$-cap grid violate monotonicity; the r501 working note misprinted
the pair count), because both scalarizations it reads jump with the
certificate-ordered prefix. Yet its full-radius crossing set is
always a \emph{prefix}: on the fine grid, every cap at or below $c^*$
certifies the whole simplex and no cap above $c^*$ does ($0$ holes
and $0$ overhangs across all $72$ cells). The reconciliation is
structural, not curve-fitting: shrinking the cap excludes a
certificate-ascending prefix, so both scalars $\max_K g_S$ and
$\mathrm{base}_S$ are monotone in the cap ($0$ and $0$ violations
respectively); once $\tau^*{=}1$ both lie at or below $\alpha$ and
must stay there, while inside the sub-unit region the two
scalarizations disagree and the staircase interior is free to wobble.
The deadline's flip sequence is the same object read along the time
axis ($0.014, 0.357, 0.214, 0.5, 0.243, 0.5$ as $\kappa$ shrinks on
the probed RLVE atom), and the closed-form edge is the same object
read exactly: the hypergeometric flip masses are the quantum, the
prefix structure gives the edge $c^*$, and the slack sign at one step
below the closed edge decides its tightness. Reading any of the three
as a smooth dial invites exactly the three failures documented above;
reading it as a discrete set gives the safe budget, the derived edge,
and the two operating ends (cheapest marginal repair at
${+}0.09\%$--${+}1.29\%$ votes; universal repair at
${+}1.05\%$--${+}16.9\%$) of one rule.

\begin{proposition}[Prefix law and two-scalar monotonicity of the
full-radius crossing set]
\label{prop:prefix}
Fix a cell $(\widehat H_m, \alpha)$ and let $g_{S(c)}$ be the realized
profile of the certificate-ordered state-subset rule at flip budget
$c$, with scalarizations $\overline g(c)=\max_K g_{S(c)}(K)$ and
$\mathrm{base}_S(c)=\sum_K \widehat H_m[K]\,g_{S(c)}(K)$, and write
$\tau(c)=\tau^*(\widehat H_m, g_{S(c)}; \alpha)$. Then:
\emph{(i)}~both scalarizations are nondecreasing in $c$;
\emph{(ii)}~for every $c$, $\tau(c)=1$ if and only if
$\mathrm{base}_S(c)\le\alpha$ and $\overline g(c)\le\alpha$;
\emph{(iii)}~the full-radius crossing set $\{c:\tau(c)=1\}$ is downward
closed --- a prefix --- while $\tau(c)$ itself is free to be
non-monotone inside the sub-unit region; and the crossing out of
$\tau{=}1$ as the budget grows is $\mathrm{base}_S$-driven: below
$\alpha$ both conditions of \emph{(ii)} already hold at $c=\alpha$ (by
domination $\overline g(\alpha)\le\alpha$) and persist for all
$c<\alpha$ by \emph{(i)}, so at $c>\alpha$ only the
$\mathrm{base}_S(c)\le\alpha$ condition can bind.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{(i)}~With nondecreasing budget, the greedy keep rule retains a
nested family of kept sets: every state kept at $c'$ is also kept at
$c>c'$ --- the certificate-sorted scan reaches that state with at least
as much remaining budget, and its own contribution is unchanged. Hence
$S(c')\subseteq S(c)$, and since each kept state's flip contribution is
nonnegative, both $\overline g$ and $\mathrm{base}_S$ are nondecreasing.
\emph{(ii)}~By Proposition~\ref{prop:tv} the two-sided greedy over
$B_R$ adds mass at the highest-$g$ atom and drains it from the
lowest-$g$ one, so for $R\in[0,1]$,
$V(R)=\mathrm{base}_S(c)+R\,(\overline g(c)-\mathrm{base}_S(c))$ ---
linear in $R$ with slope $\overline g(c)-\mathrm{base}_S(c)\ge0$, hence
$\sup_{R\le1}V(R)=V(1)=\overline g(c)$, and
$\sup_{R\le1}V(R)\le\alpha$ holds exactly under the two stated
conditions. \emph{(iii)}~By \emph{(ii)} the crossing set is an
intersection of two sublevel sets of functions nondecreasing in $c$
(\emph{i)}), and a sublevel set of a nondecreasing function is downward
closed; the intersection of two prefixes is a prefix. Inside the
sub-unit region neither condition of \emph{(ii)} is binding, so $\tau$
may wobble there (and does: $323$ of $792$ adjacent-cap comparisons on
the coarse grid); on the fine grid the crossing set has $0$ holes and
$0$ overhangs in all $72$ cells, and on the coarse grid the $323$
violations decompose into $253$ strictly sub-unit wobbles plus $70$
upward jumps \emph{at} the prefix edge, with zero holes
(\texttt{prefix\_prop\_r508\_result.json}).
\end{proof}

\emph{Unified mechanism.} One quantity --- the prior's $g$-profile ---
organizes every panel of the paper. (i) \emph{In-distribution}, replay count
reduction tracks the resolvable mixture structure: mass on extreme-$K$
problems stops at $k\approx5$ (OMR, $81\%$ reduction at $\alpha{=}0.05$);
with only $N{=}8$ draws and $74$--$87\%$ of prior mass on zero-$g$
atoms, three stoppable budgets remain and the count reduction interpolates to
$45$--$58\%$ (RLVE); at $M{=}2$ the single stoppable budget yields
$32$--$50\%$ and the certificate degenerates to never-stop exactly when
$\max_K g(K)\le\alpha$ (OpenR1 at $\alpha{=}0.02$: count reduction $0$, flip $0$
--- the certificate never over-stops). (ii) \emph{Under prior shift},
the same profile sets the critical radius: $\tau^*$ is increasing in the
zero-$g$ atom mass across carriers (Spearman $1.0$ on carrier means, 16
cells; het-excess variance is not the driver: RLVE $0.0999<$ OMR $0.129$
yet tolerates more), with the within-OMR negative trend
($-0.95$/$-0.89$) a staircase artifact (decomposition audit,
\texttt{spearman\_decomposition\_r486\_result.json}). (iii) \emph{Under
synthetic ordered drift}, adaptivity is itself the robustness channel in the
specified harness: BAYES-H re-reads the accumulating evidence each draw, so it
is the most robust stopper under the smooth E2/E3 mechanisms (valid to $\delta{=}0.20$
under E2 at $\alpha{=}0.10$ where FIXED-$k$ breaks at $0.15$), and a
margin $\gamma$ on the certificate buys flip control at a quantified replay-count
cost ($\gamma{=}0.03$ covers E2 $\delta\le0.20$: flip $0.0491$, count reduction
$71.8\%$ vs $79.7\%$ unmargined; $\gamma{=}0.025$ covers E3
$\delta\le0.15$: flip $0.0495$, count reduction $77.2\%$ vs $81.0\%$;
\texttt{margin\_repair\_r469\_result.json}). (iv) \emph{The failure
boundary is sharp on every axis}: premise-breaking E1 breaks all
certificates together by $\delta{=}0.10$--$0.15$; on shard~1 at
$\alpha{=}0.01$ the observed cross-shard mismatch $0.042$ exceeds
$\tau^*(0.01){=}0.024$ and TV alone does not certify the transfer; the
coarsest fit $m{=}125$ sits outside its ball (TV $0.175$) and genuinely
breaks at $\alpha\le0.02$ (Appendix~\ref{app:tau}(e)). Where the paper
claims a win, the margin is large and certified; where it does not, the
limiting quantity is named and measured.
