\title{Data Value Is a Path Functional of the Training Schedule:\\
Absorption--Erasure Asymmetry and a Contraction-Budget Law\\
for When Poisoned Data Can Be Removed for Free}

\author{Anonymous}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Data-valuation and machine-unlearning methods (influence functions, TRAK, datamodels,
retraining-based attribution) assign each training example a single scalar value, implicitly
assuming that value is a property of the example and the endpoint of training, not of the
\emph{path} taken to get there. We show this assumption fails in a controlled but standard
setting, and characterize a regime in which it can be analyzed exactly. In ridge-regularized
logistic regression with a WSD/cosine learning-rate (LR) schedule, we isolate a
\emph{conflicting block} $D$ (near-duplicate points with flipped labels). A fixed-quadratic
model proves that equal cumulative LR exposure $\Expo=\sum_t\eta_t$ gives the same first-order
contraction, with a finite-step discrepancy controlled by $\sum_t\eta_t^2$; update count is
invariant only after total exposure is held fixed or after convergence to the common attractor
(Theorem~\ref{thm:t1}). General strong convexity gives full-parameter return after $D$ is
dropped whenever enough clean-tail exposure remains, while a faster directional rate requires
an explicit invariant-subspace condition (Theorem~\ref{thm:t2}). Finally, an affine scalar
recursion gives an exact product law for the mean under conditionally zero-mean noise; the
familiar exponential contraction-budget curve is an upper bound and small-step approximation,
not a general SGD identity (Theorem~\ref{thm:t3}). Controlled logistic experiments test how far
these exact laws transfer beyond their formal domains. Absorption and erasure are therefore
\emph{non-isomorphic}: a block can be absorbed irreversibly early yet erased for free later.
Practically, our results imply the detection window for poisoned/mislabeled data can be
deferred to just before LR decay in the measured reversible regime, and give a quantitative
diagnostic for when endpoint attribution is biased. We verify the three formal laws in their
controlled domains and test their transfer on synthetic logistic problems; on real-image
benchmarks we test the budget trend and give a report-only R3
endpoint for the EQRA real-noise arm (MNIST; CIFAR-10N human label noise), while
nonconvex transfer of T1/T2 remains open.
\end{abstract}

Data valuation underlies data cleaning, poisoning defense, and training-data attribution.
The dominant tools---influence functions \citep{koh2017understanding}, TRAK
\citep{park2023trak}, datamodels \citep{ilyas2022datamodels}, forgetting/memorization scores
\citep{toneva2019forgetting}, and unlearning-by-retraining (SISA; \citealp{bourtoule2021sisa})---all assign each example a scalar that is treated as a property of the
example relative to the \emph{final} model. This is an \emph{endpoint} view: it assumes the
value of a datum does not depend on \emph{when} during training the datum was seen, nor on the
learning-rate (LR) schedule or update structure that followed.

Modern training, however, is dominated by warmup--stable--decay (WSD) LR schedules
\citep{hu2024minicpm}, in which a long high-LR phase is followed by a short low-LR decay. A
natural question arises: does the \emph{phase} in which a harmful datum appears determine how
much damage it causes, and---separately---how easily that damage can be removed? If so, data
value is a \emph{path functional} of the training trajectory, and endpoint attribution is
estimating the wrong object.

We study this in the cleanest setting that still exhibits the phenomenon: ridge-regularized
logistic regression trained by full-batch gradient descent (GD) or SGD under a WSD schedule,
with a \emph{conflicting block} $D$ of near-duplicate points whose labels are flipped. This
is the minimal object that creates a stable gradient-conflict field against a clean block $A$
(we show separable label noise alone does \emph{not} create the object---gradient conflict is
necessary, \S\ref{sec:object}). Figure~\ref{fig:main} previews the three laws.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Object and asymmetry (\S\ref{sec:object}).} We exhibit an
absorption--erasure \emph{non-isomorphism}: damage absorption is driven by high-LR exposure,
while the remaining clean tail erases it across the tested synthetic drop-time grid. A block can
be absorbed early yet later return close to the clean attractor when the T2 tail condition holds.
\item \textbf{Theory (\S\ref{sec:theory}).} Three formal results. T1: parameter error in a
fixed quadratic follows a product whose first-order term depends only on exposure $\Expo$, with an explicit
$\sum_t\eta_t^2$ remainder. T2: general strong convexity gives full-norm attractor return;
the measured faster damage-direction rate is a conditional corollary, not a consequence of a
Rayleigh quotient alone. T3: an affine scalar recursion gives an exact contraction product and
an exponential budget upper bound under conditionally zero-mean noise. The corresponding
logistic/optimizer trends are empirical transfer claims rather than theorem-level identities.
\item \textbf{Method (\S\ref{sec:method}).} In the convex/controlled-corruption regime
the theory yields an operational rule: poison detection/removal can be deferred to just
before LR decay without loss of recovery quality, and the budget law $\B$ predicts when
late injections survive; under real human-noise hysteresis the same law instead prescribes
early quarantine (deferred erasure is not lossless there).
\item \textbf{Evidence (\S\ref{sec:exp}).} Pre-registered synthetic tests verify the formal
laws in controlled cases and measure their approximation error in the non-quadratic logistic
cell; real-image arms (MNIST; CIFAR-10N human label noise) test the T3-inspired ordinal trend
and report the EQRA endpoint, with theorem-level nonconvex transfer open.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Setup and the Object of Study}
\label{sec:object}

\paragraph{Model.} Clean block $A$: $n$ samples $(x_i,y_i)\in\mathbb{R}^d\times\{0,1\}$,
$x_i=(2y_i-1)\mu+\varepsilon_i$, $\mu=(\mathrm{sep}/2)e_0$, $\varepsilon_i\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_d)$,
where $e_0$ is the first Euclidean unit vector.
Damage block $D$: near-duplicate label-flipped, $x_j^D=x_j^A+O(\varepsilon)$,
$y_j^D=1-y_j^A$. Model: $w\in\mathbb{R}^d$, logistic loss + ridge,
$L_A(w)=\frac1n\sum_i \ell(y_i,w\!\cdot\!x_i)+\frac{\lambda_r}{2}\|w\|^2$.
Optimizer: full-batch GD or SGD, WSD schedule $\eta_t\in[\eta_{\mathrm{lo}},\eta_{\mathrm{hi}}]$,
total exposure $\Expo=\sum_t\eta_t$.

\paragraph{Existence of the object (pre-registered).} Damage is defined as
$\mathrm{acc}(w^*_A)-\mathrm{acc}(w_T)$. Pilot 1 (separable label noise): damage $\approx-0.008\approx0$---separable noise is memorized without conflict, \emph{no object}. Pilot 2 (conflicting
near-dup flipped block): damage $=0.3395$---strong object. Pilot 10 (conflict-strength sweep):
object appears at label-conflict rate $\rho\in(0.1,0.25]$; below threshold there is no object
to erase (boundary, not erasure failure). \textbf{The mechanism requires gradient conflict, not
arbitrary noise.}

\section{Theory}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{T1: Exact exposure product for a fixed quadratic}
\label{sec:t1}

\begin{lemma}[T1, fixed-quadratic exposure product]
\label{thm:t1}
Let $Q(w)=\frac12(w-w^*)^\top H(w-w^*)$ with
$0\prec\mu I\preceq H\preceq LI$, and run
$w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta_t\nabla Q(w_t)$ with $0\leq\eta_tL<1$. Then
\begin{equation}
w_T-w^*=\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}(I-\eta_tH)(w_0-w^*).
\end{equation}
In an eigenvector of $H$ with eigenvalue $\lambda_j$, the exact contraction factor is
$P_j=\prod_t(1-\eta_t\lambda_j)$. Writing $\Expo=\sum_t\eta_t$ and
$q_j=\max_t\eta_t\lambda_j<1$ gives
\begin{equation}
0\leq-\log P_j-\lambda_j\Expo
\leq \frac{\lambda_j^2\sum_t\eta_t^2}{2(1-q_j)}.
\label{eq:t1log}
\end{equation}
Thus equal exposure gives the same first-order log contraction in this fixed quadratic, while
the finite-step schedule discrepancy is controlled by the squared-step budget. Replacing a
macro-step by $K$ substeps of size $\eta/K$ holds exposure fixed and preserves this first-order
limit; repeating $K$ steps of the original size instead multiplies exposure and is not a
finite-budget invariance. Independently, all stable schedules converge to the same $w^*$ as
their exposure diverges.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof skeleton]
The gradient is $H(w-w^*)$, so iterating the exact linear update gives the matrix product.
Diagonalizing $H$ gives $P_j$. For $x\in[0,q_j]$,
$0\leq-\log(1-x)-x\leq x^2/[2(1-q_j)]$; summing with
$x=\eta_t\lambda_j$ proves Equation~\ref{eq:t1log}. Full details and the precise scope of the
non-quadratic diagnostics are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Evidence.} Pilot 14 Arm A (pre-registered P1, passed): three schedule shapes at the
same $\Expo=105.6$ give per-seed damage identical up to one test point (max deviation
$2.5\times10^{-4}$, the $1/4000$ quantization of the test set; medians bitwise-identical);
$K=10$ rep matches $K=1$ GD to $0.0004$ despite $10\times$ effective exposure. Because this
comparison changes exposure, it is evidence of saturation at a common logistic-regression
attractor, not a finite-budget consequence of T1. Verify-T1 records the same empirical
robustness across the tested $200\times$ budget range ($\Expo\in[0.53,105.6]$).

\subsection{T2: Erasure is free via attractor regression}
\label{sec:t2}

\begin{theorem}[T2, strong-convexity tail return]
\label{thm:t2}
Let the clean objective $L_A$ be twice differentiable with
$\mu I\preceq\nabla^2L_A(w)\preceq LI$ on every segment between a tail iterate and its unique
minimizer $w_A^*$. If $0\leq\eta_t\leq1/L$, then after dropping $D$ at time $s$,
\begin{equation}
\|w_T-w_A^*\|_2\leq
\prod_{t=s}^{T-1}(1-\eta_t\mu)\|w_s-w_A^*\|_2
\leq e^{-\mu E_{\rm tail}}\|w_s-w_A^*\|_2,
\label{eq:t2full}
\end{equation}
where $E_{\rm tail}=\sum_{t=s}^{T-1}\eta_t$. Hence a uniform starting-radius bound
$\|w_s-w_A^*\|_2\leq R$ and
$\mu E_{\rm tail}\geq\log(R/\varepsilon)$ suffice for terminal error at most $\varepsilon$.
If, additionally, $\operatorname{span}(e_0)$ is invariant for every segment-average tail
Hessian, with eigenvalue at least $\lambda_0$, the same product argument gives the faster
coordinate bound
$|e_0^\top(w_T-w_A^*)|\leq e^{-\lambda_0E_{\rm tail}}
|e_0^\top(w_s-w_A^*)|$. Lemma~\ref{lem:c1} lower-bounds a Rayleigh quotient but does not by
itself establish this invariant-subspace premise.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Mechanism (diag5, decisive).} The drop arm converges \emph{exactly} to the window
attractor during the on-phase ($\|w(s)-w^{*(D)}_s\|=0.0000$), then the clean tail decays to
$w^*_A$: $s=30$ ($E_{\mathrm{tail}}=81.6$) shrinks the
residual to $0.019$; $s=120$ ($E_{\mathrm{tail}}=9.6$) to $0.43$. Both points shrink
\emph{faster} than the frozen directional heuristic $\lameff=0.037$ predicts ($0.049$ and
$0.701$ resp.):
the pointwise-implied rates are $0.048$ and $0.088$ (a $1.8\times$ spread). Equation~\ref{eq:t2full}
is the valid general certificate with rate $\mu$; the faster directional rates are measured
mechanism diagnostics unless the invariant-subspace premise is separately verified. Pilot 9 confirms erasure
is a \emph{genuine parameter-space return} (residual ratio $0.0685\approx0$, loss recovery $1.017$,
acc recovery $1.001$), not an accuracy-threshold artifact.

\paragraph{What the mechanism is \emph{not} (honest corrections).} It is not the ridge floor:
ridge-only contraction budget ($E_{\mathrm{tail}}\lambda_r=0.0096$) is far too small, yet erasure
occurs. It is not the logistic curvature shape $\lambda(\delta)=\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}\Lambda$
either---that shape is real (pilot11, Lemma~\ref{lem:c1}) but is a \emph{consequence} of the
strong-convexity attractor property, not an independent mechanism. T1 and T2 share a contraction
product in the fixed-quadratic special case; neither theorem is presented as a corollary of the
other in its full stated domain.

\begin{lemma}[C1, finite-sample directional curvature]
\label{lem:c1}
For $H_A(w)=\frac1n\sum_i q(z_i)x_ix_i^\top+\lambda_r I$,
$q(z)=\sigma(z)(1-\sigma(z))$, $z_i=w^\top x_i$, define
$M(w)=\max_i|w^\top x_i|$ and $s_0^2=n^{-1}\sum_i x_{i,0}^2$. Then deterministically
\begin{equation}
e_0^\top H_A(w)e_0\geq\lambda_r+q(M(w))s_0^2.
\label{eq:c1valid}
\end{equation}
Direct Hessian-vector diagnostics at 10 path points report positive data curvature; the measured
curvature \emph{along the damage direction} $e_0$ is
$\lambda_{e_0}(w^*)\approx0.037$, i.e.\ $37\times$ the ridge floor. This is a directional
statement: C1 lower-bounds $e_0^\top H e_0$ only; the full-spectrum minimum
$\lambda_{\min}(H)=\min_u u^\top Hu\le e_0^\top H e_0$ is still controlled by the ridge floor
($\lambda_{\min}\downarrow\lambda_r=0.001$ along saturated noise directions). Hence the
Rayleigh bound, not a certificate of coordinate decoupling. A $37\times$ faster coordinate
contraction therefore requires the additional invariant-subspace premise in T2; otherwise the
full-norm rate is governed by the slowest direction. The bound is structural, not tight.
\end{lemma}

\subsection{T3: Affine contraction budget and empirical first-cause/recency crossing}
\label{sec:t3}

\begin{theorem}[T3, affine scalar contraction budget]
\label{thm:t3}
Let $(u_t)$ be an integrable scalar process adapted to a filtration $(\mathcal F_t)$, fix
$\lambda>0$, and let $\xi_t$ be an integrable $\mathcal F_{t+1}$-measurable innovation satisfying
\begin{equation}
u_{t+1}=(1-\eta_t\lambda)u_t+\eta_t\xi_t,
\qquad \mathbb E[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_t]=0,
\qquad 0<\eta_t\lambda<1,
\label{eq:t3affine}
\end{equation}
with deterministic step sizes. Then
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E[u_T]=\mathbb E[u_s]\prod_{t=s}^{T-1}(1-\eta_t\lambda),
\qquad
|\mathbb E[u_T]|\leq |\mathbb E[u_s]|e^{-\lambda\B},
\quad \B=\sum_{t=s}^{T-1}\eta_t.
\label{eq:t3product}
\end{equation}
For constant $\eta$, the exact factor is $(1-\eta\lambda)^{N_{\rm win}}$; the exponential is
an upper bound and a small-step approximation. If normalized crossing is defined along this
one-dimensional affine state, its exact mean law is one minus the product. This theorem does
not assert that without-replacement logistic SGD obeys the same mean recursion; that transfer
is evaluated empirically below.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof skeleton]
Taking conditional expectations in Equation~\ref{eq:t3affine} removes $\xi_t$ by assumption.
Iterating gives the exact product in Equation~\ref{eq:t3product};
$1-x\leq e^{-x}$ gives the exponential bound. Nothing in this argument derives conditional
zero-mean affine noise from random reshuffling or from a nonlinear loss. Full details and the
empirical transfer boundary are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Evidence (pre-registered transfer tests).} Pilot 15 two-way switch: raising GD budget
$10\times$ flips first-cause ($0.057$) to recency ($0.922$); cutting SGD budget $10\times$ flips
recency ($1.004$) back to first-cause ($0.046$) with the noise structure unchanged; equal-budget
arms across optimizers match (ratio $0.82\in[0.3,3]$). $K=1$ is the unique robust first-cause
anchor across convex/non-convex cells. Pilot 14 P2: SGD batch-size scan ($K\approx4/9/18/37$)
gives monotone late-injection fraction $0.84/1.03/1.16/1.46$. Pilot 16: the budget switch
transfers to the non-convex MLP cell (cutting budget $10\times$ flips MLP from recency $0.94$ to
first-cause $0.027$).

These observations support the budget as an ordinal predictor in the tested cells; they do not
prove optimizer-invariant or noise-noncausal dynamics outside Equation~\ref{eq:t3affine}.
\paragraph{Honest quantitative boundary.} A parameter-free check uses
$\lampath=0.2023$ from a trapezoidal integral of the pilot11 curvature sweep (not fit on
pilot15). The scalar law gives robust \emph{ordinal/switch} predictions (monotone across
$\B_{\mathrm{crit}}\sim1/\lampath\approx4.9$; equal-$\B$ equal-coverage; $K{=}1$ anchor), but a
single scalar \emph{under-predicts} deep crossing ($\B=6$: observed $0.864$ vs predicted $0.703$,
residual $-0.161$): effective $\lambda$ grows with crossing depth, exactly the
$\lambda(\delta)=\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}\Lambda$ shape. Point-wise quantitative accuracy requires
a depth-dependent $\lambda(\B)$; the ordinal law is the robust claim.

\subsection{Unification: transient vs.\ equilibrium}
\label{sec:unify}
T1 gives an exact exposure product for a fixed quadratic and explains why stable schedules share
an equilibrium. T3 gives the corresponding affine scalar transient product. In the measured
logistic cell, incomplete convergence produces budget-controlled crossing and large budget
produces a shared saturated endpoint. This empirical seam motivates a path-aware diagnostic;
it does not make endpoint attribution a theorem-level special case of the quadratic model.

\section{Method: Deferred Detection and the Budget Rule}
\label{sec:method}

The theory yields a conditional operational principle. \textbf{Deferred detection:} under the
strong-convexity assumptions of T2, a uniform radius bound $R$, and enough clean-tail exposure
$\mu E_{\mathrm{tail}}\geq\log(R/\varepsilon)$, a removed block's parameter residual is at most
$\varepsilon$. The controlled experiments identify a regime in which removal just before decay
retains recovery quality. \textbf{Budget diagnostic:} the affine product suggests using the
measured per-step budget $\B=K\Ewin$ to diagnose incomplete crossing; it is not a universal
certificate for nonlinear SGD. The real human-noise arm is a documented boundary, handled by
conditional early quarantine (\S\ref{sec:real}). We validate the controlled-arm claims on
synthetic and real-image data (\S\ref{sec:exp}).

\paragraph{Early-quarantine salvage (real-noise regime).} Where free erasure fails (the
reversibility boundary of \S\ref{sec:real}), the theory instead prescribes acting \emph{before}
absorption: the measured budget trend says the high-LR window is where damage is absorbed, so a block that will cause
hysteresis should be isolated during that window. EQRA (early-quarantine $+$ re-admission)
monitors the gradient alignment $\cos(g_D,g_A)$ during the high-LR phase; when it falls below a
threshold the block is down-weighted/quarantined, and it is re-admitted at LR decay only if the
alignment recovers. This is the operational content of the budget law run in the
absorption-prevention direction. We pre-register its endpoints against never/always/fixed-drop
baselines at equal budget (\S\ref{sec:real}).

alternatives; this is an empirical mechanism comparison, not a universal causal theorem.

\subsection{Real-image arms (pilots 17/18/19)}
\label{sec:real}
Pilot 17: MNIST clean block $A$ + $D$ = real MNIST pixels with shuffled labels (100\% of $A$),
small CNN, WSD. Pilot 18: $D$ = 3\% of $A$ with controlled cyclic label corruption
($y\!\to\!(y{+}1)\bmod 10$)---realistic small-scale mislabeling. Pilot 19: CIFAR-10N
\emph{human} label noise---$A$ = aggregate$\,=\,$clean consensus subset, $D$ = real human
annotator-disagreement samples labeled by the aggregate human label (not synthetic corruption),
evaluated on the official CIFAR-10 test set. Pre-registered: R1 damage $\ge$ floor, R2 drop
recovery $\ge0.7$, R3 late-injection fraction (report-only for real-pixel arms, since the convex
T3 quantitative does not transfer directly).

\textbf{Results (controlled-corruption arms).} On real MNIST pixels, the object exists and
erasure is free for controlled corruption. Pilot~17 ($D$ = 100\% of $A$, shuffled labels):
damage $0.0696$ (R1 passes), drop@split recovery $1.014$ (R2 passes---drop@120 even edges above
never, the convex ``see-then-delete net gain'' reproduced on real pixels). Pilot~18 ($D$ = 3\%
of $A$, cyclic flip): damage $0.0109$ (R1 passes), recovery $1.069$ (R2 passes). R3 (late-injection
fraction $\ge0.9$) is not met on either arm ($0.894$ / $0.479$): the recency quantitative is
partial on real pixels, as pre-registered report-only.

\textbf{Results (real annotator-noise arm) and a reversibility boundary.}
On pilot~19 (CIFAR-10N real human disagreement), damage is $0.0427$ (R1 passes: the object
exists for real human noise), but drop recovery is only $0.240$---\emph{the pre-registered R2
fails}: real annotator disagreement on CIFAR-10N is \emph{not} freely erased by the low-LR tail,
in contrast to the near-complete recovery for controlled MNIST corruption. Late injection is
worse than always-on exposure (inject@late acc $0.6018$ vs.\ always $0.6165$; frac$_{\text{late}}
=-0.345<0$): a super-recency effect. Together these establish a \emph{reversibility boundary}:
whether a harmful block can be removed at no cost by the schedule tail depends on the data
modality, model, and corruption type. We diagnose the mechanism with per-epoch trajectories
(D-block loss/accuracy, gradient alignment $\cos(g_D,g_A)$, representation drift) and a
fixed-drop grid (diag20). We report this failure honestly rather than relabeling it a pass; the
pre-registered thresholds were fixed before the runs.

\paragraph{Mechanism of the boundary (diag20).} Three findings localize the boundary
(Table~\ref{tab:diag20}).
(i) \emph{No drop time erases.} A fixed-drop grid ($s\in\{90,\dots,210\}$) recovers at most
$0.406$ (at the earliest $s{=}90$) and decays anti-monotonically to $0.171$ ($s{=}210$): the
damage is absorbed during the high-LR warm-up and is never undone by the tail---there is no
detox deadline. (ii) \emph{Representation-level persistence.} After drop@120, the D-block
training loss does \emph{not} return toward the never baseline ($d_{\text{loss}}$ $0.061$,
$d_{\text{acc}}$ $0.985$ at $T$): the block is durably memorized in representation, so the
failure is not at the decision layer. (iii) \emph{Conflict is persistent and late-peaking, and
late steps are not cheap.} $\cos(g_D,g_A)$ turns negative around epoch $70$ and reaches $-0.95$
near the end (conflict spans the whole run, falsifying a purely early-window account); each
late exposure step produces a large representation drift, so the convex ``low-LR $=$ weak
absorption'' law does not transfer. Together: hysteresis $=$ durable representation memory $+$
persistent negative conflict $+$ costly late perturbation. This motivates an
\emph{early-quarantine} salvage that acts before absorption rather than after
(\S\ref{sec:method}).

\paragraph{Salvage: early-quarantine recovers reversibility (positive endpoint).}
The budget law says damage is absorbed in the high-LR window; we therefore test whether
quarantining the harmful block \emph{during} that window restores reversibility. Two detection
signals, identical quarantine/re-admission framework, pre-registered endpoints
(P1: $\ge$ fixed-drop$+0.02$; P2: $\ge$ never$-0.01$). (i) \emph{Block-level gradient
alignment} $\cos(g_D,g_A)<0$: \textbf{fails} (P1/P2 both fail; $-0.013$/$-0.007$ vs.\ fixed-drop,
below even always-on)---$\cos$ is positive during warm-up (diag20), so isolation triggers too
late. (ii) \emph{Per-sample loss} quarantine (highest-$10\%$ loss isolated during warm-up,
re-admitted at LR decay; loss-based detection
lineage~\citep{arazo2019unsupervised,jiang2018mentornet,han2018coteaching}): \textbf{passes}---
acc $0.6514$ vs.\ fixed-drop $0.6271$ ($+0.0243$, P1 passes; $5/6$ seeds positive) and within
$0.0083$ of the never oracle ($0.6597$, P2 passes); one seed even exceeds never ($0.6670$ vs.\
$0.6495$), echoing the convex see-then-delete net gain. The detection signal is the decisive
variable: per-sample loss identifies real human-noise examples during high-LR warm-up
\emph{before} absorption into the representation attractor, turning the hysteresis regime back
into a reversible one. We report both signal variants honestly (the negative $\cos$ result is
not hidden); the contribution is the theory-driven closure---the budget law predicts which
block gets absorbed and early loss-quarantine prevents absorption---not the detection heuristic
itself. CIFAR-10N human labels from \citet{wei2022cifar10n}.
\emph{Precision boundary (pre-registered P3, fails honestly):} on a \emph{harmless} clean block
(MNIST, $D$ labels unflipped), EQRA-loss with $q_{\text{frac}}{=}0.10$ costs $-0.026$ vs.\
always-on ($6/6$ seeds, $-2.5$ to $-2.8$pp): quarantining $10\%$ when the true harmful fraction
is $0$ removes clean hard examples, whose early loss is high by nature, and re-admission at LR
decay does not recover them (hard examples stay above the clean median). The loss signal is
therefore precise \emph{only when $q_{\text{frac}}$ matches the true noise rate}---matched on
CIFAR-10N ($10\%{\approx}10\%$: $+2.4$pp), mismatched here ($10\%$ vs.\ $0\%$: $-2.6$pp).
Routing rule: estimate the noise rate first with a calibrated signal (e.g.\ a mixture model on per-sample
loss~\citep{arazo2019unsupervised}); use EQRA-loss when it is non-negligible and estimable,
fixed-drop otherwise. Raw repeated-label disagreement is \emph{not} a sufficient estimator:
pre-registered as a gate, it miscalibrated on CIFAR-10N ($\hat d{=}0.396$) and failed its
smoke test (regret $0.0076>0.005$; Appendix~\ref{app:dgrouter}).

\subsection{Boundary and negative results (honest)}
(i) Separable label noise creates no object (pilot 1). (ii) Independent-class conflict
(Fashion-MNIST as $D$) creates negligible damage ($\sim10^{-3}$)---the model allocates separate
capacity; a valid conflicting block needs the \emph{same input manifold + opposed label function}
(pilot 17 first run, negative). (iii) The convex quantitative T3 does not transfer point-wise to
real pixels (R3 report-only). (iv) MLP+GD needs more cleanup steps than the convex cell
(drop@235 recovery $0.150$): erasure timescale is condition-dependent. (v) \emph{Reversibility
boundary (pilot 19):} real human annotator disagreement (CIFAR-10N) is not freely erased by the
schedule tail (recovery $\approx0.24<0.7$), and late injection is worse than always-on---so the
``deferred detection at no recovery cost'' rule holds for controlled MNIST-scale corruption but
must be conditioned on corruption type and model for real noise; the fixed-drop grid and
trajectory diagnosis localize the boundary.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Data valuation and attribution.} Influence
functions~\citep{koh2017understanding} approximate the effect of upweighting an example via a
single Newton step at the trained parameters; TRAK~\citep{park2023trak} and
datamodels~\citep{ilyas2022datamodels} scale retraining-based attribution through linearized
projections. All three assign each example one scalar tied to the \emph{endpoint} of training.
T1 supplies an exact fixed-quadratic path product, while our experiments show block value flipping
with budget $\B$; we do not cast endpoint methods as special cases. Example forgetting
\citep{toneva2019forgetting} tracks events, whereas controlled exposure interventions make $\B$
the stronger absorption/erasure predictor in our cells.

\paragraph{Machine unlearning.} SISA~\citep{bourtoule2021sisa} achieves exact unlearning by
sharded retraining. T2 instead gives a sufficient free-return condition:
$\|w_s-w_A^*\|\leq R$ and $\mu E_{\mathrm{tail}}\geq\ln(R/\varepsilon)$. Human-noise
hysteresis marks the boundary; EQRA-loss is the absorption-prevention fallback.

\paragraph{Learning-rate schedules and SGD noise.} The WSD schedule and its decay-phase loss
drop are documented at scale in MiniCPM~\citep{hu2024minicpm}, whose dynamics chapter leaves
the data-side question---\emph{which} examples drive the decay-phase improvement---open; our
exposure--timing decomposition lands in that gap. The formal strong-convexity certificate uses
the conservative tail factor $\exp(-\mu E_{\mathrm{tail}})$; faster directional rates are
empirical unless the invariant-subspace premise of T2 is verified. Bayesian accounts of SGD
noise~\citep{mandt2017sgd,smith2017bayesian} and the implicit-regularization view of
\citep{smith2021origin} treat gradient noise as the operative variable; our diag8/pilot15
find a fluctuation radius $30$--$50\times$ below attractor separation and observe budget switches
with the tested noise configuration held fixed. This does not prove that noise is generally
non-causal; it shows that the budget $\B$ is the stronger ordinal predictor in these controlled
comparisons.

\paragraph{Learning with noisy labels.} Per-sample loss as a noise detector is classical:
MentorNet~\citep{jiang2018mentornet}, Co-teaching~\citep{han2018coteaching}, and mixture-model
loss correction~\citep{arazo2019unsupervised} all exploit the small-loss tendency of clean
examples, and \citet{wei2022cifar10n} provide the real human-annotation noise we evaluate on.
Our salvage uses the same signal and inherits its known precision boundary (P3: clean hard
examples are over-quarantined when $q_{\text{frac}}$ exceeds the noise rate, $-2.6$pp). The
delta is not the detector but the \emph{timing theory} around it: the budget law predicts
\emph{when} a block will be absorbed (high-LR window), hence when detection must act---early
quarantine prevents absorption rather than attempting erasure after it, which diag20 shows is
impossible at any drop time in the hysteresis regime.

\paragraph{Positioning against DataProphet.} DataProphet~\citep{dataprophet2026} values examples
under a fixed training procedure; we study how schedule timing creates and destroys block value.
Appendix~\ref{app:neighbors} gives the 8+-paper first-hand audit.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
(a) \emph{Controlled vs.\ real label noise:} pilots 17/18 use controlled corruption (shuffled /
cyclic-flipped labels on real pixels); pilot 19 uses real human annotator disagreement
(CIFAR-10N). Real per-annotator NLP noise (ChaosNLI) was unreachable (HTTP 401)---residual
limitation. (b) \emph{Theory domain:} T1 is exact only for a fixed quadratic; T2's general
guarantee is the full-norm strong-convexity rate, and its faster directional rate additionally
requires a common invariant subspace; T3 is exact only for the stated affine scalar recursion.
Logistic, without-replacement SGD, cross-optimizer, and MLP behavior beyond those domains is
empirical (pilot 16), and nonconvex generalization is open. (c) \emph{Scale:} conflict
blocks are small-to-moderate; adversarial full-set corruption (pilot 17) is a stress test, not a
realistic scenario. (d) Point-wise T3 accuracy requires $\lambda(\B)$; the ordinal law is robust.
(e) \emph{Salvage scope and precision:} EQRA-loss is validated on CIFAR-10N human noise with the
quarantine fraction $q_{\text{frac}}{=}0.10$ set a priori to the (known) damage-block fraction;
deploying it requires estimating the noise rate (e.g.\ via a GMM on per-sample
loss~\citep{arazo2019unsupervised}). The pre-registered P3 precision check \emph{fails} on a
harmless clean block (MNIST, $-0.026$ vs.\ always-on, $6/6$ seeds): a $q_{\text{frac}}$ much
larger than the true noise rate over-quarantines harmless hard examples, and re-admission does
not recover them (\S\ref{sec:real}). EQRA-loss is therefore conditionally, not universally,
applicable: noise rate non-negligible and estimable---and the cheapest signal does not
discharge the estimation step: a pre-registered disagreement-only gate (repeated-label
disagreement, thresholded before training) overestimated the harmful fraction on CIFAR-10N
($\hat d{=}0.396$ vs.\ $q{=}0.10$) and lost $0.0076$ macro regret to fixed-drop
(Appendix~\ref{app:dgrouter}); a calibrated estimator (e.g.\ a per-sample-loss mixture)
remains necessary. The detection signal is a standard
loss-based heuristic---our contribution is the theory-driven closure (budget law $+$
absorption-prevention), not the detector itself.

\section{Conclusion}
Data value can be a path functional of the training schedule rather than an endpoint property.
Fixed-quadratic and affine models give exact contraction products; controlled logistic and image
experiments show an absorption--erasure asymmetry and a strong ordinal contraction-budget trend,
with explicit failures of a single pointwise rate. This yields a conditional boundary for
endpoint attribution and a deferred-detection rule in the verified reversible regime. Where free erasure fails
(real human-noise hysteresis), the same budget law instead prescribes acting before absorption:
per-sample-loss early-quarantine recovers
reversibility, beating post-hoc removal by $+2.4$pp at equal budget and closing to within $0.8$pp
of the never-oracle.

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}
\subsection{T1 (Theorem~\ref{thm:t1})}
\begin{proof}[Proof]
Write $e_t=w_t-w^*$. For the fixed quadratic in T1,
$\nabla Q(w_t)=He_t$, hence $e_{t+1}=(I-\eta_tH)e_t$. Iteration gives the exact matrix product.
Because $H$ is fixed and symmetric, it has an orthonormal eigenbasis; the $j$th coordinate is
multiplied by $P_j=\prod_t(1-\eta_t\lambda_j)$.

For $0\leq x\leq q<1$, the Taylor series gives
\begin{equation}
0\leq-\log(1-x)-x=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{x^k}{k}
\leq\frac{x^2}{2(1-q)}.
\end{equation}
Set $x=\eta_t\lambda_j$, sum over $t$, and use
$q=q_j=\max_t\eta_t\lambda_j$ to obtain Equation~\ref{eq:t1log}. Thus two schedules with the
same $\Expo$ have the same leading log contraction, but their exact finite-step products can
differ through $\sum_t\eta_t^2$ and higher moments.

If one macro-step of size $\eta$ is replaced by $K$ substeps of size $\eta/K$, then total
exposure is still $\eta$ and
$(1-\eta\lambda/K)^K\to e^{-\eta\lambda}$; Equation~\ref{eq:t1log} controls the discretization.
By contrast, $K$ repetitions of the original step have exposure $K\eta$ and exact factor
$(1-\eta\lambda)^K$, so there is no finite-budget $K$-invariance. All stable products tend to
zero as their cumulative exposure diverges, which explains common-attractor saturation without
equating the finite trajectories.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope and diagnostics (T1).} The theorem is deliberately fixed-quadratic. In the
logistic cell, diag9 reports that a frozen-Hessian calculation matches the observed cross-shape
spread to $10^{-5}$ in one starved regime, and the saturated endpoints match across all tested
schedules. Those measurements support approximation quality on the realized paths; they do not
upgrade the theorem to arbitrary strongly convex objectives with noncommuting path Hessians.

\subsection{T2 (Theorem~\ref{thm:t2}) and Lemma~\ref{lem:c1}}
\paragraph{Assumptions.} The full-norm theorem requires only the stated segment-wise Hessian
bounds and $\eta_t\leq1/L$. Ridge supplies the global lower bound $\mu=\lambda_r$ in the
logistic cell. The faster coordinate statement is separate: it additionally assumes that
$e_0$ is a common eigenvector of every segment-average tail Hessian. The measured
damage-direction ratio and curvature are diagnostics for this approximation, not substitutes
for the invariant-subspace assumption.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:c1}]
The exact directional curvature is
$e_0^\top H_A(w)e_0=\lambda_r+n^{-1}\sum_iq(z_i)x_{i,0}^2$.
Because $q$ is even and decreases with $|z|$, the definition
$M(w)=\max_i|z_i|$ gives $q(z_i)\geq q(M(w))$ for every sample. Factoring out this common
lower bound yields Equation~\ref{eq:c1valid}. This argument is deterministic and uses the
realized $M(w)$ and $s_0^2$; it needs neither a Gaussian norm event nor a claim that a mean
logit controls every sample. Direct Hessian-vector diagnostics at five displacement levels and
two seeds report positive data curvature on all 10 measured points. The empirical curvature
grows from $0.036$ to $0.51$ along the path; this supports a fast direction on those paths but
does not establish common-eigenvector dynamics.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:t2}]
Let $e_t=w_t-w_A^*$. The fundamental theorem of calculus gives
$\nabla L_A(w_t)-\nabla L_A(w_A^*)=\bar H_te_t$, where
$\bar H_t=\int_0^1\nabla^2L_A(w_A^*+\tau e_t)\,d\tau$ and
$\mu I\preceq\bar H_t\preceq LI$. Thus
$e_{t+1}=(I-\eta_t\bar H_t)e_t$. For $0\leq\eta_t\leq1/L$, every eigenvalue of this symmetric
operator lies in $[0,1-\eta_t\mu]$, so
$\|e_{t+1}\|_2\leq(1-\eta_t\mu)\|e_t\|_2$. Iteration and
$1-x\leq e^{-x}$ prove Equation~\ref{eq:t2full}.

If $\bar H_te_0=\lambda_te_0$ for every tail step, projecting the same recursion onto $e_0$
gives the exact scalar product and the conditional faster bound with
$\lambda_t\geq\lambda_0$. Without invariance, off-diagonal Hessian terms mix other coordinates
into $e_0$ and a Rayleigh lower bound alone cannot justify this projection.

Empirically, diag5 finds $\|w(s)-w_s^{*(D)}\|$ below displayed precision in the measured
on-phases, and pilot 9 measures return in parameter space (residual ratio $0.0685$), loss
($1.017$ recovery), and accuracy ($1.001$). These observations validate the realized return;
the theorem's sufficient condition still depends on the actual $E_{\rm tail}$ and radius $R$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Honest quantitative boundary (pre-registered failures disclosed).}
The \emph{linearized product law} of step (2) is not pointwise tight: pilot 11's two
pre-registered quantitative criteria both \textbf{failed} (V1: predicted/measured residual
ratio $0.325$ vs bound $0.5$; V3: $1.686$ vs bound $2$). Along the measured trajectory
($t=120{\to}230$, 110 steps), a path-averaged linearization \emph{over}-predicts the log
shrinkage by $\approx2.1\times$ (predicted $\sum\eta_t\lambda_t\approx1.67$ vs measured
$|\Delta\ln D|=0.785$), while a frozen near-field rate $\lam_{e_0}(w^*)=0.037$ under-predicts
it ($0.328$): the true contraction is sandwiched between the two, because the curvature decays
along the path as the iterate approaches $w^*_A$ ($\lambda_t: 0.512\to0.094$ --- self
\emph{de}celeration). This is the same depth dependence that T3's $\lampath$ averages over,
and it is why the fast directional rate is reported as an empirical path diagnostic unless the
common-invariant-direction premise is verified; the rigorous general rate is $\mu$. The
failed V1/V3 are themselves evidence for the sandwich picture and are recorded in the
pre-registration verdict file unchanged.

\paragraph{Open items (T2).} (i) Proving a faster multidimensional rate requires a common
invariant subspace or a quantified coupling remainder; numerically the damage direction
satisfies $u\approx e_0$ (projection $-0.92$), which is not itself a proof. (ii) Non-convex erasure timing is
conditional: MLP+SGD retains free erasure (recovery $\ge0.78$) but MLP+GD at a very late drop
does not (recovery $0.150$ with $>5$ cleanup steps needed) --- stated as a limitation.

\subsection{T3 (Theorem~\ref{thm:t3})}
\begin{proof}[Proof]
Put $a_t=1-\eta_t\lambda$. Because $u_t$ is $\mathcal F_t$-measurable,
$\eta_t$ is deterministic, and $\mathbb E[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_t]=0$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E[u_{t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t]
=a_tu_t+\eta_t\mathbb E[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_t]=a_tu_t.
\end{equation}
Taking total expectations gives $\mathbb E[u_{t+1}]=a_t\mathbb E[u_t]$.
Repeated substitution from $s$ to $T-1$ therefore yields the exact identity
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E[u_T]=\mathbb E[u_s]\prod_{t=s}^{T-1}a_t
=\mathbb E[u_s]\prod_{t=s}^{T-1}(1-\eta_t\lambda).
\end{equation}
Every factor is positive by assumption. Applying $1-x\leq e^{-x}$ factor by factor gives
\begin{equation}
\prod_{t=s}^{T-1}(1-\eta_t\lambda)
\leq \exp\!\left(-\lambda\sum_{t=s}^{T-1}\eta_t\right)
=e^{-\lambda\B},
\end{equation}
which proves Equation~\ref{eq:t3product}. For constant $\eta$, there are
$N_{\mathrm{win}}=T-s$ identical factors, so the exact factor is
$(1-\eta\lambda)^{N_{\mathrm{win}}}$. If $\mathbb E[u_s]\neq0$, the normalized mean
crossing $1-\mathbb E[u_T]/\mathbb E[u_s]$ is exactly one minus this product.

This proof is deliberately confined to Equation~\ref{eq:t3affine}. It neither proves that
without-replacement logistic SGD has conditionally zero-mean additive noise about a fixed
scalar contraction nor controls a nonlinear accuracy functional. The GD/SGD and accuracy
comparisons below test those two transfer steps empirically.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Parameter-free verification and its failure (the depth-dependent boundary).}
A self-fit scalar $\hat\lambda=0.262$ reproduces all four pilot-15 arms but has only one
effective degree of freedom (the two $\B=24$ arms saturate for any $\lambda\gtrsim0.2$), so we
do not count it as evidence. The meaningful check is parameter-free: $\lampath=0.2023$ from a
trapezoidal integral of the pilot-11 curvature sweep $\int_0^1\lambda(\delta)\,d\delta$, never
fit on pilot 15. It predicts the two $\B=2.4$ arms with residuals $-0.078$/$-0.085$ and the
$\B=24$ arms within $0.007$ (verify\_t3\_ou\_out.json, cond\_iv passed), and measured
equal-budget arms across optimizers match ($|0.4696-0.4629|=0.0067$ GD vs SGD at $\B=2.4$;
$0.0002$ at $\B=24$). Two additional intermediate-budget GD arms (pilot 17 budget-mid,
pre-registered to straddle $\B_{\mathrm{crit}}=1/\lampath\approx4.9$): coverage is strictly monotone
($\B=1.2: 0.249 < 2.4: 0.470 < 6.0: 0.864 < 24: 0.999$) --- the ordinal law holds across the
seam --- but the scalar prediction \emph{under}-predicts the deep-crossing arm
($\B=6$: observed $0.864$ vs predicted $0.703$, residual $-0.161$, marginally outside the
pre-registered $0.15$ band). This is a boundary of the scalar empirical approximation, not a
failure of the affine theorem: the measured effective curvature \emph{grows} with crossing depth
($\lambda(\delta)=\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}\Lambda$, pilot 11), so a shallow-path average
necessarily under-rates deep crossings, and the self-fit $\hat\lambda=0.262$ sits between the
near-field $0.037$ and the far-field saturation $0.51$, as a path average must. We therefore
claim the scalar law as a robust \emph{ordinal/switch} law (monotonicity across
$\B_{\mathrm{crit}}$, equal-budget equal-coverage, the $K{=}1$ anchor), with pointwise
accuracy requiring a depth-dependent $\lambda(\B)$ --- stated as the paper's quantitative
boundary, pre-registered verdicts unchanged.

\paragraph{Empirical seam with T1.} In the measured arms, crossing saturates near
$\lampath\B\gtrsim1$, with $\B_{\mathrm{crit}}\sim1/\lampath\approx4.9$, and the saturated
endpoints become insensitive to the tested schedules. T1 supplies an exact fixed-quadratic
product explanation for saturation; T3 supplies an exact affine scalar product. Their shared
product shape motivates the empirical seam but does not identify the nonlinear logistic
dynamics with either theorem. In particular, $\lameff\B\lesssim1$ is a diagnostic regime in
this experiment, not a universal failure certificate for endpoint attribution methods.

available signal --- repeated-label disagreement among the three CIFAR-10N human annotations,

can select per environment between EQRA-loss quarantine and the no-quarantine default. The

RED: macro accuracy $0.8197$ versus the pre-registered fixed-drop comparator $0.8274$

versus fixed-drop) while the harmless environment is routed correctly ($-0.0002$).
Repeated-label disagreement therefore conflates annotator ambiguity with harmful mislabeling
and overestimates the harmful fraction: disagreement alone did not safely route the current
method in this regime. We make no universal impossibility claim --- a calibrated estimator
(e.g.\ a per-sample-loss mixture) may still route correctly; the result closes only this
disagreement-only gate instance, not the routing question itself.
