014                                              A BSTRACT
015
016            We study how prompt length relates to the signed confidence–accuracy gap in
017            masked-diffusion decoding and whether that relationship is causal. On GSM8K
018            with LLaDA-8B-Instruct, the observational contrast across prompt-length vari-
019            ants is +0.2227 with a primary 95% cluster-bootstrap interval [0.169, 0.277]. An
020
               exact two-component decomposition shows that accuracy falls much faster than
               confidence. In a randomized within-item experiment with 400 paired problems,
021
               content-free redundant filler, and a fixed decode budget, the signed-gap contrast
022
               is −0.0075 [ −0.052, +0.039]. At the +37-token intervention dose, this inter-
023            val includes zero but excludes the +0.2227 observational magnitude (a dose-
024            local, not exactly dose-matched, comparison; §2). The preregistered verdict is
025            therefore NOT _ IDENTIFIED: content-free length is not detected to widen the gap
026            on this bed, and the observational contrast does not establish a length mecha-
027            nism. On MATH500, the post-repair v2 judge agrees with all 30 human anchors
028            and yields a designated v2 estimate of +0.2382 [0.1639, 0.3125]. Under the le-
029            nient judge, a 30-anchor pair-bias correction gives +0.1606 [ −0.059, +0.380],
030            an anchor-dependent design sensitivity interval rather than a repeat-sampling con-
031
               fidence interval. Because that interval contains zero, cross-bed transport remains
               POWER _ LIMITED (§A). Within GSM8K, the point estimate also decreases toward
032
               and through zero in harder outcome-defined accuracy regimes; this is a boundary
033
               on the association, not a mechanism claim.

036   1   I NTRODUCTION
037
038   Masked-diffusion language models decode by iteratively unmasking tokens under a scheduled tran-
039   sition. A decoder’s self-reported probability when it commits an output is a natural confidence signal
040   for downstream use, but its relationship to correctness may change with prompt length. Accuracy
041
      and confidence can move in opposite directions; their signed gap—confidence minus correctness—
      therefore distinguishes a change in confidence from a change in calibration-in-the-large. We measure
042
      the length-associated signed gap in a fixed GSM8K (Cobbe et al., 2021) / LLaDA-8B-Instruct (Nie
043
      et al., 2025) setting and use a within-item intervention to test whether the observed association is
044   attributable to content-free length. Our contributions are:
045
046         1. an exact two-component decomposition ∆SCE = ∆conf − ∆acc showing that the signed-
047            gap difference is dominated by the accuracy component (accuracy share 114.1%, confi-
048            dence share −14.1%);
049         2. a preregistered, within-item randomized intervention (N =400 paired problems, content-
050            free redundant filler) that detects no causal widening of the signed gap while bounding the
051            effect ([−0.052, +0.039]) tightly enough to exclude the +0.2227 observational magnitude;
052         3. an audited answer-matching judge and a human-anchor correction on a second, harder bed,
053            reported conservatively as POWER _ LIMITED with the judge-convention dependence made
               explicit;

            4. a traceable evidence package that links each headline number to a frozen artifact and a
055            one-command reproducer.

065   Definitions. A bed is one fixed task–model–decoder evaluation setting. The signed calibration
066   error is SCE = E[conf] − E[acc], the mean commit-step confidence minus mean correctness;
067   commit-step confidence is the decoder’s final self-reported probability at the commit step. The
068   reporting grade set is four-valued: SUPPORT means evidence reproducible under the predeclared
069   audit, POWER _ LIMITED means not established (here because of human-anchor volume, not GPU),
070   NOT _ IDENTIFIED is the preregistered verdict for a causal check whose within-item interval con-
071   tains zero, and NOT _ MEASURED means that no instrument is available. The V-gate is the count-
072   form threshold on false-reject and false-accept counts. Prompt-length tertiles are formed by the
073
      lower/upper natural-language token terciles of each bed’s prompt lengths: bed 1 (t0≤62, t2≥80
      tokens) and bed 2 (t0≤48, t2≥77 tokens). MDLS-101 and FULL-193 are respectively the mini-
074
      mum and full two-layer human-anchor fills (101 and 193 labels). Sealed or fragility-lineage marks
075
      bootstrap or agreement figures computed under an earlier pre-repair snapshot and retained for prove-
076   nance, not as a v2 measurement.

364   Decoder family. Masked text diffusion includes structured, simplified, ratio-estimation, and repa-
365   rameterized discrete formulations (Austin et al., 2021; Shi et al., 2024; Lou et al., 2024; Zheng
366   et al., 2024) and the LLaDA family (Nie et al., 2025; 2026). Variable-length or confidence-guided
367   decoders (Li et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026) and continuous language-flow alterna-
368
      tives (Hu et al., 2026) delimit our fixed single-bed setting: we make no decoder-generality claim.
369
      Calibration. Language-model confidence is a calibration-relevant observable (Kadavath et al.,
370
      2022). We study the signed confidence–accuracy gap SCE = E[conf]−E[acc] (a signed calibration-
371
      in-the-large quantity, distinct from the binned expected calibration error) and its length contrast with
372   per-problem cluster-bootstrap inference (Davison & Hinkley, 1997). For completeness Table 1 also
373   reports expected calibration error (ECE) with fixed 10 equal-width bins (Guo et al., 2017), adaptive
374   (equal-mass) ECE (Naeini et al., 2015), Brier score (Brier, 1950), and negative log-likelihood; each
375   is computed on the same per-problem confidence and correctness events as SCE. Because SCE is a
376   first-order mean gap, it is not a substitute for a full calibration curve, and we make no equivalence
377   claim to ECE or temperature scaling; we report them as companion descriptors with the definitions
      above.

383   Positioning. To our knowledge, prior work has not reported this exact two-component length con-
384   trast inside a masked-diffusion decoder with a problem-multiplicity-preserving bootstrap, an audited
385   judge, and a within-item causal check that separates the length association from a length mechanism.

      et al., 2012; Westreich & Greenland, 2013).

420   9   C ONCLUSION
421
422   On GSM8K, the supported result is an observational association across prompt-length variants,
423   decomposed into opposing accuracy and confidence components (Figure 2a). The within-item ran-
424   domized experiment (Table 1) finds that content-free length lowers confidence and accuracy together
425   but does not detect a widening of their signed gap (NOT _ IDENTIFIED). Its interval excludes the
426   observational magnitude without ruling out a small positive effect; the observational contrast there-
427   fore does not establish a length→gap mechanism. Cross-bed transport remains POWER _ LIMITED.
428   The reusable outputs are the predeclared within-item check and the judge-audit trail that separates
429   pre-repair diagnostics from the validated v2 measurement. The outcome-defined accuracy-regime
430
      analysis is a boundary condition, not a reproduction of a second-bed null. Remaining work in-
      cludes the FULL-193 human fill, an exogenous difficulty control, a second model, and a same-bed
