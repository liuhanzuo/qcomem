011                                               A BSTRACT
012
013            We document the withdrawal of a frozen repeated-query serving study whose
014
               evidence did not support its original cost–quality interpretation. The note con-
               tributes three reporting instruments: scoped statements for each surviving claim,
015
               a NOT_MEASURED register that is complete relative to an author-enumerated
016
               claim ledger (not an independent ground truth; Section 4), and frozen values with
017            per-row scope labels. Pattern-enumerated checks make violations within their de-
018            clared phrasing universe re-runnable, while leaving coverage outside that universe
019            explicitly open.
020            Static audit identified two application-level defects that had previously been read as
021            hardware boundaries: a thread-local grad-mode leak in the full-context worker (≈2
022            lines, fixed and re-measured) and an argument mismatch in a vendored KV-selector
023            (≈6–10 lines, statically diagnosed only). The guarded concurrent rerun (D1)
024            establishes feasibility at L=32k: at concurrency G=1 the peak is 29.31 GB, decode
025            is finite, and no out-of-memory failure occurs. At 16k, the measured pre-guard
026            peak is 139.2 GB, but its attribution to the missing guard remains inspection-grade
027
               because no guarded concurrent rerun was produced. The 16k rerun, KV-selector
               arm, and adapter-matched arm therefore remain NOT_MEASURED. The earlier
028
               “infeasible environment” interpretation is withdrawn, not replaced by a positive
029
               serving claim.

032   1    I NTRODUCTION AND S COPE
033
034   An archived study of multi-query KV-selective serving was withdrawn after its load-bearing claims
035   could not be supported: the worker’s GPU allocation was exhausted, the declared adapter was
036
      unrecoverable, and concurrent serving aborted. We report both the withdrawal and the reporting
      apparatus developed to delimit what remains measurable and citable.
037
038   Contributions. Figure 1 summarizes four outputs: (a) scope statements for each surviving claim
039   with re-runnable, pattern-scoped checks (Section 3); (b) a NOT_MEASURED register complete
040   only relative to the author-enumerated ledger (Section 4); (c) frozen values labelled by concurrency,
041   estimand class, and status (Section 5); and (d) a documented withdrawal in which a guarded rerun
042
      resolves the apparent memory ceiling at one length but not the other (Section 6). All measurements
      are scoped to one H20-3e GPU (141 GB (Yang et al., 2025)), Qwen3-8B in bfloat16, SDPA, and
043
      no adapter unless stated. Multi-GPU, multi-day, and 1M-token concurrent serving are outside scope.
044

046   a concurrent throughput or latency result, or verify adapter-arm identity; these omissions are recorded
047   as register rows and a binding consumption discipline (Section 7). The archived study used short
048
      internal identifiers; Table 1 defines the ones the argument needs and Table 8 (Appendix F) the full

187   Table 2 is the register, and its universe is declared, not rhetorical: the rows enumerate the load-
188   bearing claims of the archived claim ledger at closure — the C-numbered serving/quality claims,
189   D-numbered decisive-evidence debts, and S-numbered soundness-deficit classes. Completeness is
190   asserted relative to that enumerable, shipped ledger, not to every statement a reader might form, and is
191
      falsified by exhibiting a ledger item with no row. The limit is that the ledger is author-constructed: the
      register certifies no ledgered claim lacks a row, but cannot prove none was ever omitted (third-party
192
      recovery is the narrowing check we have not run); and the exact-set semantic check is clue-scoped,
193
      not a general guarantee — each registered ID appears and every canonical semantic clue in the text
194   after it belongs to that same ID (a swap that moves one ID’s meaning onto another’s row is caught
195   by the shipped mutation fixture), but a substitution borrowing no canonical clue, or one outside the
196   per-ID text window, is unseen. A new failing mutation falsifies one of these bounds, as the red-team
197   fixtures do.
198
      Exact-set completeness is machine-checked. Table 2 is generated from the checked-in single source
199
      tools/register_ledger_check.py (LEDGER + REGISTER + DECLARED_MERGES),

201   C-numbered claims retained, D-numbered decisive-evidence debts, S-numbered soundness-deficit
202   classes. The generator and the checker enforce the same invariants — every ledger ID has a row, no
203   row adds an unknown ID, and any many-to-one merge (e.g. S1/S3/S6 → one row) is declared and
204   counted — so deleting a row (e.g. omitting D4) or diverging a status canonical token makes the build
205   fail non-zero, rather than silently passing (Section 3.1).
