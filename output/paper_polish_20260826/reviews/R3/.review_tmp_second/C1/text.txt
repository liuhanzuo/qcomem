                                                  A BSTRACT
011
012            Multiple-choice scores are commonly interpreted relative to nominal chance, even
013            when an input-blind predictor can do better. We separate two reporting ques-
014            tions. For interface comparison, an arm-independent reference uses the construct’s
015            best constant input-blind predictor. For item-level information in one arm, an
016            arm-conditional permutation null preserves the arm’s prediction marginal within
017            option-count strata. The first reference is a one-directional reporting check, not
018            a certificate of construct validity; the second assigns every pure constant emitter
019
               exactly zero regardless of its collapse letter.
020            The best-constant floor must itself be calibrated under a null that the construct
021
               can realise. Restricting that null to each item’s legal letters leaves only MMLU
               and BoolQ with label floors that a balanced null could not plausibly produce;
022
               for the other letter constructs, the analysis motivates a different reference without
023
               establishing a floor–chance difference in magnitude. On MMLU-Pro, only 1/12
024            non-OLMo cells clears the best-constant floor under the matched paired-bootstrap
025            standard. Across five smaller benchmarks, 9/85 designated damaged cells clear
026            the floor on the interval standard, all of them healed OLMo-2 arms; achieved
027            precision explains why most per-benchmark tests remain inconclusive (Table 1).
028            Applying the arm-conditional null to 27 cells withdraws one above-floor capa-
029            bility label, removes both below-floor competence labels, and exposes a weak
030            intact-family anchor. Full-fp32 evaluation eliminates every bf16 exact tie without
031            recovering accuracy, so exact numerical ties do not explain the observed failure.
032            These results support a practical rule: state the construct-specific input-blind ref-
033            erence, its uncertainty, and its intended inferential role before interpreting arm
034
               differences.

      1   I NTRODUCTION
037
038   A recent multilingual evaluation reported that every tested model exceeded chance while none ex-
039   ceeded the benchmark’s majority-class baseline (Arčon et al., 2026). Printing both references ex-
040   posed the discrepancy. A systematic construct-validity review of 445 language-model benchmarks
041   provides 27 actionable checklist items but does not require reporting a null, chance level, or constant
042   predictor (Bean et al., 2025). We address this operational gap: before comparing model arms, report
043   whether the measured construct clears an appropriate input-blind reference. Figure 1 previews the
044
      two references needed for interface comparability and arm-specific item-level information.
045   For a letter-valued multiple-choice construct, the floor is the most accurate constant letter under the
046
      benchmark’s empirical gold marginal. For content likelihood, an analogous input-blind heuristic can
      exploit option length. Neither reference need equal nominal chance. An arm can therefore appear
047
      above chance while conveying no more usable signal through the measured interface than a constant
048
      predictor, especially after structural damage, when option-token selection bias (Zheng et al., 2024)
049   can dominate the argmax even if some content knowledge remains.
050
      We study null calibration as a measurement protocol, not a new multiple-choice interface or debias-
051   ing algorithm. Majority-class baselines for MCQA already exist (Balepur et al., 2024); letter-versus-
052   content formulations are established (Gu et al., 2025; Cho et al., 2026); constant outputs can game
053   automatic judges (Zheng et al., 2025). Our contribution is to make the construct-appropriate input-

112
113   Input-blind and constant baselines. Balepur et al. (2024) define a majority-class baseline for
114   letter MCQA and recommend stronger-than-chance references; their object is dataset cheatability
115   under a black-box generative setup, ours a per-arm gate for likelihood-scored constructs. The dis-
116   tinction is operational: their invalid-output analysis imputes either 0.0 or 0.25 on MMLU, while the
117   construct’s best-constant letter value is 0.2689. Zheng et al. (2025) use “null model” for crafted
118
      constant responses that adversarially exploit an LLM judge — an attack optimized against a judge,
      whereas ours is a reference derived from benchmark statistics, requiring no attacker. The metalin-
119
      guistic preprint motivating our opening reports the above-chance/below-majority pattern descrip-
120
      tively on one new benchmark, without turning it into a pre-comparison protocol (Arčon et al., 2026).
121
122   Selection bias and MC interfaces. Letter preference is not a new observation. Zheng et al. (2024)
123   identify selection and token bias and propose PriDe, which estimates an option-ID prior from per-
124   muted contents. PriDe fixes the predictor; null calibration fixes the reference line. Even after pre-
125   diction debiasing, reporting only against chance can leave the benchmark’s gold-marginal floor un-
126   reported. We therefore do not reject permutation controls on cost grounds — they are a useful and
127
      potentially inexpensive diagnostic, answering whether predictions should be corrected, while our
      arm-independent floor answers whether the resulting interface score is comparable across arms.
128
129
      OLMES standardizes both multiple-choice formulation (MCF) and cloze formulation (CF), then
      uses the better score per task and model (Gu et al., 2025); this is not a size-keyed rule. Its reference
130
      discussion is nevertheless framed around a random baseline rather than a measured label-marginal
131
      floor, so the max rule can select an interface without testing whether that interface clears an input-
132   blind reference. Our ARC-Easy result shows CF rescuing an at-floor letter read-out; MMLU shows
133   that an interface swap need not do so. Cho et al. (2026) independently study symbols, cloze, and
134   hybrid formats and propose a new question-sensitive score. We do not claim the interface contrast;
135   our method instead gates whichever construct is reported.
136
137
      Length-based scoring. Length-normalized likelihood can over-correct and its token-based form is
      tokenizer-dependent (Oostermeijer, 2026), so we claim no generic length sensitivity or tokenizer de-
138
      pendence of the scoring rule. We show the induced input-blind floor inherits these choices: its value
139
      changes with tie convention, character versus continuation-token length, and tokenizer. OLMES’s
140   observation that tokenizer dependence may be irrelevant when ranking options for one fixed model
141   and tokenizer is compatible with ours; our scope is cross-model comparison of the reference itself.
142
143   Nulls as interpretive controls. Our arm-conditional read-out v2 is a chance-corrected agreement
144   statistic: ∆perm equals the numerator of Cohen’s κ (Cohen, 1960) within option-count strata, and
145   the constant-emitter zero we rely on is that literature’s standard property, not a new result. We claim
146
      only the stratification by variable option count and the statistic’s use as a pre-comparison gate.
147   Control tasks and selectivity can reverse conclusions about which representation layer is informative
148
      (Hewitt & Liang, 2019) — the closest structural precedent, their null randomizing supervision for a
      probe while ours removes input dependence from an MC construct. Ding et al. (2021) similarly mo-
149
      tivate sensitivity and specificity tests for representation similarity. Finally, Bean et al. (2025) provide
150
      the construct-validity vocabulary and a broad benchmark checklist. We position null calibration as
151   the missing operational item, not as the invention of construct validity.

907
      Section 3.2 claims the within-stratum permutation and its use as a gate, and nothing else. This
908   appendix attributes the rest, result by result. Bennett’s S sets pe = 1/k for k response categories
909   (Bennett et al., 1954); Brennan & Prediger (1981) recommend exactly that free-marginal alternative
910   wherever κ’s marginal dependence misleads; formula scoring corrects for guessing as an explicit
911   function of the per-item option count (Frary, 1988); and Brenner & Kliebsch (1996) show that a
912   weighted κ drifts systematically with the number of categories — the very pathology our stratifi-
913   cation is designed to avoid. Pooling an agreement coefficient across heterogeneous item groups is
914   likewise established (Vries et al., 2008). We therefore claim none of the following: correcting for
915   the number of options, choosing pe as a methodological decision, or noticing that κ-family statistics
916
      depend on k. The works above assume a single global k; what we are not aware of in that literature
      is a null in which k varies item to item (n opt ∈ {3, . . . , 10}, eight strata here), with the permu-

      tation confined within strata so that a letter illegal for an item can never be credited to it. That is a
