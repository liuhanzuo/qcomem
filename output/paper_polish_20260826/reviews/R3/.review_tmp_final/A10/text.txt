011                                                    Abstract
012
013              We examine a negative control on one 135M-parameter masked diffusion language
014              model bed. Under a matched short budget, autoregressive initialization has a 2.26-
015              nat lower last-logged loss than one fixed random initialization, with the same di-
016              rection across four paired data-order seeds. Because the random initialization is a
017
                 single fixed draw, these repeats do not measure weight-draw variance. In a single-
                 prompt generation pilot, both arms are visibly degenerate. Teacher perplexity
018
                 ranks them opposite to the paired loss benchmark, whereas effective length, alpha-
019
                 betic yield, and emitted-token concentration distinguish their emissions. Neither
020              comparison establishes a quality ranking or metric capability because the pilot has
021              no independent quality anchor. MAUVE is not recorded under this configuration,
022              so we form no ratio and draw no MAUVE inference. The resulting engineering
023              deliverable is a companion record that places emission length and degeneration
024              beside per-token scores; it is not a new scalar metric, training method, or claim
025              beyond this bed.
026

028   1     Introduction
029
030   Masked diffusion language models generate by repeatedly denoising a canvas of masked tokens rather
031
      than extending a causal prefix one token at a time (Sahoo et al., 2024; Nie et al., 2025). This design
      exposes intermediate signals—entropy, maximum probability, mask state, and changes in the current
032
      argmax—that can guide adaptive decoding (Li et al., 2025). Generated-text PPL, MAUVE, and
033
      trajectory summaries describe different projections of an emission; none is an independent human-
034   quality anchor in our experiment. Wang et al. diagnose length, repetition, and punctuation as factors
035   that can distort PLM-based perplexity (Wang et al., 2022). We therefore ask only whether two known-
036   degenerate outputs in one small dLLM pilot differ on analogous emission fields that our initial report
037   did not place beside each score.
038   This paper turns a small training bed into a controlled negative-control pilot case study. The bed
039   starts from SmolLM2-135M (Ben Allal et al., 2025) and converts the same architecture to a masked
040   denoiser. One arm reuses autoregressive weights; the other uses a fixed random-initialization check-
041   point. Data, optimizer, schedule, token count, hardware, sampler, and evaluation are held constant.
042   This design does not estimate performance over the space of random initializations. It asks a nar-
043   rower question: at a fixed 30.0M-token budget, how do the two degenerate arms differ on directly
044   audited emission fields when the initialization is replaced by a constructive negative control?
045   We treat reproducibility and engineering diagnosis as distinct from multisite replication or a new
046   method. The paper makes three concrete, bed-scoped contributions:
047
048           1. A matched-budget calibration shows a same-signed loss separation over four paired data-
049              order seeds: the mean last-logged loss gap is 2.262, with 95% CI [2.220, 2.305]. The result is
050
                 explicitly conditional on the two fixed initialization checkpoints and short 458-step horizon.
051           2. In this single pilot, a report-level audit shows that the two degenerate arms differ in effective
052              emission length and emitted-token degeneration. These fields are not part of the reported
053              PPL value, while the attempted MAUVE value is not recorded; we place them beside each
                 PPL cell only for this setting, not as a general requirement or new metric.

088   2   Related Work and Evaluation Framing
089
090   Masked diffusion language modeling. Masked diffusion objectives can be written as mixtures of
091   masked-language modeling losses and paired with iterative unmasking samplers (Sahoo et al., 2024).
092   LLaDA demonstrated that the paradigm scales to an 8B parameter model trained from scratch (Nie
093   et al., 2025); more recent systems span reusable training frameworks (Zhou et al., 2026), continuous
094   embedded flows (Hu et al., 2026), and adaptive generation length (Li et al., 2025). AR-to-diffusion
095   adaptation has already been studied systematically in the DiffuLLaMA family (Gong et al., 2025);
096   PreDiff-LM studies pretrained masked diffusion with hybrid attention (Yao et al., 2026), and UNIFU-
097
      SION studies AR adaptation under a unified reverse-rate objective (Jiang et al., 2026). Accordingly,
      our matched initialization arms are a small reproducible case study, not a new adaptation method or
098
      training discovery.
099
100
      Metrics score different projections of emitted text. Generated-text PPL scores teacher likeli-
101
      hood; Wang et al. show that length, repetition, and punctuation can distort that score (Wang et al.,
102   2022). MAUVE instead compares generated and human distributions (Pillutla et al., 2021); surface
103   form also matters. Final-argmax share describes concentration of the generator’s decisions. Within
104   the 11-source local verification ledger used for this paper, we did not establish that these sources
105   report whether the emitted object is a short EOS stub or a long lexical loop. Our fields expose those
106   factors on one dLLM bed. A within-arm, within-prompt before/after check (Section 4.2) establishes
107   complementarity with teacher PPL only for that pair; discriminative evidence across initializations
      and prompts remains unresolved. Here PPL ranks the arms opposite to the matched four-seed loss

      3.1    Setup and configuration
136
137   We fix the training, initialization, generation, scoring, and signal-dump knobs in Table 2; Table 3
138   separately records the pre-registered multi-initialization control. This moves reconstructive values
139   out of prose while preserving their scope and provenance.

141   3.2    Matched arms and training budget
142
      Both arms use the converted SmolLM2-135M architecture (Ben Allal et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2026).
143   The AR-init arm retains pretrained weights; random-init is the fixed, audited from-scratch draw in
144   Table 2. Crucially, the four paired repeats vary data order and training stochasticity, not this weight
145   draw, so their loss variance cannot be transferred to independently drawn random initializations. To
146   test that gap we ran a pre-registered multi-init negative control (Table 3) on three independently
147   drawn random-initialization checkpoints, each trained under the same 30M-token / seed-42 data or-
148   dering as the two principal arms (Section 5). On the loss side the negative control reproduces: the
149   paired last-logged-loss gap (random − initR − AR − init) is positive on all three draws (bootstrap
150   95% CI lower bound > 0), so the sign-consistency of the principal result does not collapse across
151
      random initializations. The emission-side degeneration mode, however, is initialization-dependent:
      two draws degrade into a repeated-word lexical loop while the third is a shorter “gray” profile, so
152
      the emission narrative does not transfer across draws and is scoped to this effect (Section 5; evidence
153
      hash and verified fields are in paper/anonymous_artifact/data/reproducibility_
154   anchors.json).
155
      All token accounting, runtime, logging, objective, scheduler, hardware, and provenance fields are
156
      frozen in Table 2; in particular, “final” means the step-450 log inside the 458-step run, not a separate
157
      step-458 evaluation.

363   The probes give three complementary views. First, random-init has the more concentrated final-step
364
      argmax (Table 8). Second, the original claim that low-confidence layers cause drift is unsupported:

365
      bound and decoding-alignment checks establish only that the serialization is internally bounded and
366
      aligned; they do not recover the failed counts or a directional mechanism. Third, AR-init falls from

368   stays nearly flat (Figure 3). This stored-entropy contrast does not use the failed drift field, but the
369   nearly untrained control (458 steps, final loss 7.36) still cannot distinguish learned structure from the

401   5   Discussion
402
403   A negative control is a reporting unit test. The random-initialized arm is a unit test for describing
404   distinct degeneration modes, not a competitive baseline. For this single 458-step, single-checkpoint
405   pilot, the within-arm check in Section 4.2 supports only this companion bundle:
406                            (median teacher PPL, distributional similarity, E),                         (1)
407
      where E is effective length, alphabetic yield, and emitted-token concentration. The attempted
408
      MAUVE value and argmax diagnostic follow the same retention standard: raw records remain, but
409   neither supports inference. Slot (ii) is therefore unexercised; argmax is not an emission slot because
410   it decouples from sampled emissions here (Section 4.2, Figure 1). These fields are not a new metric,
411   and the multi-init control supports only the loss-side sign, not the PPL/loss ordering.
412
413   Loss calibration and generation quality answer different questions. The paired loss gap is sta-
414   tistically repeated; generation uses only seed-42 checkpoints and diagnoses emission coordinates,
415   not production quality. Neither implies good text, a MAUVE gap, or a quality ranking for AR-init.
416
      The signal layer is infrastructure, not a new decoder. The schema separates masked from com-
417
      mitted positions, current argmax from held token, and top-k ties. It diagnoses this control; adaptive-
418
      decoder accuracy or efficiency remains outside the evidence.
