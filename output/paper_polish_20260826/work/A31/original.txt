016                                              A BSTRACT
017
018            Position-decomposed diagnostics of diffusion large language models can confuse
019            sampler geometry with model behavior because an active-block recorder exposes
020            positions only through the current sampling window. In a recorder-scoped com-
021
               parison, we hold the model, questions, canvas, decoding rule, and total function
               evaluations ﬁxed while applying a composite intervention: block partition changes
022
               and the required per-block step allocation necessarily co-varies. The detected
023
               boundary period follows block length on this bed; separately registered non-nested
024            probes on a distinct canvas-96 bed do not reveal a recorder-observable ﬁxed-32
025            component. We contribute a reporting canon that separates what boundary-set
026            composition makes reachable before data from what measured magnitudes sup-
027            port after data. The amended decision branch, composite intervention, and unmea-
028            sured cross-model, cross-schedule, and cross-recorder scope preclude a model-
029            mechanism claim.
030

032   1   I NTRODUCTION
033
034
      Diffusion language models generate discrete sequences by iterative denoising (Austin et al., 2021;
      Zheng et al., 2024; Lou et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2024; Nie et al., 2025). As decoding schedules
035
      become variable or structured (Li et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026), a position-
036
      resolved diagnostic can inherit periodicity from the schedule that exposes its positions. Without an
037   intervention, such a readout cannot distinguish model-intrinsic state, sampler state, and what the
038   recorder makes observable.
039
040
      We examine identity-lock time on a blockwise-decoded LLaDA-style model (Nie et al., 2025; 2026).
      An apparent period of 32 motivates a matched composite intervention over the block partition and
041
      its required per-block step allocation: the model, 300 questions, canvas, total NFE, decoding rule,
042
      and temperature remain ﬁxed. The active-block recorder, however, logs only masked positions in
043   the currently active block. Its lock-time proﬁle is therefore deﬁned relative to the sampling window
044   (Figure 1).

319   3.2   S EPARATION AND STABILITY, NOT AN EFFECT- SIZE POWER CLAIM
320
321   The central estimand is discrete period identity. On the observed samples, positive min–max sepa-
322   ration is a deterministic property of the recorded proﬁles; the bootstrap then probes the stability of
323   period selection to resampling decodes. The selected period is stable under both the per-arm boot-
      strap and the joint paired bootstrap that re-applies one shared question-index draw to all ﬁve arms

      (Section 2); the detection rule ﬁres in 0.88–1.0 of resamples across arms, with the L32 development
325   control the weakest at Pr[sep(32)>0]=0.8805. A robustiﬁed non-extremal rank-sum separation still
326   selects L on all ﬁve arms. This does not establish an arbitrarily small effect or a universal period:
327   it establishes stable selection among the registered candidates on the stated bed, and the low-factor

      The nearest methods on the schedule axis make the distinction sharper. Fully parallel NAT (Gu
487   et al., 2018), semi-autoregressive grouped decoding (Wang et al., 2018), conﬁdence-ordered mask-
488   predict decoding (Ghazvininejad et al., 2019), LLaDA-style decoding (Nie et al., 2025; 2026), and
489   variable or reversible schedules (Li et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026) all change how
490   positions are generated or revisited. Table 9 asks the narrower question: does each work report
491   position-resolved commit/lock timing, and does it vary block length as a controlled intervention?
492
      Among these veriﬁed works, none reports position-resolved commit/lock timing across a controlled
493   block-length intervention. The recorder-scoped differential in Section 3.1 is therefore new on that
494   axis, while Wang et al.’s positionwise cross-entropy periodicity is the closest partial precedent.
495   These setup differences do not imply a ﬁeld-wide absence claim. Appendix Table 14 gives the
496   fuller row-wise comparison. Section 3.4 also differs from conﬁdence-dynamics work: it measures
497   association between the ﬁrst-step conﬁdence and later recorder lock ordering, rather than proposing
498   a new conﬁdence-based remasking policy.
499   A second, closer axis is the measurement literature that already warns that a readout instrument can
500   induce the signal it claims to observe. Sahoo et al. (2026) show that a linear probe’s near-perfect
501   accuracy on language-model hidden states can be driven entirely by task-format confounds (source
502   identity, option count, response length), vanishing after deconfounding — an instrumentation-bias
503   lesson we mirror in the recorder-window geometry. Jiang et al. (2020) argue that any prompt-based
504   measurement is at best a lower bound on stored knowledge because a sub-optimal prompt fails to
505   retrieve facts the model does hold; this is exactly the a-priori-reachability vs. measured-magnitude
506   split we make explicit. Belinkov & Glass (2019) survey how the choice of analysis method changes
507
      what is measured about a representation, and Feng et al. (2024) show a model can internally encode
      a faithful world state while decoding unfaithfully — the observational-equivalence gap between a
508
      recorded layer and the behavior that produced it. Conversely, Stolfo et al. (2023) attribute mech-
509
      anism from internal states via causal mediation; we deliberately do not — our contribution is the
510   reporting rule, not a mechanism claim. To make the boundary checkable, the appendix setup com-
511   parisons (Appendix A) state each prior object’s observation window, intervention, and measurement-
512   layer contrast. Table 15 makes the same-dimensionality question explicit: each closest measurement
513   work operates on a scale other than recorder-window period separation, so our addition is a differ-
514   ential separator and negative probes on that dimension, not a claim to out-measure those works on
515   their own scale.
516   More generally, construct validity requires matching an instrument to the quantity its coefﬁcient or
517   probe can support: conditioned coefﬁcients can answer a different target than the headline exposure
518   (Westreich & Greenland, 2013), and representation analyses inherit the choices and confounds of
519   their probes (Belinkov & Glass, 2019; Sahoo et al., 2026). Preregistration separates planned analysis
520   from outcome-contingent interpretation but does not itself validate a construct (Nosek et al., 2018).
521   Our two-layer rule adds a precise audit record rather than a general validity theory: before events,
522   freeze the set-algebraic outcomes the estimator makes reachable; after events, report the magni-
523   tude ordering without retroactively treating reachability as measured evidence. The post-treatment
524
      amendment remains visible, so the split does not convert it into an a-priori claim.
525   This work is therefore not a decoder or a mechanism attribution. Its contribution is the two-layer
526   reporting canon, exercised with a matched recorder-level differential. The min–max estimator sep-
527   arates the displayed divisors and multiples only under the stated strictly-L-supported condition; the
528   exploratory DFT control covers only the tested sinusoidal residual family.

530   6   C ONCLUSION
531
532   Under the tested active-block recorder, the detected identity-lock period follows block length. De-
533   velopment controls, matched arms, non-nested probes, bootstrap stability, and negative evidence
534   support that recorder-level statement. They do not distinguish model-intrinsic state, sampler state,
535   and recorder observability. The transferable result is the reporting rule: state what is reachable from
536   set composition before data, then separately state what the measured magnitudes show. Calibrate
537   position-decomposed measurements to the schedule that recorded them, and do not infer a mecha-
538   nism or cross-bed relation that the intervention did not measure.

      A.3   M EASUREMENT CANON
703
704   For blockwise decoding observed through an active-block recorder, a position-resolved analysis
705   should:
706
707         1. record block length, per-block steps, and generated-canvas coordinates;
708         2. calibrate within-block windows to the generating run rather than a ﬁxed model constant;
709         3. report reachability twice: a priori, what estimator algebra and boundary-set composition
710            imply; from events, what the measured magnitude ordering implies. In particular, B32 ⊂
711            B16 does not force sep(32) ≤ 0;
712
            4. perturb the schedule when attributing a position-resolved diagnostic;
713
714
            5. test content claims against a structure-preserving null and report its granularity; and
715         6. mark cross-bed relationships unmeasured until the corresponding intervention is run.
716
717
      This is a measurement canon, not a mechanism claim. The registered estimator returns P ∗ = L on
      the displayed strictly L-supported proﬁles: non-degenerate jumps occur on all L boundaries and no
718
      L-multiple’s exclusive-boundary residual outscores the L-boundary minimum. It is not a guarantee
719
      for arbitrary residual proﬁles, and identifying commit internals would require instrumentation of
720   sampler-state transfer.

      marginal. This supports genuine schedule-aligned structure rather than within-proﬁle jitter. A gen-
