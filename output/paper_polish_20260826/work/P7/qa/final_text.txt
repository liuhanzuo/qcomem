011                                                A BSTRACT
012
013
014         We test whether a quantization-free activation statistic can rank layer-wise post-training
015         quantization (PTQ) damage. On Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-1.7B, under 4-bit weight-only
016         HQQ and a templated calibration-to-deployment prompt shift, we correlate each probed
017
            block’s activation-tail turnover with the perplexity increase caused by quantizing that
            block alone. On corrected pad-masked labels, the point association is positive on Qwen3-
018
            4B (ρ ≈ 0.56) and weak with an uninformative interval on Qwen3-1.7B (ρ ≈ +0.12).
019
            The 4B direction survives the reported outcome-noise and prompt-level predictor analyses,
020         but interval conventions disagree about zero exclusion. Moreover, turnover is nearly
021         collinear with block depth; the masked partial correlations are wide and include zero, so
022         independent predictive value beyond depth is unresolved. The payload is bit-identical
023         across three physical hosts, establishing pipeline determinism rather than independent
024         replication (neff = 1 across runs). Because the analysis units are 11–12 dependent
025         layers from one checkpoint per size, only two of eight preregistered cells were run, and
026         the registered trigger gate is not resolved at the achieved label granularity, this is an
027         exploratory boundary study rather than a deployable trigger certificate.

      1    I NTRODUCTION
033
034
035
      Serving a post-training-quantized (PTQ) language model raises a question that calibration-time
036   accuracy does not answer: can current traffic reveal which quantized blocks will be most damaging?
037   PTQ reliability can change under calibration drift and input noise (Yuan et al., 2023), and aggregate
038   quality need not capture all quantization risks (Kadadekar, 2026). Prior fallback logic uses signals
039   such as numerical overflow (Zhang et al., 2025), while other methods remove calibration dependence
040   from the quantizer (Son et al., 2025). A candidate runtime trigger must be computable without
041   executing the quantized model and must rank blocks by their measured quantization damage.
042   We take one concrete candidate for such a signal and try to falsify it. For each block we measure the
043   activation-tail turnover: how much the ratio of the largest to the mean absolute activation entering that
044   block shifts when the input distribution moves from the calibration prompt family to the deployment
045   prompt family, both measured on the full-precision model. As the outcome we take the true per-block
046   damage: quantize exactly that one block to 4-bit HQQ (Badri & Shaji, 2023) and measure the relative
047   increase in deployment perplexity. If the candidate signal is a usable trigger, these two quantities
048   should agree in rank within a model. Figure 1 summarizes the measurement and the answer.
049   The two measured checkpoints give different point readings, but not an established population
050   difference: Qwen3-4B (Yang et al., 2025) has a positive corrected-label association (ρ ≈ 0.56),
051   whereas Qwen3-1.7B is weak and uncertain (ρ ≈ +0.12; Table 1, Fig. 2). On 4B, turnover is nearly
052   collinear with depth, and the corrected partial analysis cannot separate them (§3.2). Bit-identical
053   replay across three hosts makes the pipeline auditable, but not independently replicated. We therefore
      report the candidate signal and its failure modes, not a trigger threshold or deployment certificate.

117   2.3   C ARRIERS , DOMAIN PROXIES , PROBE RULE
118
119   Two carriers of the Qwen3 family (Yang et al., 2025) are measured, Qwen3-4B (36 blocks) and
120   Qwen3-1.7B (28 blocks), in bfloat16, on one NVIDIA H20-3e device per cell. The probe rule
121   is the fixed index list [0, 1, 4, 7, 12, 17, 22, 27, −4, −3, −2, −1] resolved against the block count and
122
      de-duplicated, giving 12 probed blocks on 4B and 11 on 1.7B, i.e. early, middle and the four deepest
      blocks; the largest probed index therefore pins the block count of each carrier. The two prompt families
123
      are 64 programmatically templated sentences each, one narrative and one arithmetic; they are labelled
124
      wikitext and gsm8k in the run configuration but are not those corpora, and Limitation C11 states
125   exactly what they are. Tail statistics use up to 192 tokens per prompt and perplexity up to 256 tokens,
126   with identical batching for the full-precision and the single-block-quantized models. Each cell writes
127   one row containing the probed layer set, per-block turnover, full-precision deployment perplexity and
128   per-block degradation; the decisive cells are 4B_hqq4_gsm8k and 1.7B_hqq4_gsm8k of one
129   registered run configuration, replayed under a second trial on a different physical host (run, trial and
130   host identifiers are withheld for double-blind review and given in the anonymous manifest).

132   2.4   E STIMATORS
133
134   Within a carrier we use the Spearman rank correlation over
                                                             √ probed blocks. Uncertainty is reported two
135
      ways on purpose — a Fisher-z interval with SE = 1/ n − 3 and a percentile bootstrap resampling
      blocks — because at these n the two families disagree about whether zero is excluded on 4B
136
      (Table 2); the same holds for a two-sided permutation p, which lands on either side of 0.05 depending
137
      on convention. We therefore condition no claim on a significance threshold, and instead report the
138   point estimate together with leave-one-block-out (LOO) resampling and the pre-registered drop-
139   block-27 re-check. Partial rank correlations are computed by residualizing ranks on ranks; they
140   agree with the closed-form first-order partial correlation to better than 5 × 10−4 on both carriers,
141   so the depth adjudication in §3.2 does not depend on that implementation choice. Cross-carrier
142   difference is summarized as ∆z under Fisher’s transform with a bootstrap interval. An independent
143   re-implementation of the estimators by a separate process (NumPy rank/tie handling, its own bootstrap
144   and a fixed-seed permutation null with B=20000) reproduces ρ on both carriers to the digits printed
145   here: the readout is estimator-independent, which is a different and weaker property than being
146
      data-independent.

192   3.2    W HETHER THE 4B RELATION IS A DEPTH PROXY IS NOT RESOLVED
193
      Turnover and block index (depth) are nearly collinear on 4B (ρ(τ, d) ≈ 0.91), which makes the
194
      raw positive relation plausibly confounded by depth. On the unmasked (pad-polluted) labels the
195
      partial contrast appeared to resolve it: turnover given depth collapsed toward zero while depth
196   given turnover stayed positive. On the corrected masked labels (the primary analysis) that contrast
197   does not reproduce: the masked partial is ρ(τ, δ | d) ≈ +0.27 for turnover given depth and
198   ρ(d, δ | τ ) ≈ +0.01 for depth given turnover (Table 1; layer-resampling bootstrap CIs, which are
199   wide, in Appendix Table 3). Because turnover and depth are so collinear and n=12, neither partial is
200   statistically resolved, and the two inferential endpoints point in different directions: on the AUROC
201   endpoint turnover exceeds the depth baseline (0.800 vs. 0.686; Limitation F), while on the masked
202   partials the direction is ambiguous. We therefore do not claim that turnover is a redacted depth proxy,
203   nor that it has independent predictive value beyond depth: with 11–12 layer-level points we can
204
      locate the confound, not the cause (Limitation C5). Re-weighting the probed sample to uniform
      depth coverage — removing the probe rule’s deliberate deep-decile dominance — lowers the reported
205
      association (0.559 → 0.401, R37_DEPTH_REWEIGHT_GATE3; Limitation E), and the masked
206
      leave-one-block-out range stays positive ([0.43, 0.72]; the minimum is the masked dominant-block
207   removal, block 35). The 4B headline is thus positive and robust to noise (L C12) but its attribution
208   between turnover and depth is genuinely unresolved. On 1.7B there is little to mediate: depth and
209   damage are essentially unrelated (Table 1), consistent with the weak masked ρ. A mechanistic reading
210   (weight-only damage concentrating where the residual stream has saturated) is directional only and
211   unsupported by the unresolved partials.

           analysis was performed for ρ(turnover, degrad) itself; the only power numbers available

385

           the WikiText-2/GSM8K corpora but 64 single-template sentences each (a record-filing and

         • (C12) Effects are of the same order as the outcome’s reproduction noise except at one
400        or two dominant blocks, and only 2 of the pre-registered 8 cells were run. The measured
401        within-cell reproduction spreads of near-zero blocks are milliscale on both carriers (five-arm
402        re-measures, R39_NOISE_RANK_SYNTHESIS; noise-floor rows in Table 2, Panel B), yet

           the 4B positive relation is robust to both noise sources, and the 1.7B cell is uninformative

         • (E) The deep-oversampling probe rule makes the depth structure partly a design artifact.
416
           The fixed probe rule [0, 1, 4, 7, 12, 17, 22, 27, −4, −3, −2, −1] deliberately places four of the
417
           twelve probed blocks in the deepest decile, so the depth-graded pile-up of §3.2 is in part
418        a consequence of where we chose to probe rather than of the underlying per-layer damage
419        distribution alone. We therefore read the depth result as a descriptive statement about this probe
420        design, not as evidence about the uninspected layers, and the inference we draw is limited
421        to the probed blocks. We quantify this probe bias on the measured sample by re-weighting
422        each probed block to uniform depth coverage (R37_DEPTH_REWEIGHT_GATE3): on 4B

424        correlations shrink to the same degree (Table 2, Panel A, re-weighted rows) — so the depth
425        proxy survives re-weighting but is partly inflated by the probe rule. Re-running the single-
426
           block-quantized outcome for the unprobed blocks (a depth-uniform or full-layer probe) remains
           the more expensive step beyond this study’s budget and is not performed here.

      5    R ELATED WORK
433
434
      Triggering and fallback. Runtime-safe quantization triggering has been approached from the nu-
435   merical side, with block-level fallback to higher precision when overflow is detected during INT8
436   training (Zhang et al., 2025), and from the quantizer side, by removing the calibration dependency that
437   creates drift sensitivity (Son et al., 2025); neither certifies a deployment-time predictor of per-layer
438   damage, which is what we test. We flag that Son et al. (2025) is a KV-cache method, whereas this
439   paper quantizes weights only; the comparison is by intent (runtime-safe, calibration-free) rather than
440   by modality. Reliability studies of PTQ motivate the test itself by showing how much worst-case
441   behaviour moves under calibration and input shift (Yuan et al., 2023), and by arguing that task-quality
442   metrics do not stand in for safety once weights are quantized (Kadadekar, 2026).
443   Per-layer sensitivity. A long line of work treats blocks as individually vulnerable, but through
444   different mechanisms. GPTQ minimizes second-order error compensation at a fixed width rather
445   than allocating width by sensitivity (Frantar et al., 2023); LLM.int8() splits outlier columns within
446   a single layer into high precision rather than ranking layers (Dettmers et al., 2022); AWQ applies
447   activation-aware per-channel scaling without a layer-level sensitivity score (Lin et al., 2024); and
448   mixed-precision schemes that do allocate width by a Hessian- or weight-based per-layer sensitivity
449
      score (Dong et al., 2019; Dettmers et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2024) — which are the natural competitors
      to both turnover and our depth baseline — are computed from an offline model/service analysis, not
450
      from a deployment-time shift of the input distribution.
451
452   Activation outliers. The activation-outlier story that motivated activation-aware quantization has
453   been shown to have a diminishing, calibration-set-dependent effect in modern LLMs (Paglieri et al.,
454   2024); this provides relevant context for our observation. A tail-derived, calibration-shift-based signal
455
      that is positive and depth-confounded on one checkpoint (Qwen3-4B) and weak-to-uninformative
      on a smaller sibling (Qwen3-1.7B, masked ρ = +0.12 with a wide interval) is consistent with a
456
      diminishing-outlier-effect account, but does not identify that mechanism. Our per-block framing
457
      asks the deployment-time question both literatures leave open — can the shift of a tail statistic rank
458   per-block damage? — and finds that where it appears to, the ranking is largely inherited from block
459   depth, so any candidate runtime signal must first beat the trivial structural baseline of block index.
460

      6    C ONCLUSION
463
464
      Activation-tail turnover has a positive corrected-label point association with single-block PTQ damage
      on Qwen3-4B (ρ ≈ 0.56) and a weak, uninformative association on Qwen3-1.7B (ρ ≈ +0.12). The
465
      cross-checkpoint difference is observed rather than established. On 4B, turnover is strongly entangled
466
      with block depth: reweighting and leave-one-block-out analyses retain a positive association, but
467   the masked partial correlations do not resolve independent value beyond depth. Bit-identical replay
468   makes the readout auditable, not independently replicated. These two checkpoints therefore support
469   an exploratory design lesson—candidate layer-level signals should be tested against simple structural
470   baselines and across domains—but not a universal fallback trigger. A second prompt domain and a
471   measured fallback policy are the direct next tests.
