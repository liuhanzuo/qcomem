      The Measured Cost of Cached-Depth Read-Out:
002   a Matched, Pre-registered Negative Result for Hidden-
003
004   State Cache in Long-Context Inference

008       Paper under double-blind review

011                                             Abstract
012
013             Long-context systems usually retain or reconstruct a key–value state at
014             every layer. We study a different trade-off: cache one layer activation
015             tensor per chunk at depth j, retrieve a fixed number of chunks, and recom-
016             pute only the upper layers. CoMem implements this write–retrieve–read
017             pipeline training-free, on a frozen backbone—which is also the scope con-
018
                dition of every result below. For fixed top-k the model read pack length is
                independent of document length and the GPU read-time working set stays
019
                approximately flat through 128k tokens, but the external hidden store, raw
020
                text, and BM25 index grow with the document, so we claim no system-level
021             constant memory.
022
                The contribution is the matched pre-registered protocol and a bounded
023             negative result: fixed-k retrieval preserves the short-read operating point,
024             while the depth-cache component is a measured quality-and-cost, not a
025             system win.
026             Our measurements separate two findings on Qwen3-8B. First, fixed-k re-
027             trieval provides the short-read working-set benefit. In a matched 128k mem-
028             ory check, moving the cache from j=0 to j=12 saves only about 0.3 GB
029             at a 6.7k-token read; this is the analytic, read-length-scoped quantity
030             (j/L)BKV read_len, not evidence that depth caching is generally memory-
031             free. Second, depth independently costs read-out fidelity. A five-arm mech-
032
                anism decomposition holds selection and decoding fixed and freezes both
                chunk-independent encoding and position relocation in its depth-only arm;
033
                that arm remains significantly worse than matched j=0 at both tested
034
                lengths, while the relocation-only contrast is null.
035
                The preregistered depth sweep agrees in direction: pointwise and with-
036
                out multiplicity correction, every tested j≥2 read loses to matched j=0 on
037             the same samples and no deeper point recovers it, with the load-bearing
038             j=12 comparison still significant under Bonferroni correction and the curve
039             not strictly monotone. The headline 4k magnitude is a selected-magnitude
040             negative result whose value depends on the registered read-out convention,
041             so the direction—not a convention-independent effect size—is the reusable
042             conclusion. Being training-free, the measurement bounds what a frozen
043             backbone gives up, not what a backbone trained for cache compressibility
044             could recover. We contribute the matched-retrieval measurement proto-
045
                col and its depth-cost curve; CoMem is the tested vehicle, not a claim of
                superiority over unimplemented prior systems.

048   1     Introduction
049
050   Long-context inference has two different scaling problems: the model’s active working set
051   grows with the sequence it rereads, and persistent state grows as more text is retained. KV
052   eviction bounds the former by discarding tokens (Xiao et al., 2024b; Zhang et al., 2023; Li
053   et al., 2024) and retrieval bounds it by selecting a short pack, but both usually retain raw
      tokens or full-layer KV. We ask whether the depth axis provides another operating point:

073   can a system retain one intermediate activation tensor per chunk and reconstruct only the
074   layers above it when that chunk is retrieved?
075   CoMem answers this question with a deliberately simple, training-free pipeline on a frozen
076   backbone (Figure 1). A write pass chunks a document, runs each chunk through layers
077   [0, j) with chunk-local positions, and stores the c × d residual activation hj together with
                                                                                    (1)     (k)
078   a raw-text BM25 index. A read selects fixed top-k chunks, forms [sink; hj , . . . , hj ; hqj ]
079   with fresh contiguous positions, and recomputes layers [j, L) with full cross-pack attention.
080   This is neither a fixed-size memory nor an exact transformation of the deployed input: the
081   persistent hidden/text store is O(context), and relocating chunk-local j > 0 states changes
082   the computation. The narrow benefit is that, for fixed k, model read length is independent
083   of document length; the measured GPU read-time peak stays approximately flat through
084   128k (see Limitations).
085   The central experimental question is consequently not whether retrieval helps, but what the
086   depth cache contributes given the same retrieval. We isolate it by comparing j = 12 with
087   j = 0 while holding the selected chunks, query, scorer, and decoder fixed; the j = 0 arm
088   re-forwards the same retrieved raw tokens through all layers and is the matched reference.
089   That comparison exposes a directional read-out cost rather than a free compression: at the
090   preregistered BABILong qa1 decision cell the matched j=12 read is worse than j=0 under
091   both the frozen reader and a sensitivity reader that removes model-generated option enu-
092   merations, with the magnitude reported as an interval rather than a point value (Table 2).
093
      We therefore treat depth caching as a quality–systems trade-off, not evidence that a mid-
      layer state is a suﬀicient statistic for general “understanding.” A five-arm decomposition
094
      then freezes chunk-independent encoding and position relocation separately and together:
095
      the pure-depth arm remains worse than j = 0, whereas relocation is null at the endpoint,
096   so the result is not explained away by either encoding factor.
097
098   Closest work and the gap we fill. The closest prior designs are Layer-Condensed KV (Wu &
099   Tu, 2024), which also caches a single shallow state on the layer axis but lacks top-k retrieval,
100   and HCache (Gao et al., 2025) and KV-Direct (Qasim et al., 2026), which restore or replace
101   state from intermediate activations without document retrieval (Section 2, Appendix A).
102   What this comparison set lacks is a matched, preregistered measurement that holds retrieval,
103   selector, scorer, and decoder fixed while varying only cache depth—and an honest report of
104   the resulting quality cost. This paper fills that gap; the answer, a consistent directional loss
105   under an overall downward trend in cached depth, is a measurement result, and CoMem is
106
      the vehicle on which the protocol runs rather than the contribution by itself. Our empirical
      positioning is internal—full context, matched j=0, cached j=12, and the author re-forward
107
      controls; a same-bed comparison to a prior system is not run and remains future work.

123   2   Related Work and Contribution Boundary
124
125
      Token-axis methods choose which token states to retain (Xiao et al., 2024b; Zhang et al.,
      2023; Li et al., 2024; Cai et al., 2024; Lin et al., 2026), and retrieval-and-reuse serving
126
      systems keep full-layer KV for retrieved chunks (Yao et al., 2024; Jin et al., 2024; Gim
127
      et al., 2024); both leave the layer axis untouched. The three nearest neighbours act on that
128   axis: Layer-Condensed KV shares one condensed KV computed at a shallow depth across the
129   upper layers (Wu & Tu, 2024), HCache restores evicted state from intermediate activations
130   without document retrieval (Gao et al., 2025), and KV-Direct replaces full-layer KV with a
131   residual-stream read once all retained tokens are reconstructed (Qasim et al., 2026). Each of
132   them either lets the read grow with retained tokens or omits top-k selection, and—decisively
133   for this paper—none measures cache-depth fidelity under a fixed retrieval, so the quality
134   price of reading a cached mid-depth state has never been isolated from the retrieval that
135   makes the read short. CoMem’s boundary is accordingly the combination of a one-depth per-
136
      token store, external BM25 top-k selection, and full upper-band recomputation: we claim
      neither the individual ingredients, nor numerical exactness for relocated j > 0 states, nor
137
      superiority over designs we do not implement, and the author’s full-depth re-forward control
138
      is labeled as ours rather than as an implementation of any published method. Appendix A
139   gives the per-family comparison, the mechanism-boundary table (Table 4), the position-
140   remap and external-memory neighbours (Xiao et al., 2024a; Sun et al., 2024; Mohtashami &
141   Jaggi, 2023; Tworkowski et al., 2023; Borgeaud et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2022; Bertsch et al.,
142   2023), and the independence statement for the 2026 preprints (Qasim et al., 2026; Gelberg
143   et al., 2026; Lin et al., 2026).

      3   CoMem
146
147
      Let a decoder-only transformer contain L layers of width d. We use half-open layer ranges:
148   F[0,j) contains the bottom j layers and F[j,L) the remaining layers. Documents are divided
149   into M disjoint chunks xm of c = 512 tokens. The mechanism is training-free: no parameter
150   of the frozen backbone is updated at any point.
151
152   Write.   For every chunk, CoMem computes
153                            (m)                               (m)
154                          hj      = F[0,j) (xm ; 0:c − 1),   hj     ∈ Rc×d ,                 (1)
155
      using chunk-local positions, and writes one layer’s per-token residual tensor per chunk to
156   an external store. It also retains the raw text needed to build a CPU BM25 index. The
157   hidden store therefore grows as O(M cd) and the text index as O(|x|): the layer axis buys a
158   persistent-storage rate reduction, not a constant-size memory (Appendix B).
159
160   Retrieve and read. Given query q, BM25 (Robertson & Zaragoza, 2009) ranks raw-text
161   chunks and returns Sk (q), the fixed top-k indices. CoMem sorts those indices back into

167   1 + kc + |q| and is independent of M when k and c are fixed. Thus model read FLOPs and

      than an end-to-end quantity are in Appendix B.
172
173
      What is and is not exact. On one contiguous sequence with unchanged positions,
174
      F[j,L) (F[0,j) (x)) = F (x) up to implementation precision; this is an algebraic split/rejoin
175   check. The deployed j > 0 path is different: chunks are encoded independently, selected,
176   relocated, and jointly read with a query. There is no exactness guarantee for that trans-
177   formation, so we measure its fidelity against the same-retrieval j = 0 arm, where selected
178   raw tokens are re-forwarded through the whole model; at j = L no model layers remain to
179   condition the stored representation on the query, so intermediate j is a quality–compute
180   knob. Two factors are confounded at every j > 0: (i) chunk-independent encoding (each
181   chunk is written with chunk-local positions, without cross-chunk attention during the write),
182   and (ii) position relocation (retrieved chunks are reassigned fresh contiguous positions at
183
      read). An identical-position split/rejoin is lossless by design and so isolates neither factor;
      disentangling (i) from (ii) requires the registered dual-step ablation of Section 5.2, reported
184
      separately from the headline depth curve.

      5.1   Direction estimand: the cached read-out is not exact
280
281   Under the same retrieved chunks, reading from cached j=12 states is less accurate than
282   re-forwarding those chunks from j=0. This qa1 intervention is the direct depth test, and
283   Table 2 is its single canonical view: the mnt160 context sweep, the second registered cap
284   (mnt512), and the pairing-free floor for the decision cell in one table. The load-bearing result
285   is the direction j=0>j=12 at both registered caps, and the two cap-specific estimates are
286   not a single interval. Every cell is computed on the content-hash-verified paired subset —
287   the two arms share all 100 contexts with no orphaned, duplicated, or question-mismatched
288   context (Appendix D) — and the recompute reproduces each published value exactly. The
289
      direction holds in every displayed convention cell and across the overlapping half-split audit,
      which is resampling stability, not independent replication; the full six-cell convention matrix,
290
      with the missing-excluded rows and the answer-anchored null cell, is preserved in Appendix
291
      Table 8.
292
293   Three caveats bound how much of this may be reused, and all three are kept rather than
294   resolved. First, generation-cap truncation does not explain the direction — the better
295
      readout is cut more often, so truncation would compress rather than create the gap — but the
      effect size is convention-dependent, both endpoints of the conditional range use the stripped
296
      reader, and a wider independent strip rule can reverse the sign, so no convention-independent
297
      magnitude is load-bearing. Second, the +27.0 pp 4k value is a selected magnitude rather
298   than a pre-specified effect size: the preregistered anchor moved from 1k to 4k before the
299   decision cell was inspected, and 4k is the largest significant gap among the valid displayed
300   2k/4k/8k cells. Third, the gap is not monotone in context length (Appendix Figure 4),
301   and the answer-anchored sensitivity preserves the sign on the same samples while remaining
302   underpowered at the longer cap. The supporting diagnostics (Appendix Tables 10 and 13)
303   sharpen but do not change the pinned direction and pair.
304
305   Depth-partition curve. A preregistered depth sweep provides a complete curve of cached
306   depth versus accuracy under the pinned reader (Appendix Table 5, Appendix Figure 2).
307
      Accuracy follows an overall downward trend in cached depth, and, pointwise without mul-
      tiplicity correction, every tested point from j=2 onward is a significant paired loss against
308
      j=0 on the same samples (we claim no strictly monotone curve and run no ordered-trend
309
      test). The 16k dense and full-depth re-forward (no retrieval) baselines show no significant
310   difference from j=0 at n=100; this does not establish equivalence but is consistent with a
311   depth-carried read-out cost within fixed retrieval. The system benefit is the fixed-k short
312   read, not depth: the same runs produce the quality and the wall-clock numbers on matched
313   operation points, and that run-level wall clock does not support a time saving from depth.
314   Per-depth memory peaks were not recorded (Appendix J); the separate E1 check measures
315   a 0.3 GB read-length-scoped depth difference at 128k but yields neither a memory curve nor
316   an end-to-end saving.

400   5.3   Serving estimand: what the fixed read costs and buys
401
402   Across the separate niah_single serving sweep, fixed-read CoMem retains perfect accuracy,
403   whereas the full-context baseline’s longest point was run outside this Qwen3 checkpoint’s
404
      enabled positional regime; that point is greyed and annotated in Appendix Figure 3 rather
      than treated as a comparison, so its 128k cliff is a positional-regime artifact, not evidence
405
      of systems-level full-attention collapse. At the longest context CoMem uses 3.9× less GPU
406
      memory and its registered per-query read remains approximately length-invariant, while full
407   attention stays feasible below the card’s 97.8 GB capacity rather than failing out of memory.
408   This memory contrast is read/prefill-only — decode rebuilds the upper pack at each token,
409   so it is not an end-to-end win, and a matched j=0 short read (Section 5.2) delivers the same
410   operating point. The recorded read/prefill endpoints are tabulated in Appendix Table 7
411   rather than plotted, because three endpoints are not a sweep; that table also prevents this
412   comparison from being misread as a depth-cache ablation, since full context versus CoMem
413   changes both selection and representation. Retrieval keeps the read in-window; it is a
414   whole-system retrieval comparison, not a depth saving.
415   The matched E1 check instead holds the selected 6.7k-token read fixed: its median-of-
416   three peaks are 18.2 GB at j = 0 and 17.9 GB at j = 12, reproduced in two trials (six of six

418   and matches the analytic read-time band (j/L)BKV read_len with j/L = 12/36. We call
419   this a read-length-scoped analytic null: weights and the 128k write transient dominate the
420   18.2 GB peak, so it is not general evidence that depth caching is memory-free. E1 is a

      memory endpoint and the direction estimand’s primary endpoint is qa1 accuracy, so neither

      6   Limitations
440
441
      The system is not constant-memory. The fixed-k pack is bounded, but GPU peak is
442
      not constant: it stays nearly flat through 128k and rises materially at the separate
443   one-million-token serving point (Appendix H). The activation/text/index stores grow as
444   O(context); index size and BM25 latency remain unmeasured.
445
446   Depth caching loses information needed by the deployed read. The same-retrieval j = 12
447   arm is worse than j = 0 on qa1 and the registered RULER/LongEval aggregates, and no
448   tested deeper point recovers j=0 accuracy (Appendix Table 5); identical-position split/rejoin
449   does not validate relocated chunks. The answer-anchored 512-cap result is underpowered
450   and the directional diagnostics are post-hoc, leaving a clean magnitude open. The quality
451   cost is also task-dependent: the fixed read retains near-perfect recall on needle-style single-
452
      supporting-fact probes, so that claim does not extend to multi-hop composition, which is
      not evaluable under the registered adjudicator (Appendix G). The 16k dense/re-forward
453
      controls are a non-rejection, not an equivalence test, so a retrieval-side deficit of a few pp is
454
      not excluded, though it remains far below the depth effect.
455
456
      The result is training-free and therefore bounded. Every number here comes from a frozen
457   backbone with no parameter update, so the measured depth cost is what a training-free
458   deployment pays. It is not a statement about what is achievable in principle: representations
459   can be trained to be more cache-compressible (Gelberg et al., 2026), and a backbone or
460   adapter optimized for reading relocated mid-depth states could recover part of the gap we
461   measure. We ran no such training, so we neither claim nor exclude that recovery.
462
463   Selection, uncertainty, and scorer resolution. The archived best-k sweeps lack a valida-
464   tion/test separation, so we report fixed k = 12; primary RULER and qa1 cells have n = 50
465   and n = 100 under one checkpoint and seed, and overlapping half-splits diagnose resampling
466
      stability, not independent replication. The pairing join is over the formatted model input
      rather than an independently stored 64-hex document digest, which was never retained, so
467
      the load-bearing cell also carries the pairing-free two-proportion floor. The oﬀicial scorer
468
      collapses roughly six in ten raw-generation differences onto one label, so the 0/100 end-
469   point agreement between arms (e.g. E and C) is indistinguishability at label resolution, not
470   identity or inertness of a deleted factor (Appendix F).
471
472   Mechanism-attribution boundary of the closest prior. NOT_MEASURED: the attributed-
473   mechanism support of the closest prior citation(s) is not verified item-by-item by a human
474   reader; the claim of mechanism attribution to those loci (Layer-Condensed KV, HCache,
475   KV-Direct) is therefore bounded and not asserted as human-verified.
476
477   Serving and data governance. Full attention at 128k uses 71.4 GB and remains feasible on
478   the H20, CoMem recomputes the upper band per decoded token so 0.68 s is read/prefill
479   rather than end-to-end latency, and the 0.3 GB E1 difference establishes no general depth-
480
      memory or end-to-end benefit. Raw text and activations require encrypted storage, access
      control, and source-bound deletion.

      A   Extended Related Work and Contribution Boundary
595
596   Token-axis KV reduction. StreamingLLM keeps recent tokens and attention sinks (Xiao
597   et al., 2024b); H2O keeps heavy hitters (Zhang et al., 2023); SnapKV predicts important
598   positions from an observation window (Li et al., 2024); and PyramidKV varies the token
599   budget across layers (Cai et al., 2024). These methods choose which token states to retain.
600   CoMem instead retains every token of a selected chunk at only one depth, so its storage
601   reduction is on the layer axis and does not imply that the external store is fixed-size. Layer-
602   Condensed KV compresses the cache on the same layer axis, sharing one condensed KV
603   computed at a shallow depth across the upper layers (Wu & Tu, 2024), but it keeps a state
604
      for every token and performs no top-k retrieval, so its read still grows with retained tokens.
      CoMem likewise stores at a single depth but only for selected chunks and recomputes the
605
      upper band with full cross-pack attention, bounding the read at fixed k, c. YOCO shares
606
      one global cache across a decoder–decoder architecture rather than storing retrieved per-
607   chunk residual tensors (Sun et al., 2024). These layer-axis designs motivate the comparison
608   but are not implemented in our harness; the paper’s empirical claims are therefore internal
609   matched controls, not a performance ranking over prior systems.
610
611   Retrieval and cached blocks. Landmark Attention and Focused Transformer learn mech-
612   anisms for selecting distant content (Mohtashami & Jaggi, 2023; Tworkowski et al., 2023).
613   Serving systems such as CacheBlend and RAGCache reuse KV for retrieved chunks and
614   selectively repair cross-chunk interactions (Yao et al., 2024; Jin et al., 2024). CoMem shares
615   their retrieval-first view but stores a layer-j activation rather than full-layer KV, then re-
616
      computes the complete upper band with full cross-pack attention. BM25 is an external,
      non-differentiable selector rather than a learned contribution. InfLLM keeps distant con-
617
      tent in memory blocks that use unit/separate positions and anchors the relevant blocks into
618
      the current segment, selecting blocks by attention (Xiao et al., 2024a). CoMem shares the
619   position-remap idea (fresh contiguous positions for retrieved layer-j activations) but selects
620   chunks by external BM25 rather than attention, stores a single-layer activation instead of
621   a unit-positioned block summary, and recomputes the complete upper band with full cross-
622   pack attention rather than attending to compressed anchors. RETRO injects retrieved
623   neighbors through deep cross-attention (Borgeaud et al., 2022); Memorizing Transformers
624   query an external kNN memory from a single layer (Wu et al., 2022); Prompt Cache reuses
625   modular prompt segments through a position-remapping schema (Gim et al., 2024); and
626   Unlimiformer scales retrieval into encoder–decoder cross-attention (Bertsch et al., 2023).
627
      These are closest on retrieval or reusable state, but none couples a one-depth per-token
      store with fixed top-k selection and full upper-band recomputation.
628
629
      Intermediate-state restoration. HCache restores evicted model state from intermediate ac-
630   tivations, primarily as a serving mechanism without document retrieval (Gao et al., 2025).
631   KV-Direct shows that a residual stream determines keys and values and can replace full-
632   layer KV when all retained tokens are reconstructed (Qasim et al., 2026). CoMem combines
633   partial-depth recomputation with top-k retrieval; unlike KV-Direct, it does not retain all
634   tokens in the active read, and unlike HCache it explicitly selects chunks. This combination is
635   the contribution boundary. We do not claim the individual ingredients, numerical exactness
636   for relocated j > 0 states, or a system-level constant-memory store. Training representations
637   for KV compressibility (Gelberg et al., 2026) and CompressKV’s semantic-retrieval-head-
638
      guided token eviction (Lin et al., 2026) are complementary token/representation-axis ap-
      proaches. We treat Qasim et al.’s KV-Direct, Gelberg et al.’s representation-training study,
639
      and Lin et al.’s CompressKV as 2026 arXiv preprints (Qasim et al., 2026; Gelberg et al.,
640
      2026; Lin et al., 2026); CoMem was developed independently, and its contribution boundary
641   does not rely on any one of them.

682   B   Method Details
683
684   Autoregressive generation boundary. The recorded 0.68 s quantity ends after read/prefill.
685   During generation the current reference path rebuilds the upper-band pack at each decoded
686   token; it does not reuse an upper-layer KV cache across decode steps. Generated tokens
687   are appended after the query at fresh contiguous positions and pass through the same F[j,L)
688   band. We therefore do not convert the recorded prefill endpoint into an end-to-end decode
689   or throughput claim.
690
691   Sink and query construction in the read pack. In Eq. (2) the sink is one BOS state com-
692   puted independently through F[0,j) and reused within the read; its lower-layer context is
693
      only that BOS token. The query is separately computed through F[0,j) with query-local
      positions and occupies the final fresh pack positions after the retrieved chunks.
694
