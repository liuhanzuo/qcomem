\section{Related Work and Contribution Boundary}
\label{sec:related}

Token-axis methods choose \emph{which} token states to
retain~\citep{streamingllm,h2o,snapkv,pyramidkv,compresskv}, and
retrieval-and-reuse serving systems keep full-layer KV for retrieved
chunks~\citep{cacheblend,ragcache,promptcache}; both leave the layer axis
untouched.  The three nearest neighbours act on that axis: Layer-Condensed KV
shares one condensed KV computed at a shallow depth across the upper
layers~\citep{lckv}, HCache restores evicted state from intermediate
activations without document retrieval~\citep{hcache}, and KV-Direct replaces
full-layer KV with a residual-stream read once all retained tokens are
reconstructed~\citep{kvdirect}.  Each of them either lets the read grow with
retained tokens or omits top-$k$ selection, and---decisively for this
paper---none measures cache-\emph{depth} fidelity under a fixed retrieval, so
the quality price of reading a cached mid-depth state has never been isolated
from the retrieval that makes the read short.  CoMem's boundary is accordingly
the \emph{combination} of a one-depth per-token store, external BM25 top-$k$
selection, and full upper-band recomputation: we claim neither the individual
ingredients, nor numerical exactness for relocated $j>0$ states, nor superiority
over designs we do not implement, and the author's full-depth re-forward control
is labeled as ours rather than as an implementation of any published method.
Appendix~\ref{app:related} gives the per-family comparison, the
mechanism-boundary table (Table~\ref{tab:method-boundary}), the position-remap
and external-memory neighbours~\citep{infllm,yoco,landmark,longllama,retro,memorizingtransformers,unlimiformer},
and the independence statement for the 2026
preprints~\citep{kvdirect,kvcat,compresskv}.
