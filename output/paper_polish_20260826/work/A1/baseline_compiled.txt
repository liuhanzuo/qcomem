009                                              Abstract
010
011             Answer parsers are part of an evaluation’s measurement instrument: a per-
012             missive fallback can rescue a correct value without the requested explicit for-
013             mat. We audit this effect on two frozen beds by separating format coverage,
014             accuracy conditional on explicit format, and their strict joint product. Both
015             beds use the free-form generative GSM8K split (no multiple-choice wrap-
016             per is defined in the frozen artifacts; option-count and chance-rate baselines
017             are therefore not applicable), so cross-bed levels are selected-population de-
018
                scriptions rather than capability rankings. On every Bed 1 arm, the format
                channel is 5.8–9.5 pp higher, far too small to explain the 35–67× rescue-
019
                ratio contrast. Order is rule-dependent: the raw point-estimate rule gives
020
                3/28, whereas the favorable paired gate—pre-registered in estimand word-
021             ing but operationalized post hoc—gives 0/23 and is scoped only to these
022             two beds. These Fstrip counts use, respectively, the raw identifiable-pair
023             and paired-definite calibers; the separately reported adversarial planned-
024             arm bounds use distinct gated and raw-plan denominators (Table 6), and
025             the denominators are not interchangeable. The registered pattern question
026             is not_identified from the shipped summaries: the data cannot decide, and
027             the primary conditional MDA80 is not identified.1

      1     Introduction
031
      Evaluation code is part of the measurement instrument. When a parser first looks for an
032
      explicit answer format and then rescues an otherwise unparseable response from its final
033
      position, the reported accuracy mixes at least two events: producing the requested format
034   and answering correctly within that format. A comparison is especially fragile when different
035   experimental beds invoke the rescue path at very different rates.
036
037
      We study this failure mode in two frozen beds evaluated by canonical v1.1. Table 1 makes
      the central contrast reconstructible: the rescue share differs by 35–67× between B1-32 and
038
      the three Bed 2 arms used in the primary level/pattern analyses. This prevents a raw
039
      canonical level from carrying the same operational meaning across beds, but does not imply
040   that every relation measured by the judge disappears.
041
042   This paper separates three questions that are often conflated:
043
             1. Level: can normalized accuracies be read as a cross-bed capability ordering?
044
045          2. Order: does removing fallback ever reverse an observed strict ordering between
046
                arms?
047          3. Pattern: does a registered canonical-tie/strict-separation label revert to a tie after
048             judge normalization?
049
      Our contribution is a measurement audit with pre-registered estimands and primary-arm
050
      assignment; the favorable order gate and final synthesis were pre-registered in their estimand

      2.1    Related work and closest-work map
135   The beds use GSM8K-derived items (Cobbe et al., 2021) and LLaDA (Nie et al., 2025);
136   dLLM generation and acceleration methods change decoding or compute rather than the
137   downstream answer parser (Zhou et al., 2026; Ye et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025; Wu & Kong,
138   2026; Wu et al., 2025b;a). Zheng et al. (Zheng et al., 2023) belongs to the separate LLM-
139   as-judge thread and is not evidence for deterministic extraction.
140
      The closest extraction work is Jo et al. (Jo et al., 2025), who sweep judges and prompts over
141   reasoning traces and show that where an answer is found can change reported performance.
142   xFinder (Yu et al., 2024) directly targets key-answer extraction and reports failures of regex-
143   based evaluation harnesses. Our narrower delta is empirical: we isolate a frozen parser-
144   fallback rescue path, decompose its reported score, and compare the xFinder regex tier on
145   the same two beds; we do not test xFinder’s learned tier or Answer Regeneration. In selective
146   prediction, coverage and conditional accuracy are standard paired quantities in the risk–
147   coverage trade-off (El-Yaniv & Wiener, 2010). Equation 3 is therefore a probability identity,
148   not a novelty claim; the contribution is its registered co-report and its use to audit this
149
      specific parser artifact. Table 3 is the positioning boundary: it anchors each component to
      verified Jo/xFinder/selective-prediction precedents and does not turn the regex-tier reparse
150
      into a same-bed comparison with xFinder’s learned tier or Answer Regeneration.
151
152   The inferential pieces are likewise standard. (i) Format-emission base rate. The format
153   share q = P (fmt) and Wilson score intervals (Wilson, 1927) summarize how often the judge
154   must fall back; exact binomial limits are from Clopper and Pearson (Clopper & Pearson,
155
      1934). (ii) Correctness given format. Conditional contrasts a = P (correct | fmt) are judged
      per-pair with McNemar’s paired test (McNemar, 1947), with item-paired bootstrap inter-
156
      vals; we explicitly decline a post-hoc equivalence claim, so the post-hoc 0.5 pp tolerance is
157
      reported without a formal TOST (Schuirmann, 1987), and family-wise control across the
158   tested definite pairs uses Dunn’s multiple-comparison correction (Dunn, 1961). Conditioning
159   on format emission—a post-generation property of the scored string—opens a collider-type,
160   selected-population contrast (Elwert & Winship, 2014), which is why J1 is scoped as de-
161   scriptive rather than a capability claim. (iii) Evaluation hygiene. Answer-extraction rules

245   3.2   Why conditioning does not create a common scale
246
247   Conditioning removes the fallback channel but changes the population. Format emission
248
      is a post-outcome (post-generation) behavior: the judge decides F and C from the same
      generated string. Selecting on F can therefore induce a selected-population (collider-type)
249
      contrast when response properties associated with correctness also affect explicit format-
250
      ting; it is not clean pre-treatment stratification. Across Bed 1 arms, q ∈ [0.0993, 0.2661]; on
251   Bed 2, q ∈ [0.9747, 0.9831]. Thus Bed 1’s conditional accuracy describes a formatted subset
252   comprising roughly 10–27% of its items, while Bed 2’s describes nearly all items. Unless
253   formatting is ignorable or common support is restored, comparing the two a values is a
254   selected-population contrast. It is useful as a sensitivity diagnostic but not as a pure capa-
255   bility scale. The beds also differ in prompt and task, adding an inseparable task confound.
256   Accordingly, all contrasts below are decoding-budget × task-format comparisons on a single
257   fixed model, not comparisons of model capability.
258

      5.1   J1: the level residual is real but not comparable
325
326   B1-32 has a = 0.5267, whereas B2-96 has a = 0.8543 (per-arm values in Table 13). Their
327   difference is −32.76 pp (SE 4.75 pp, 95% CI [−42.07, −23.45]), plotted on its own scale
328   in Figure 2(b) and audited in Table 9, so the interval excludes zero and the registered
329   5 pp neighborhood. Normalization therefore does not erase the numerical residual. It also
330   does not turn that residual into a capability ordering: the comparison conditions on q =
331   0.0993 versus q = 0.9831, hence on sharply different item populations, and task diﬀiculty is
332
      confounded.
333   The auxiliary joint quantity reinforces the channel diagnosis. Table 13 makes the anchor-free
334   log identity primary and reports additive attribution as an anchor-dependent range. Neither
335   is causal: the correctness component still contrasts non-common selected populations.
336   We therefore do not report a scale discrepancy: the additive share is not identified, its
337   identified range [59.11%, 95.87%] contains the unique anchor-free log share (82.58%), and
338   format-dominance survives on every identified line. The log share is anchor-free only when
339   the target is exactly multiplicative and factors are positive; pp-level additive shares are
340   reported as a range, the log share as the anchor-free complement—neither substitutes for
341   the other. Pre-registered falsification criterion: if, after this revision, the technical-soundness
342   and experiments-and-statistics seats of the next panel still name the Table 13 decomposition
343   rule as defective, this entire decomposition block demotes to an appendix sensitivity analysis.

345   5.2   The format channel is 5.8–9.5 pp higher on every Bed 1 arm—far too little to explain
346         a 35–67× rescue ratio
347
      The experiment’s headline states a share of canonically correct answers, not the fallback
348   channel’s quality. Table 5 separates those quantities from the same frozen records: on
349   B1-32, the fallback and formatted channels have accuracies 0.450 and 0.527, respectively.
350   This supports only a channel-precision reading on the shipped items: the strict-accuracy
351   columns are the parser-precision proxy in Table 5, reconciled by source in Table 12; parsing
352   is a deterministic regex/Judge-v1.1 priority match with no free-text LLM scoring. The
353   statement is bounded to Bed 1 free-form generative GSM8K, which has no multiple-choice
354   option set, so no option-count or chance-rate number is claimed. The fallback path is
355   therefore productive under this reference-match proxy, not established as human-labelled
356
      precision/recall. The table gives this comparison on all eight reproducible Bed 1 arms, using
      the identity strict + rescue = canonical (verified arm-by-arm from the per-arm accuracy
357
      table; every rescued row is unformatted and every format-correct row is strict-correct, a
358
      property we revisit in §6).
359
360   Both channels classify many frozen items correctly, but the format channel is consistently
361   5.8–9.5 pp higher (5/8 uncorrected-significant under S1 and 6/8 under S2; 3–4/8 Bonferroni
362
      across the two frozen strict-cell sources; Table 5). That modest directional gap is real
      evidence against channel equivalence, yet it is far too small to explain the 35–67× rescue-
363
      share contrast. The contrast is therefore prevalence-dominated: q is low on Bed 1 and near
364
      one on Bed 2. The gate makes that distinction visible by requiring q, a, and s together; the
365   strict ruler’s treatment of all 535 rescued B1-32 answers as wrong is not a clean capability
366   correction.

370   The estimand wording “definite” was pre-registered, but its criterion was operationalized
371   post hoc after inspecting the data, in the claim-preserving direction: a canonical relation
372   is definite only when exact two-sided McNemar has p < .05 and an item-paired bootstrap
373   interval excludes zero. This is a directionally favorable rule sensitivity: the gate was pre-
374
      registered in estimand wording and operationalized post hoc. Pairing itself is appropriate

375
      the gate was chosen. The full matrix uses B = 2000 paired resamples. J3 uses B = 5000
376
      for its single primary interval; each count was fixed in its analysis script before resampling,
377   and the unequal computational allocation carries no evidentiary ranking.
