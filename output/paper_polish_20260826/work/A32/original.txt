013
014
               We report a corrected read of the length-associated signed conﬁdence–accuracy
015
               gap in masked-diffusion decoding. On GSM8K with LLaDA-8B-Instruct, the
016
               signed conﬁdence–accuracy gap is +0.2227 with a primary 95% cluster-bootstrap
017            interval [0.169, 0.277], an observational contrast across prompt-length variants;
018            accuracy falls much faster than conﬁdence (exact two-component decomposition
019            with opposing-sign shares). A within-item randomized experiment (400 paired
020            problems, content-free redundant ﬁller, ﬁxed decode budget) yields a within-
021            item signed-gap contrast of −0.0075 [ −0.052, +0.039], and at the +37-token
022            intervention dose that interval excludes the +0.2227 observational magnitude
023            (dose-local, not dose-matched; §2); the preregistered verdict is NOT _ IDENTIFIED:
024            content-free length is not detected to widen the signed gap, so the read is
025
               an association across prompt-length variants, not a demonstrated mechanism
               for this bed. On MATH500, the post-repair v2 judge agrees with all 30 hu-
026
               man anchors, and the explicitly designated second-bed v2 estimate is +0.2382
027
               [0.1639, 0.3125]; the lenient judge with a 30-anchor pair-bias correction gives
028            +0.1606 [ −0.059, +0.380] as an anchor-dependent design sensitivity interval
029            (not a repeat-sampling conﬁdence interval) that contains zero, so the honest
030            reporting-layer conclusion remains POWER _ LIMITED: cross-bed transportability
031            is not established (§A). Within GSM8K, the point estimate also decays toward and
032            through zero as the accuracy regime hardens, a boundary on the headline associa-
033            tion rather than a mechanism claim.

036   1   I NTRODUCTION
037
038
      Masked-diffusion language models decode by iteratively unmasking tokens under a scheduled tran-
      sition, and the decoder’s per-output self-reported probability at the commit step is the natural conﬁ-
039
      dence signal a downstream system would consume. Yet little is known about when that conﬁdence
040
      is trustworthy as input length varies, and in particular whether an observed “conﬁdent and long”
041   regime coincides with a lengthening of the signed conﬁdence–accuracy gap. When a prompt is
042   lengthened, accuracy and conﬁdence can move in opposite directions, and the signed gap between
043   them—conﬁdence minus correctness—is the quantity that decides whether high conﬁdence is being
044   bought at the cost of lower accuracy. We study the length-dependence of this signed gap in one ﬁxed
045   GSM8K (Cobbe et al., 2021) / LLaDA-8B-Instruct (Nie et al., 2025) setting, and test whether an
046   observed length association is causal. Our contributions are:
047
048
            1. an exact two-component decomposition ∆SCE = ∆conf − ∆acc showing that a signed-
               gap difference is almost entirely an accuracy-side effect (accuracy share 114.1%, conﬁ-
049
               dence share −14.1%);
050
051         2. a preregistered, within-item randomized intervention (N =400 paired problems, content-
052
               free redundant ﬁller) that returns an honest null on causal widening of the signed gap while
               bounding the effect ([−0.052, +0.039]) tightly enough to exclude the +0.2227 observa-
053
               tional magnitude;

055            reported conservatively as POWER _ LIMITED with the judge-convention dependence made

367
      Decoder family. Masked text diffusion includes structured, simpliﬁed, ratio-estimation, and repa-
368   rameterized discrete formulations (Austin et al., 2021; Shi et al., 2024; Lou et al., 2024; Zheng
369   et al., 2024) and the LLaDA family (Nie et al., 2025; 2026). Variable-length or conﬁdence-guided
370   decoders (Li et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026) and continuous language-ﬂow alterna-
371   tives (Hu et al., 2026) delimit our ﬁxed single-bed setting: we make no decoder-generality claim.
372
373   Calibration. Language-model conﬁdence is a calibration-relevant observable (Kadavath et al.,
374   2022). We study the signed conﬁdence–accuracy gap SCE = E[conf]−E[acc] (a signed calibration-
375   in-the-large quantity, distinct from the binned expected calibration error) and its length contrast with
376   per-problem cluster-bootstrap inference (Davison & Hinkley, 1997). For completeness Table 1 also
377   reports expected calibration error (ECE) with ﬁxed 10 equal-width bins (Guo et al., 2017), adaptive
      (equal-mass) ECE (Naeini et al., 2015), Brier score (Brier, 1950), and negative log-likelihood; each


                                                         7
      Under review as a conference paper at ICLR 2026




378
      is computed on the same per-problem conﬁdence and correctness events as SCE. Because SCE is a
379   ﬁrst-order mean gap, it is not a substitute for a full calibration curve, and we make no equivalence
380   claim to ECE or temperature scaling; we report them as companion descriptors with the deﬁnitions
381   above.
382
383   Answer matching. Free-form mathematics ﬁnals require an answer-matching judge. Our judge-
384   and-human audit is anchored in GSM8K veriﬁer and matching methodology (Cobbe et al., 2021);
385   the audited per-stratum failure ledger, including the separation of pre- and post-repair snapshots, is
386   our reusable contribution.
387
388   Positioning. To our knowledge, prior work has not reported this exact two-component length con-
389   trast inside a masked-diffusion decoder with a problem-multiplicity-preserving bootstrap, an audited
390   judge, and a within-item causal check that separates the length association from a length mechanism.
391
      We report a boundary limited by the second bed, not a transportable law; our single-bed length
      claim is scoped to the within-item causal check on this bed and does not extend to nearest-neighbor
392
      (non-masked-diffusion) decoders, which remain an open same-bed contrast. Outcome-stratiﬁed con-
393
      trols additionally require positivity/common-support and coefﬁcient-interpretation caution (Petersen
394   et al., 2012; Westreich & Greenland, 2013).

397   8   L IMITATIONS OF THIS CALIBRATION READ
398          • Single-bed anchor. The uncorrected cross-bed read and its current human-anchor correc-
399            tion are reported in Table 6; the corrected read is POWER_LIMITED. Four-state grading
400            awaits the predeclared expanded two-layer human-label set, and two beds are not universal.
401            Neither “transportable-positive” nor “collapse” is citable until then.
402          • No single-bed control arm is included in the second bed, so we cannot fully separate
403            cross-task transportability from a second-bed main effect (difﬁculty reconciliation is not
404            clean; interpreting tier-stratiﬁed associations as the effect carries the usual coefﬁcient-
405            over-interpretation (Westreich & Greenland, 2013) and positivity/coverage (Petersen et al.,
406            2012) cautions).
407          • The within-bed outcome-stratiﬁed sensitivity shows a point-estimate decrease through zero
408            across nested accuracy subsets (Table 2, §4); the CIs retain signiﬁcance only at the all sub-
409            set under the primary cluster bootstrap. At the accuracy ﬂoor (acc=0) ∆acc ≡ 0 by con-
410            struction, so the equality ∆SCE = ∆conf there is mechanical; only the low-accuracy tier
411            can move ∆acc, and under the wide primary intervals neither the collapse nor its ﬂatness is
412            statistically pinned. We therefore report the collapse as an acc-regime-driven limitation and
413            boundary, not a mechanism claim — and we do not use control-arm CI non-signiﬁcance
414
               to dismiss the difﬁculty explanation, as interpretation in either direction is convention-
               sensitive.
415
416          • Content-free length only. The within-item manipulation adds semantically null redundant
417            ﬁller (validity gate: 0/200 drift), whereas the observational contrast varies natural prompt
418
               lengths that co-vary with content and difﬁculty. The intervention therefore identiﬁes the
               effect of content-free length on this bed, but does not exclude a length mechanism that acts
419
               through content-bearing or difﬁculty-changing length; a content-bearing-length arm is left
420
               as future work.
421
422
             • The conﬁdence component is reported sign-consistently only; its cross-bed magnitude dif-
               ference is summarized with Figure 2a and Table 2.

426
427   The supported single-bed result is an observational association across prompt-length variants; Fig-
428   ure 2a reports the signed gap and its opposing accuracy- and conﬁdence-side shares. A within-
429   item randomized experiment (Table 1) shows that adding content-free length lowers conﬁdence and
430   accuracy in tandem, while we do not detect a widening of the signed gap (preregistered verdict
      NOT _ IDENTIFIED ). Its interval excludes the observational magnitude but does not rule out a small
431
      positive effect, so we read the “widens with length” contrast as an association across prompt-length


                                                        8

432
      variants for content-free length, not as a demonstrated length→gap mechanism. Cross-bed trans-
433   port is not established and remains POWER _ LIMITED. The reusable contribution is the judge audit
434   and label-corruption repair trail that separates fragility-lineage diagnostics from the validated v2
