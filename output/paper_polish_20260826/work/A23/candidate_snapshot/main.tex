\begin{abstract}
Generative (masked-diffusion) language decoding (LLaDA-style) produces a \emph{single}
decode per prompt, and the decoder's internal confidence is a candidate signal for
\emph{risk-gated deployment} (Chow~1970-style selective prediction: abstain on the
highest-risk fraction to cover a target risk). We study whether, in block-wise denoising
decoding, revision-end confidence
(\texttt{commit}), first-exposure confidence (\texttt{first}), within-block mean
(\texttt{all}), early versus late windows, and intra-block versus cross-block aggregation
read the \emph{same stable construct} or \emph{measurement artifacts of different sources}.
On one frozen LLaDA-8B-Instruct $\times$ GSM8K bed ($n{=}974$ distinct problems, one
decode each, $k{=}1$ --- no best-of-$k$ candidate pool), zero-GPU reproducible
analysis finds intra-block discrimination statistically measurable (AUROC gain $+0.0144$
exceeds its full-width MDE80), yet \emph{at this bed's decision-readout power no intra-block
risk-gated selection increment is resolvable}: every registered intra-block
decision-readout anchor is \texttt{POWER\_LIMITED} under the full-width MDE80
(H2). This is an underpowered \emph{non-detection}, not a rejection: on this bed we cannot
rule out an intra-block selection increment smaller than the decision-anchor MDE80, and we
do \emph{not} claim in-block selection is unusable elsewhere. (R3 is therefore a bounded
power-bound negative, not the construct claim ``measurable $\neq$ usable''.) Four further
judgment-hygiene working instances bound that result: at the registered cov$=0.2$
anchor the three readouts agree in direction and are point-identical, while the $c\ge0.55$
low-selectivity band is power-limited rather than a measured agreement or null;
\texttt{commit} and \texttt{all} are distinct constructs but
their decision-validity gap is compatible with reliability/SNR; a desaturated readout
reproduces the early/late effect; and that effect is high-selectivity-specific,
\textbf{power-limited under the bed's stated full-width MDE80 ($2.8016\,\mathrm{SD}$) at
every registered anchor}, and fails family-level correction
(\texttt{holm3\_survivors\_commit=[]}, \texttt{BORDERLINE\_EQUIVOCAL}, non-load-bearing).
Thus reading
``readout difference'' as ``information difference'' is an overclaim: most aggregation-axis
differences are explained by the measurement layer, while the early/late working instance
retains a power and family-level boundary. We provide a reusable
judgment-hygiene chain covering power-bound non-detection (measurable discrimination vs.\
decision-readout resolvability), desaturation falsification,
BORDERLINE--MDE co-reporting, and form d1/d2 grading.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

In risk-gated deployment (selective prediction; Chow~1970), a decoder is asked to emit
its best decode together with a confidence-derived score, and the system abstains on the
highest-risk fraction to cover a target error --- self-consistency and numerical confidence
calibration are standard ways to build such a score \cite{wang2023selfconsistency,
guo2017calibration}. In
masked-diffusion language decoding (LLaDA-style, block-wise parallel denoising), ``which
positions' confidence to aggregate into a score'' is not unique: one may take a position's
last revision confidence (\texttt{commit}), its first-exposure confidence (\texttt{first}),
or the within-block mean (\texttt{all}); one may look only at early blocks (early window) or
late blocks (late window); and one may aggregate intra-block or cross-block. This paper asks
whether these aggregation choices read one construct --- a stable, decision-usable confidence
--- or different sources of measurement artifact. (We do \emph{not} study best-of-$k$
candidate selection: this bed decodes once per prompt, so no candidate pool exists; see
Estimand, \S\ref{sec:method:estimand}.)

\paragraph{Terminology used in this paper.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Construct}: a stable interpretative unit read from a signal.
  \item \emph{Power-bound non-detection} (supersedes our earlier ``measurable $\neq$ usable''
  phrasing): a readout may discriminate (AUROC / risk@coverage) statistically while, at this
  bed's decision-readout power, no selection increment is \emph{resolvable} (all registered
  decision anchors \texttt{POWER\_LIMITED} under the full-width MDE80). This is an
  underpowered non-detection on this bed, \emph{not} a claim that in-block selection is
  unusable; it bounds only this bed and this decision anchor set.
  \item \emph{f-rated readout family}: the three aggregation readouts \{\texttt{commit},
  \texttt{first}, \texttt{all}\}; judgment-hygiene levels d1 (direction-consistent,
  report interval) and d2 (direction-flipping, withdraw claim) characterize how they agree.
  \item \emph{BORDERLINE\_EQUIVOCAL}: a family-level verdict when a raw CI excludes 0 but
  Holm correction fails; always co-reported with MDE (non-degradable).
  \item \emph{load-bearing}: a stable analytical anchor. R3 is the headline negative
  result; R1/R2/R4/R5 are boundary instances. The R5 full-width MDE correction remains a
  load-bearing soundness correction even though R5 is not a headline finding.
\end{itemize}

This paper studies whether these aggregation axes read \emph{one construct} or
\emph{different sources of measurement artifact} (Estimand, \S\ref{sec:method}). On a single
frozen-decode bed we run zero-GPU reproducible analysis. The headline result, R3, is a
\textbf{power-bound non-detection}: the intra-block AUROC gain is measurable ($+0.0144$,
above the intra-block full-width MDE80), yet no intra-block selection increment is
\emph{resolvable} at this bed's decision-readout power (risk@coverage CIs include 0 and
every registered decision anchor is \texttt{POWER\_LIMITED} under the same H2 full-width
MDE rule). Because the decision-anchor MDE80 (not a single threshold) bounds what this bed
can detect, this is honestly stated as an underpowered non-detection rather than as the
stronger claim that in-block selection is unusable. Four boundary-strict judgment-hygiene
working instances (\S\ref{sec:results}) delimit that result:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Direction-consistent at the registered anchor, magnitude-varying} (R1):
  at cov$=0.2$ all three readouts agree point-identically
  ($\delta_{\mathrm{EL}}{=}-0.076923{=}15/195$ on the support grid); the sign agreement is
  \emph{not} claimed beyond that anchor --- at $c\ge0.55$ the readouts diverge in sign and
  the band is power-limited, not a measured agreement or null.
  \item \textbf{Not the same construct, yet decision-difference compatible with measurement}
  (R2): \texttt{commit} and \texttt{all} are not the same latent variable, but their
  decision-validity gap is explainable by the reliability gap/SNR.
  \item \textbf{Early/late-window difference is robust to a first-exposure desaturating readout} (R4): a
  desaturated readout reproduces the effect point-for-point.
  \item \textbf{High-selectivity-specific boundary} (R5, \emph{non-load-bearing}): every
  registered anchor is \texttt{POWER\_LIMITED} under the full-width MDE80, and the
  family-level Holm(3) list has no survivor, so the raw cov$=0.2$ effect remains
  \texttt{BORDERLINE\_EQUIVOCAL} rather than a usable finding.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Contributions.} (1) R3, a bounded power-bound negative: on this frozen bed, intra-block
discrimination is measurable but no intra-block \emph{selection increment} is resolvable at the
decision-readout power (all registered anchors \texttt{POWER\_LIMITED} under the full-width MDE80);
this is an underpowered non-detection, deliberately \emph{not} promoted to an ``unusable'' construct
claim; (2) R1/R2/R4/R5 as explicitly bounded judgment-hygiene working instances, not load-bearing
headline claims; (3) a reusable H1--H6 chain for judging ``readout$\rightarrow$selection'' claims
while separating the aggregated selection readout from internal-representation mechanisms.

readout difference is not automatically a usable selection signal.

\section{Measurement Construct and Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Identifiability of the readout construct}
\label{sec:method:ident}

We study a concrete, measurable question in masked-diffusion language decoding: when
confidence readouts are aggregated into a decode-level score used to select likely-correct
decodes, do different aggregation axes read \emph{one stable construct} or \emph{different
sources of measurement artifact}? We compare three axes: \textbf{aggregation score}
(\texttt{commit} = within-block last-step confidence; \texttt{first} = within-block
first-step confidence, desaturated; \texttt{all} = within-block mean), \textbf{aggregation
window} (early = blocks 0--3 vs late = blocks 4--7), and \textbf{aggregation scale}
(intra-block vs cross-block). All analysis is on a single frozen-decode bed, zero GPU.
We make \emph{no mechanism claim} about the model's internals; all claims are restricted to
the ``readout $\rightarrow$ decision score'' measurement layer.

\subsection{Estimand (decision readout)}
\label{sec:method:estimand}

\textbf{Estimand = pool-level selective abstention over $974$ distinct problems,}
with one decode per problem ($k{=}1$) and \emph{no candidate pool}: this bed has no
best-of-$k$ selection, so candidate selection ($k{>}1$, within-prompt candidate pools) is
\emph{outside the measured estimand} and is left to a separate recovery that would require
new data ($k{\ge}4$ decodes per prompt). The decision question is the selective-prediction
one: can a decode-level confidence readout be used to \emph{risk-gate} (abstain on) a
coverage-$c$ fraction of decodes so as to reduce expected error. Formally, for coverage
point $c$, readout $r$, and window $W\in\{E{=}\text{blocks 0--3},
L{=}\text{blocks 4--7}\}$:
\[
  \mathrm{risk}_{W,r}(c) \;=\; \text{error rate of the top-}(c\cdot n)\text{ decodes by}
  \ W\text{-window } r\text{-readout decode-level mean},
\]
where the selected count is $k=\max(1,\mathrm{round}(c\cdot n))$ retained decodes (the
\emph{top-}$k$ by descending score), i.e.\ \emph{banker's rounding} of $c\cdot n$ to an integer
with a floor at $1$ (at $c{=}0.2$, $n{=}974$: $\mathrm{round}(194.8){=}195$ decodes retained).
Ties in score are resolved by stable (mergesort) index order, so the retained set is
deterministic for a fixed event order; the $\delta_{\mathrm{EL}}$ values below are
point-identical on the discrete support grid (e.g.\ $-0.076923=-15/195$ at $c{=}0.2$).
with $\mathrm{base}=$ full-pool error rate (this bed: $0.2947$), and
\[
  \delta_{\mathrm{EL},r}(c) \;=\; \mathrm{risk}_{E,r}(c) - \mathrm{risk}_{L,r}(c),\quad
  \text{($<0 \Leftrightarrow$ early window is better at selecting decodes)}.
\]
The early/late $\delta_{\mathrm{EL}}$ is the \emph{window-comparison} quantity used by R1, R4,
and R5; it is \emph{not} the R3 decision estimand. R3 is the \emph{intra-block between-arm
selection increment}: the change in risk@coverage when the decode-level score is built from the
\texttt{commit} readout versus the \texttt{all} readout, restricted to a single block (rank the
$n$ decodes by one block's readout, take risk@coverage per block, average across the $8$ blocks)
\begin{equation}
  \Delta^{\mathrm{intra}}(c')\;=\;\mathrm{risk}^{\mathrm{intra-block}}_{\texttt{commit}}(c')
   \;-\;\mathrm{risk}^{\mathrm{intra-block}}_{\texttt{all}}(c'),
  \label{eq:r3-intra-arm}
\end{equation}
the quantity reported in the R3 row of Table~\ref{tab:results} and gated in
Table~\ref{tab:r3grid}. The selection \emph{benefit} of a single readout against no selection,
$\mathrm{gain}=\mathrm{base}-\mathrm{risk}$ (large, e.g.\ $\approx{+}8.7$\,pp at cov$=0.2$), is a
separate, resolvable statement about each individual readout's usefulness; it is \emph{not} the R3
increment and is kept distinct so that R3's negative applies only to the between-arm increment.
Inference uses a multiplicity-preserving cluster bootstrap over decodes and family-level Holm(3);
Table~\ref{tab:readouts} collects the exact registered settings. Here and throughout, the \emph{twin $p$} of an
estimate is the two-sided bootstrap $p$-value against the paired null, computed as
$p_{\mathrm{twin}}=2\min\big(\widehat{\mathrm{Pr}}_b[\hat\delta_b>0],\,
\widehat{\mathrm{Pr}}_b[\hat\delta_b<0]\big)$ over the $B{=}2000$ cluster-bootstrap
resamples $\hat\delta_b$; it measures direction stability of the estimate on the original
(not shuffled) labels, with the independent statistical unit being the decode ($n{=}974$). The BORDERLINE third-state discipline requires
co-reporting the full-width MDE80 (Method appendix H2). Under that budget all six registered
\texttt{commit}/\texttt{first} anchors are \texttt{POWER\_LIMITED}; consequently
BORDERLINE is a separate family-level statement, never an above-MDE anchor claim. At the
registered bootstrap setting, Holm(3) gives \texttt{holm3\_survivors\_commit=[]}: the raw
lowest-coverage CI excludes 0, but no family member survives correction. Table~\ref{tab:results}
records the deterministic-order high-$B$ seed audit and the resolved scan-order artifact,
separating those comparable values from the method narrative.

\textbf{Efficiency / power footnote.} The full-width minimum detectable effect is defined at
80\% power, two-sided $\alpha{=}0.05$,
$\mathrm{MDE}_{80}=(z_{1-\alpha/2}+z_{1-\beta})\,\mathrm{SD}=2.8016\,\mathrm{SD}$, where
$\mathrm{SD}$ denotes the \emph{observed} cluster-bootstrap standard error of the relevant
decision-anchor effect (per-anchor, Table~\ref{tab:r3grid} for R3 and
Table~\ref{tab:anchormde} for the early/late grid). Because this symbol carries
one value per scale and cov, we tag it where ambiguous: $\mathrm{MDE}_{80}^{(\mathrm{AUROC})}$
for the AUROC scale, $\mathrm{MDE}_{80}^{(\mathrm{risk},c)}$ for the decision-anchor
risk@coverage at coverage $c$, and $\mathrm{MDE}_{80}^{\mathrm{(xblk)}}$ for the cross-block
anchor (collectively, all values are registered per-anchor in Table~\ref{tab:r3grid} and
Table~\ref{tab:anchormde}). This is a
\emph{post-hoc / observed} power statement, not a design-power statement: because
$\mathrm{MDE}_{80}/|\Delta|\equiv2.8016/z$ it is a monotone rescaling of the $z$/p statistic and
cannot by itself separate ``underpowered study'' from ``small true effect.'' We therefore
co-report an \textbf{equivalence (TOST) complement} at a pre-registered practical bound
$\delta^*{=}1$\,pp (E0). Table~\ref{tab:powerplan} groups the three TOST intervals and the
corresponding planned-power sample requirements rather than stacking them in prose. Under the observed-power budget all
registered \texttt{commit}/\texttt{first}$\times\{0.2,0.5,0.8\}$ anchors are
\texttt{POWER\_LIMITED} at the observed effect sizes. The $c\ge0.55$ band is likewise
power-limited, not a measured null.

\subsection{Unified measurement narrative (all ``consistent-with'', non-causal)}
\label{sec:method:narrative}

\paragraph{Symmetric-gate summary.}
The \emph{symmetric-gate} rule (H2/H3) is applied to \emph{every} result, including the
load-bearing R3 headline: any readout whose decision-readout CI is below the registered
full-width MDE80 at its anchors is labelled \texttt{POWER\_LIMITED}, and a
\texttt{POWER\_LIMITED} result is reported as an underpowered non-detection, never promoted
to a confident positive or negative conclusion. Applied uniformly, this makes R3 itself
\texttt{POWER\_LIMITED} at every registered decision anchor: a confidence readout can
discriminate measurably (AUROC above its MDE80) while, at this bed's power, no intra-block
selection increment is resolvable. R1/R2/R4/R5 are bounded working instances that diagnose
direction, reliability/SNR, desaturation, and power/family boundaries; each is bound by the
same rule (e.g.\ R5's raw cov$=0.2$ CI excludes 0, but every registered anchor is
power-limited and Holm(3) has no survivor). Thus, under symmetric gating, no readout
difference is promoted to an independent-information or mechanism claim, and R3 states only
an underpowered non-detection on this bed --- not that intra-block selection is unusable.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Machine-readable R1--R5 numbers, evidence paths, and boundaries accompany the source in the
factsheet and claim--evidence map. Table~\ref{tab:results} moves the comparable quantities
out of prose and pairs each numeric anchor with its permitted interpretation.

\textbf{R1: Direction-consistent at the registered anchor (not noise flip).} At the
registered cov$=0.2$ anchor, all three readouts agree on the sign of the early/late
comparison and are point-identical ($\delta_{\mathrm{EL}}{=}-0.076923{=}15/195$ on the
support grid; Table~\ref{tab:results}). This is form d1 restricted to that anchor: the
direction is stable at cov$=0.2$ even though magnitude may vary by axis. The sign
agreement is explicitly \emph{not} claimed over the full coverage axis: the fine-grid scan
(r21, descriptive) shows the readouts diverge in sign toward $c\ge0.55$; Table~\ref{tab:results}
groups the three registered high-coverage estimates and their null-crossing CIs. That band
is power-limited rather than a measured agreement or null.

\textbf{R2: Distinct construct, decision gap compatible with pure SNR.} \texttt{commit} and
\texttt{all} are not the same latent variable, but the observed decision-validity difference
does not require an independent-information explanation beyond the reliability gap.
\textbf{Boundary:} R2 concerns \texttt{commit} versus \texttt{all} as latent variables on
this single frozen bed; the disattenuated reliability-correlation upper bound $0.979<1$
rejects $\rho{=}1$ on this bed, but this rejection rests on the (positively estimated)
residual-covariance correction $\mathrm{cov}_u$: breakeven analysis
(\texttt{results/r15\_singlefactor\_pointpred.json} :: \texttt{P1\_cov\_u\_sensitivity})
shows that a small over-estimate of $\mathrm{cov}_u$ erases the rejection; Table~\ref{tab:results}
reports the point and CI-upper break-even multipliers. The correction is \emph{not} protected
against noise-model misspecification for $\mathrm{cov}_u\neq0$ on real data. The
decision-validity gap is compatible with a pure SNR/structure explanation
and does not require an independent-information mechanism. The claim is bounded to this
bed and these readouts; it is not a cross-bed construct claim.

\textbf{R3: Measurable intra-block discrimination, no resolvable intra-block selection
increment at this power (a power-bound non-detection).} \emph{R3 estimand} is the intra-block
between-arm selection increment \eqref{eq:r3-intra-arm},
$\Delta^{\mathrm{intra}}(c')=\mathrm{risk}^{\mathrm{intra-block}}_{\texttt{commit}}(c')-\mathrm{risk}^{\mathrm{intra-block}}_{\texttt{all}}(c')$,
over decode-level cluster units ($\mathrm{risk}^{\mathrm{intra-block}}$ = per-block
risk@coverage averaged across the $8$ blocks). Table~\ref{tab:r3grid} gives the complete
registered R3 decision-anchor grid, including the point, CI, MDE80, gate, and control arms.
The early/late $\delta_{\mathrm{EL}}$ used by R1/R4/R5 is tabulated separately in
Table~\ref{tab:anchormde}.

\emph{Controls and symmetric gate.} The positive control is resolvable under the same
full-width MDE80 rule, whereas the shuffled-label negative control has null-crossing CIs;
Table~\ref{tab:r3grid} reports their gains, intervals, and gates. Thus the rig can detect
an effect, and R3 is a precision limit rather than a dead-rig result. Cross-block aggregation
grows the raw signal, but its registered anchor remains \texttt{POWER\_LIMITED} under the
same gate (Table~\ref{tab:r3grid}), so it does not independently
establish resolvability.

\emph{Headline comparison.} The intra-block AUROC increment $+0.0144$ is above its
AUROC-scale $\mathrm{MDE}_{80}^{(\mathrm{AUROC})}{=}0.0069$. By contrast, every registered
intra-block between-arm decision-anchor CI includes zero and its full-width MDE80 exceeds the
observed effect (Table~\ref{tab:r3grid}). The bed is therefore
\texttt{POWER\_LIMITED} at the decision readout. The TOST complement in
Table~\ref{tab:powerplan} shows that this is not an equivalence claim: the lower-coverage
anchors are inconclusive, and only the highest-coverage anchor meets the registered
equivalence bound. Table~\ref{tab:results} also records the Holm threshold check for the
measurable AUROC half, so the family correction is applied symmetrically rather than
one-directionally.

\emph{AUROC$\rightarrow\Delta$risk
derivation} (to place the two scales on one axis, not to claim the bound is tight): we
report the \emph{first-order} mapping used to make ``1.2\,pp'' reproducible, rather than a
looser absolute-AUC bound. Let $\Delta A{=}+0.0144$ be the AUROC gain over the
random-ordering baseline, $c{=}0.2$ the coverage, and $d$ the score-scale sensitivity
($d{=}1.04$ here, the derivative of risk@coverage w.r.t.\ the standardized score at
cov$=0.2$). The linear proxy is
\[
  \Delta\hat{\mathrm{risk}} \;=\; \Delta A\cdot(1-c)\cdot d
  \;\approx\; 0.0144\times 0.8\times 1.04 \;\approx\; 0.012 \;=\; 1.2\,\mathrm{pp}\ \text{(upper proxy)},
\]
which remains below the decision-anchor full-width MDE80 (Table~\ref{tab:results}); it is
the same order of magnitude, not an order of magnitude smaller. This rests on four explicit
assumptions (full derivation in
\texttt{docs/r106\_c4\_auroc\_risk\_derivation.md}): (i) \emph{monotone score}---selection by
descending score so AUC is well defined; (ii) \emph{first-order increment}---$\Delta$risk is
a linear-in-$\Delta A$ Taylor map, so the proxy is an \emph{upper bound}, not an equality;
(iii) \emph{i.i.d. units} (cluster unit$=$decode, $n{=}974$); (iv) \emph{no calibration
assumption} (sorting separability only), so the proxy bounds ordering-layer, not
decision-utility-layer, risk. Because of (ii)--(iv) the AUROC-implied bound is only a
\emph{weak upper proxy} for the decision-readout increment and cannot be separated from it
at this bed's power (``not resolvable'', not ``dissociate''). The honest reading is a
\textbf{power-bound non-detection} on this bed: we cannot resolve an intra-block selection
increment at this decision-readout power (it is below the decision-anchor MDE80), and we do
not promote the underpowered non-detection to the stronger claim ``measurable $\neq$ usable''
as a general construct statement.

\textbf{R4: Early/late difference survives desaturation.} The desaturated \texttt{first}
readout reproduces the effect point-for-point with complete coverage. The block-first
(decode,pos,block) construction is validated by the dedicated coverage audit: saturation is
real variance compression, but it does not measurably degrade ranking quality.

\textbf{R5: High-selectivity-specific, power-limited, family-level BORDERLINE (no survivor).}
The effect is power-limited at every registered anchor under the full-width MDE80. At the
registered family and bootstrap setting, Holm(3) gives
\texttt{holm3\_survivors\_commit=[]}---no point survives correction---so the verdict
remains \texttt{BORDERLINE\_EQUIVOCAL} and non-load-bearing. The raw lowest-coverage CI
excludes 0 but the family-level claim does not survive. Table~\ref{tab:results} reports the
exact anchors, deterministic-order seed audit, and boundary; Figure~\ref{fig:dELcov} shows
the coverage-local descriptive shape and uncertainty.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Single bed (explicit)}: all numbers are LLaDA-8B-Instruct $\times$ GSM8K g3\_main/A
  on this single frozen bed, $n{=}974$; no generalization to other models/datasets/temperatures.
  \item \textbf{Block-position confound}: block index is strongly correlated with, but not a
  partition of, canvas position; we make \emph{no position/timing mechanism claim}.
  \item \textbf{Single desaturation intervention}: \texttt{first} is one desaturation of
  \texttt{commit}; conclusions hold only ``up to desaturating to \texttt{first}''.
  \item \textbf{BORDERLINE non-load-bearing}\label{lim:mcnoise}: for the registered family
  and bootstrap setting in Table~\ref{tab:readouts},
  \texttt{holm3\_survivors\_commit=[]}. The raw cov$=0.2$ CI excludes 0, but no family
  member survives correction; therefore R5 is a boundary instance rather than a confirmed
  high-selectivity finding. The raw cov$=0.2$ exclusion is seed-stable under the
  deterministic-order $B{=}20{,}000$ bootstrap ($10/10$ seeds, CI-endpoint MC-SE
  $\approx3{\times}10^{-17}$), so the ``excludes $0$ but family-instable'' reading is not an
  artifact of resampler seed; the family-level knife-edge (p$=0.01636$ vs Holm $\alpha/3{=}0.01667$)
  persists by design and is reported as such.
  \item \textbf{Descriptive shape / power boundary}: the fine-grid R5 shape is descriptive
  and multiplicity-uncontrolled. All six registered anchors are \texttt{POWER\_LIMITED}
  under the full-width MDE80, and the apparent decay/reversal at $c\ge0.55$ is not a measured
  null. The instrument-null check instead uses the r17 shuffled-label negative control.
  \item \textbf{Readout scale}: single readout conf${=}$maxprob; content-vs-EOS-tail substructure
  is not separated.
  \item \textbf{Efficiency}: no efficiency claim; no wall-clock GPU-time comparison.
  \item \textbf{Citation live-recheck gap (disclosed)}: 5 of the 12 bibliography entries have
  not yet been live-rechecked at release egress (citation lock:
  \texttt{literature/citation\_lock.json}); metadata are pinned locally but a final live
  arXiv recheck is deferred.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

This work sits at the intersection of (i) generative masked-diffusion language decoding
(LLaDA-style block-level parallel noise$\rightarrow$token denoising with
sequential block progress \cite{llda}; block-progress simplex diffusion decoding \cite{ssdlm};
completeness-based early halting of diffusion LM generation \cite{halting};
confidence-thresholded parallel decoding (Fast-dLLM) \cite{wu2025fastdllm}), (ii) using
confidence/score readouts for risk-gated selection and self-assessment (calibration of modern NN confidences
\cite{guo2017calibration}; semantic uncertainty in natural-language generation \cite{kuhn2023semantic};
self-consistency \cite{wang2023selfconsistency} is a \emph{candidate-pool} practice --- multiple
decodes are scored and the best kept --- which our $k{=}1$ bed does not instantiate),
and (iii) measurement-artifact and calibration discipline
(separating ``readout difference'' from ``measurement-channel difference''). We do not claim masked
diffusion's internal representation mechanism; we treat the decode event stream as a
controlled confidence-readout channel and study the construct consistency of aggregation
axes on it. Our methodological contribution is a reusable discrimination chain
(power-bound non-detection, desaturation-falsification, BORDERLINE-MDE co-reporting, form
d1/d2 grading) showing two gaps between ``a readout discriminates'' and ``a readout can be
used for selection''---decision-readout and measurement-channel.

Our central negative finding---that intra-block confidence aggregation is measurable, yet
no intra-block selection increment is resolvable at this bed's decision-readout power (an
underpowered non-detection, not a rejection)---connects directly to selective prediction and
risk--coverage: statistical discrimination need not yield actionable selection utility at
a user-set risk \citep{chow1970character,bartlett2008classification,
elyaniv2010foundations,geifman2017selective}. LLM uncertainty and selective-generation work
\citep{kuhn2023semantic,lin2022teaching}, together with confidence-based early halting in
masked-diffusion language models \citep{halting}, frames the corresponding decode-time
utility question. Our judgment-hygiene chain is complementary: it proposes no new selective
classifier, but isolates decision-readout and measurement-channel gaps between ``a readout
discriminates'' and ``a readout can be used for selection.''

\textbf{Positioning against confidence-gated in-decoding decoding.} The closest prior art
\citep{wu2025fastdllm} treats in-decoding confidence as an \emph{actionable, per-token}
signal (threshold-gated parallel unmasking order), whereas our R3 test uses the \emph{decode-
level} aggregation of that same confidence as a risk-gating readout for selective prediction.
The two are not in
tension: a per-token unmasking decision can be usable while the decode-level mean over a
window fails as a selection readout at this bed's decision-readout power. Our contribution is
the separation of these two granularities (and of ``discrimination'' from ``risk-gated selection
utility''), not a claim that in-decoding confidence is generally useless---indeed the
confidence-gated line reports it is useful per-token, which is compatible with our bounded,
bed-specific decode-level null.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We study single-decode ($k{=}1$) masked-diffusion decoding under the selective-prediction
question: can an in-decoding confidence readout \emph{risk-gate} (abstain on) a coverage-$c$
fraction of decodes to reduce expected error? Best-of-$k$ candidate selection is \emph{not}
in scope --- this bed decodes once per prompt, so the question of picking among per-prompt
candidate decodes never arises. Within that scope, the headline, R3, is a
\emph{power-bound non-detection}: on this frozen masked-diffusion
bed, intra-block discrimination is measurable (AUROC gain $+0.0144$ above its full-width
AUROC-scale $\mathrm{MDE}_{80}^{(\mathrm{AUROC})}{=}0.0069$), yet at this bed's decision-readout
power no intra-block risk-gated selection increment is resolvable: each of the three
\emph{intra-block} R3 between-arm decision-anchor CIs includes $0$ and its full-width MDE exceeds
the observed effect (Table~\ref{tab:r3grid}). The single cross-block row in
Table~\ref{tab:r3grid} is the sole $0\notin$CI exception: its raw CI excludes $0$ but is itself
\texttt{POWER\_LIMITED} under the same H2 full-width rule, so it is not promoted to a resolvable
rejection. Because
this is an underpowered non-detection (not a rejection), we do \emph{not} conclude that
in-block selection is unusable on this or any other bed; the claim is bounded to
``intra-block risk-gated selection increments below the stated decision-anchor MDE80 are not
resolvable on this bed at this power.'' R1/R2/R4/R5 are judgment-hygiene boundary instances, not
headline findings. R5 is power-limited at every registered anchor and has no Holm(3)
survivor, so its raw cov$=0.2$ effect remains \texttt{BORDERLINE\_EQUIVOCAL} and
non-load-bearing. The H1--H6 chain keeps ``readout$\rightarrow$selection'' claims separated
at the decision-readout and measurement-channel layers; under symmetric gating it never
turns a readout difference -- nor an underpowered non-detection -- into an information or
mechanism claim.
