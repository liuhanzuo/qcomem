015           Masked-diffusion (LLaDA-style) decoding produces a single decode per prompt,
016           and the decoder’s internal conﬁdence is a candidate signal for risk-gated deploy-
017           ment (Chow 1970-style selective prediction: abstain on the highest-risk fraction to
018
              cover a target risk). We ask whether, in block-wise denoising, revision-end conﬁ-
              dence (commit), ﬁrst-exposure conﬁdence (first), within-block mean (all),
019
              early versus late windows, and intra-block versus cross-block aggregation read
020
              the same stable construct or measurement artifacts of different sources. On one
021           frozen LLaDA-8B-Instruct × GSM8K bed (n=974 distinct problems, one decode
022           each, k=1 — no best-of-k pool), zero-GPU reproducible analysis ﬁnds intra-
023           block discrimination statistically measurable (AUROC gain +0.0144 exceeds its
024           full-width AUROC-scale MDE80), yet at this bed’s decision-readout power no
025           intra-block risk-gated selection increment is resolvable. These statements quan-
026           tify two distinct, non-mergable estimands of different units and are never equated:
027           the AUROC gain is a single-readout discriminative lift over a random-ordering
028           baseline (AUROC units); the selection increment is the paired commit − all
029
              risk@coverage difference within blocks (risk units). Their juxtaposition is a cross-
              scale gate contrast, not a contradiction and not one merged quantity (the AUROC-
030
              side treatment contrast is readout versus random ordering, whereas the risk-side
031
              contrast is paired commit versus all — the two comparisons answer different
032           questions and are never merged). Under that gate every registered intra-block
033           decision-readout anchor is POWER_LIMITED under the full-width MDE80 (H2):
034           an underpowered non-detection, not a rejection — we cannot rule out an increment
035           smaller than the decision-anchor MDE80, and do not claim in-block selection is
036           unusable elsewhere. Four judgment-hygiene working instances bound that result:
037           at cov= 0.2 the three readouts agree in direction and are point-identical, while the
038           c ≥ 0.55 low-selectivity band is power-limited rather than a measured agreement
039           or null; commit and all are distinct constructs whose decision-validity gap is
040
              compatible with reliability/SNR; a desaturated readout reproduces the early/late
              effect; and that effect is high-selectivity-speciﬁc, power-limited under the bed’s
041
              full-width MDE80 at every registered anchor, with no family-level survivor
042
              under multiplicity correction (BORDERLINE_EQUIVOCAL, non-load-bearing),
043           with one explicit limitation here: the AUROC result uses a random-ordering base-
044           line while the selection-increment result uses a paired all baseline, so the two
045           results are not a dissociation test of one construct against the other. A label-
046           permutation zero-effect null conﬁrms the decision rule rejects at or below nominal
047           (conservative, not anti-conservative). Reading “readout difference” as “informa-
048           tion difference” is therefore an overclaim: most aggregation-axis differences are
049           explained by the measurement layer, while the early/late instance retains a power
050           and family-level boundary. We provide a reusable judgment-hygiene chain cover-
051
              ing power-bound non-detection, desaturation falsiﬁcation, BORDERLINE–MDE

      1    I NTRODUCTION
055
056
057   In risk-gated deployment (selective prediction (Chow, 1970; Bartlett & Wegkamp, 2008; El-Yaniv
058   & Wiener, 2010; Geifman & El-Yaniv, 2017)), a decoder emits its best decode together with a
059   conﬁdence-derived score, and the system abstains on the highest-risk fraction to cover a target error
060   — self-consistency and numerical conﬁdence calibration are standard ways to build such a score
061   Wang et al. (2023); Guo et al. (2017). In masked-diffusion language decoding (LLaDA-style, block-
062   wise parallel denoising), “which positions’ conﬁdence to aggregate into a score” is not unique: one
063   may take a position’s last revision conﬁdence (commit), its ﬁrst-exposure conﬁdence (first), or
064
      the within-block mean (all); only early or only late blocks; intra-block or cross-block. This paper
      asks whether these choices read one construct — a stable, decision-usable conﬁdence — or different
065
      sources of measurement artifact. (We do not study best-of-k candidate selection: this bed decodes
066
      once per prompt, so no candidate pool exists; see Estimand, §2.2.)
067
068
069
070   Terminology. A construct is a stable interpretative unit read from a signal. Power-bound non-
071
      detection (superseding our earlier “measurable ̸= usable” phrasing) names the situation where a read-
      out discriminates statistically (AUROC / risk@coverage) while, at this bed’s decision-readout power,
072
      no selection increment is resolvable — every registered decision anchor is POWER_LIMITED
073
      under the full-width MDE80; that is an underpowered non-detection bounded to this bed and
074   anchor set, not a claim that in-block selection is unusable. The f-rated readout family is
075   {commit,first,all}, whose agreement is graded d1 (direction-consistent: report the interval)
076   or d2 (direction-ﬂipping: withdraw the claim). BORDERLINE_EQUIVOCAL is a family-level ver-
077   dict when a raw CI excludes 0 but Holm correction fails; it is always co-reported with its MDE
078   (non-degradable). Load-bearing means a stable analytical anchor: R3 is the headline negative
079   and R1/R2/R4/R5 are boundary instances, while the R5 full-width MDE correction remains a load-
080   bearing soundness correction.
081   On a single frozen-decode bed we run zero-GPU reproducible analysis. The headline result, R3, is a
082   power-bound non-detection: the intra-block AUROC gain is measurable (+0.0144, above the intra-
083   block full-width MDE80), yet no intra-block selection increment is resolvable at this bed’s decision-
084   readout power (risk@coverage CIs include 0; every registered decision anchor is POWER_LIMITED
085   under the same H2 full-width MDE rule). Since the decision-anchor MDE80 bounds what this bed
086   can detect, we state it as an underpowered non-detection, not as the stronger claim that in-block
087   selection is unusable. Four boundary-strict working instances (§3) delimit that result:
088
089
             • Direction-consistent at the registered anchor, magnitude-varying (R1): at cov= 0.2
090
               all three readouts agree point-identically (δEL = − 0.076923=15/195 on the support grid);
091            sign agreement is not claimed beyond that anchor — at c ≥ 0.55 the readouts diverge in
092            sign and the band is power-limited, not a measured agreement or null.
093          • Not the same construct, yet decision-difference compatible with measurement (R2):
094            commit and all are not the same latent variable, but their decision-validity gap is ex-
095            plainable by the reliability gap/SNR.
096          • Early/late difference robust to a desaturating readout (R4): the ﬁrst-exposure readout
097            reproduces the effect point-for-point.
098          • High-selectivity-speciﬁc boundary (R5, non-load-bearing): every registered anchor is
099
               POWER_LIMITED under the full-width MDE80 and Holm(3) has no survivor, so the raw
               cov= 0.2 effect stays BORDERLINE_EQUIVOCAL.

101
102   Contributions. (1) R3, a bounded power-bound negative: intra-block discrimination is measurable
103   but no intra-block selection increment is resolvable at this bed’s decision-readout power, deliberately
104   not promoted to an “unusable” construct claim; (2) a label-permutation zero-effect check showing
105   the registered gate is conservative rather than anti-conservative, so the negative is not an artifact
106   of over-rejection; (3) R1/R2/R4/R5 as explicitly bounded judgment-hygiene working instances; (4)
107   a reusable H1–H6 chain for judging “readout→selection” claims while separating the aggregated
      selection readout from internal-representation mechanisms.

144   to the “readout → decision score” measurement layer.

      5   L IMITATIONS
438
439         1. Single bed, k=1, no generation replication: all numbers are LLaDA-8B-Instruct × GSM8K
440            g3_main/A on one frozen bed, n=974, one decode per problem. There are no independent de-
441            code seeds, repeated generations, or replicate beds, and no generalization to other models, datasets,
442            or temperatures.
443
            2. Block-position confound: block index is strongly correlated with, but not a partition of, canvas
               position. Early/late is a per-block-window factor, not a fully crossed manipulation, so the analysis
444            cannot separate window from position and makes no position/timing mechanism claim.
445         3. Single desaturation intervention: first is one desaturation of commit; conclusions hold only
446            “up to desaturating to first”.
447         4. BORDERLINE non-load-bearing: for the registered family and bootstrap setting (Table 8) the
448
               Holm(3) survivor set is empty — the raw cov= 0.2 CI excludes 0, but no family member survives
               correction, so R5 is a boundary instance rather than a conﬁrmed high-selectivity ﬁnding (Monte
449            Carlo quantities: Table 3). Seed-level survivor status can ﬂip even though CI exclusion is stable,
450            so we retain the registered empty-family verdict and report the knife-edge mechanism rather than
451            treating either side as exact.
452         5. Descriptive shape / power boundary: the ﬁne-grid R5 shape is descriptive and multiplicity-
453
               uncontrolled; all six registered anchors are POWER_LIMITED under the full-width MDE80, and
               the apparent decay/reversal at c ≥ 0.55 is not a measured null. The instrument-null check instead
454            uses the r17 shufﬂed-label negative control.
455         6. Asymmetric treatment contrasts (disclosure): the two halves of the head-to-head narrative are
456            evaluated against different treatment contrasts — AUROC is compared with a random-ordering base-
457            line, risk@coverage with paired all — so the two results do not constitute a dissociation test of
458
               “discrimination ̸⇒ selection.” We report the co-existence of a resolvable discrimination lift and a
               power-limited selection increment as exactly that, not as evidence discriminating the two constructs
459            against each other. The paired-contrast engineering (commit−all on both scales; Table 5) supplies
460            the same-contrast companion analysis and reaches the same bounded conclusion.
461         7. Type-I check scope: the label-permutation calibration (Table 4) validates the decision rule under a
462            zero-effect null on this bed only; it certiﬁes non-inﬂated rejection, not power, and a conservative gate
463
               makes a non-detection easier to obtain.
            8. Readout scale and efﬁciency: single readout conf=maxprob; content-vs-EOS-tail substructure is
464            not separated; no efﬁciency claim and no wall-clock GPU-time comparison.
465         9. Citation live-recheck gap (disclosed): 5 of the 13 bibliography entries have not yet been live-
466            rechecked at release egress (citation lock: literature/citation_lock.json); metadata are
467            pinned locally but a ﬁnal live arXiv recheck is deferred.

470
471   This work sits at the intersection of (i) masked-diffusion language decoding (LLaDA-style block-
472   level parallel noise→token denoising with sequential block progress Nie et al. (2025); block-
473   progress simplex diffusion decoding Han et al. (2023); completeness-based early halting Lo Ci-
474   cero Vaina et al. (2023); conﬁdence-thresholded parallel decoding (Fast-dLLM) Wu et al. (2025)),
475   (ii) conﬁdence/score readouts for risk-gated selection and self-assessment (calibration of modern NN
476   conﬁdences Guo et al. (2017); semantic uncertainty Kuhn et al. (2023); self-consistency Wang et al.
477
      (2023) is a candidate-pool practice — multiple decodes are scored and the best kept — which our
      k=1 bed does not instantiate), and (iii) measurement-artifact and calibration discipline. We make no
478
      claim about masked diffusion’s internal representation mechanism; we treat the decode event stream
479
      as a controlled conﬁdence-readout channel and study the construct consistency of aggregation axes
480   on it. Our central negative — intra-block aggregation is measurable, yet no intra-block selection
481   increment is resolvable at this bed’s decision-readout power — connects directly to selective pre-
482   diction and risk–coverage: statistical discrimination need not yield actionable selection utility at a
483   user-set risk (Chow, 1970; Bartlett & Wegkamp, 2008; El-Yaniv & Wiener, 2010; Geifman & El-
484   Yaniv, 2017). LLM uncertainty and selective-generation work (Kuhn et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2022),
485   with conﬁdence-based early halting in masked-diffusion LMs (Lo Cicero Vaina et al., 2023), frames
      the corresponding decode-time utility question. Our methodological contribution proposes no new


                                                             9
      Under review as a conference paper at ICLR 2026




486
      selective classiﬁer: a reusable discrimination chain (power-bound non-detection, label-permutation
487   null calibration, desaturation-falsiﬁcation, BORDERLINE–MDE co-reporting, form d1/d2 grading)
488   isolating two gaps — decision-readout and measurement-channel — between “a readout discrimi-
489   nates” and “a readout can be used for selection.”
490
      Positioning against conﬁdence-gated in-decoding decoding. The closest prior art (Wu et al., 2025)
491
      treats in-decoding conﬁdence as an actionable, per-token signal (threshold-gated parallel unmasking
492
      order), whereas our R3 test uses the decode-level aggregation of that conﬁdence as a risk-gating
493   readout. The two are not in tension: a per-token unmasking decision can be usable while the decode-
494   level mean over a window fails as a selection readout at this bed’s power. Our contribution is the
495   separation of these granularities (and of “discrimination” from “risk-gated selection utility”), not
496   a claim that in-decoding conﬁdence is generally useless — that line reports it is useful per-token,
497   compatible with our bounded, bed-speciﬁc decode-level null.

501

502   readout can risk-gate (abstain on) a coverage-c fraction of decodes to reduce expected error; best-
503   of-k selection is out of scope, as this bed decodes once per prompt. The headline, R3, is a power-
504   bound non-detection: intra-block discrimination is measurable (AUROC gain +0.0144 above its
505                     (AUROC)

      The ﬁrst-order projection ∆riskˆ = ∆A·(1−c)·d used in §3 is a scale projection of the discrimination
628
      estimand into risk units, never a merge of the two estimands. It rests on four explicit assumptions
629
      (full derivation in docs/r106_c4_auroc_risk_derivation.md): (i) monotone score —
630   selection is by descending score, so the AUC is well deﬁned; (ii) ﬁrst-order increment — ∆risk is
631   a linear-in-∆A Taylor map, so the reported value is neither a conﬁdence bound nor an equality; (iii)
632   i.i.d. units (cluster unit = decode, n=974); and (iv) no calibration assumption (sorting separability
633   only), so the proxy speaks to the ordering layer and not to the decision-utility layer. Because of
634   (ii)–(iv) the AUROC-implied value is only a ﬁrst-order point estimate for the decision-readout incre-
635   ment and cannot be separated from it at this bed’s power (“not resolvable”, not “dissociate”). The
636   measured selection estimand remains the between-arm increment of Table 2, and the AUROC-scale
              (AUROC)
637   MDE80             is never compared directly against a risk-anchor MDE80.
