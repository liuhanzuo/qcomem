011                                               A BSTRACT
012
013            Masked-diffusion decoders expose several token-conﬁdence summaries, but it is
014            unclear whether choosing among them changes decode-level selective-prediction
015            utility. On one frozen LLaDA-8B-Instruct × GSM8K bed (974 distinct prob-
016            lems, one decode each), we compare last-revision (commit), ﬁrst-exposure
017            (first), and within-block mean (all) readouts with decode-clustered in-
018            ference. Under one aligned within-block contrast and one paired bootstrap,
019            AUROC(commit)−AUROC(all) separates the two readouts in rank units
020
               (+0.0278; review-driven, post-alignment exploratory analysis), while every
               paired risk difference across the coverage grid {0.2, 0.5, 0.8} is small and has
021
               a zero-crossing CI within this intra estimand (all point values and intervals in
022
               Table 5). The early/late equality at coverage 0.2 is a shared 1/195 lattice obser-
023            vation rather than evidence of identical retained sets, and a noise-model-sensitive
024            reliability bound cannot robustly separate ρ = 1 from ρ < 1. Under a post-
025            alignment, exploratory analysis this bed separates the two readouts in rank units,
026            but not a practically equivalent or generally null selection increment; it supplies an
027            auditable analysis protocol over a frozen stream and speciﬁes the replicated bed
028            needed to falsify that bounded result.

      1    I NTRODUCTION
033
034   Selective prediction trades coverage for lower conditional risk by abstaining on low-conﬁdence ex-
035   amples (Chow, 1970; El-Yaniv & Wiener, 2010; Bartlett & Wegkamp, 2008; Geifman & El-Yaniv,
036   2017). A masked-diffusion language model revises positions in parallel, so a decode admits sev-
037   eral plausible conﬁdence summaries: last-revision conﬁdence (commit), ﬁrst-exposure conﬁdence
038
      (first), and the within-block mean (all). Their ranking quality need not imply an observable dif-
      ference in risk at a chosen coverage, and non-signiﬁcance cannot by itself establish absence (Altman
039
      & Bland, 1995).
040
041   We study that measurement problem on a single frozen event stream, not model internals and not
042   best-of-k generation. Each of the 974 prompts has exactly one decode, making prompt, problem,
043   and decode the same independent cluster. The central analysis aligns the treatment contrast on both
044
      readouts: commit−all AUROC and risk@coverage use the same decodes and paired resamples.
      A second, explicitly different AUROC-over-random estimand is retained only to connect the result
045
      to the registered discrimination analysis.
046
047   Our contributions are: (i) a same-arm comparison whose post-alignment, exploratory rank-
048   separation estimate is paired against zero-crossing risk increments; (ii) a calibrated, joint-endpoint
049   analysis showing that the three-anchor family design is not solely responsible for the negative; and
050
      (iii) boundary audits for lattice equality, retained-set overlap, residual-covariance sensitivity, prac-
      tical equivalence, and experiment recoverability. Claims are restricted to this event stream and the
051
      recorded estimator.

      2.2   G ATE AND POWER CONVENTIONS
109
110   Inference resamples decodes as clusters and preserves the readout and coverage vector within a
111   resample (Field & Welsh, 2007). Registered anchor families use Holm’s sequential procedure over
112   c ∈ {0.2, 0.5, 0.8} (Holm, 1979). A conventional two-sided 95% CI and ptwo-sided report detection.
113   Practical absence is evaluated separately by two one-sided tests (TOST) at the registered practical
114   bound δ ∗ = 1 percentage point (E0) (Schuirmann, 1987).
115
      The historical H2 ﬂag uses MDE80 = 2.8016 SE    c and labels MDE80 /|∆| ˆ ≥ 1 as PL. Algebraically
116
      this is a two-sided z threshold with de facto α = 2{1 − Φ(2.8016)} ≈ 0.0051, stricter than nominal
117
      0.05. We retain H2 only as a conservative 80%-power robustness criterion. Observed-SE MDE80
118   is a descriptive sensitivity/design reference, never a post-hoc substitute for the CI/p detection de-
119   cision (Hoenig & Heisey, 2001). Thus the cross-block row that excludes zero at nominal 95%
120   is detected but below the robustness threshold, not a non-detection. Only TOST can support a
121   practical-equivalence statement.
122
123
      Statistical conventions and prior art. Holm correction, clustered resampling, TOST, and the
124
      distinction between absence of evidence and evidence of absence are established guidance (Holm,
125
      1979; Field & Welsh, 2007; Schuirmann, 1987; Altman & Bland, 1995). Our H1–H6 appendix
126   restates those principles for readout-to-selection claims. The practice combination speciﬁc to this
127   study is to co-report the full-width, scale-tagged H2 sensitivity and to re-run the entire CI-plus-
128   family procedure under label permutation (Table 7); neither component is claimed as a new statistical
129   test.

307   Three literature streams leave the present question open. Discrete and masked diffusion work es-
308   tablishes denoising in discrete state spaces and competitive language modeling (Austin et al., 2021;
309
      Sahoo et al., 2024; Nie et al., 2025; Han et al., 2023; Ye et al., 2025); it does not establish which in-
      ternal conﬁdence aggregation is a stable decode-level selective score. Failure prediction, calibration,
310
      and LLM self-assessment show that maximum probability, verbalized conﬁdence, or self-evaluation
311
      can track correctness (Hendrycks & Gimpel, 2017; Guo et al., 2017; Lin et al., 2022; Kadavath et al.,
312   2022; Kuhn et al., 2023); they leave window and aggregation choice largely implicit. Selective clas-
313   siﬁcation and risk–coverage evaluation formalize the utility target (Chow, 1970; El-Yaniv & Wiener,
314   2010; Geifman & El-Yaniv, 2017; 2019; Ding et al., 2020), but not the measurement provenance of
315   an iterative decoder.
316
      Closest conﬁdence-gated decoding work. Fast-dLLM uses token conﬁdence as an actionable per-
317
      token signal for threshold-gated parallel unmasking (Wu et al., 2025). Our outcome is decode-level
318   risk after aggregating that signal. A per-token action can therefore be useful while a difference
319   between two decode-level summaries is unresolved. Early halting and self-consistency address stop-
320   ping or multiple-sample agreement (Lo Cicero Vaina et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023); the present
321   k = 1 bed instantiates neither a candidate pool nor repeated generation. The LLaDA line itself cou-
322   ples per-token conﬁdence with the remasking schedule (Nie et al., 2025; low-conﬁdence remasking
323   and its conﬁdence-aware variants), but does not treat any in-decoding aggregation as a decode-level
      selective score against a paired, clustered risk endpoint; that is precisely the junction measured here.

560   Each decode produces an event stream of records (pos, step, conf), one per revision of position pos:

562   that position. A position is ﬁrst exposed at its ﬁrst recorded event and committed at its last; a position
563   is revised if and only if |Ed,p | > 1. Positions with no recorded event carry no score and are excluded.
564   The three readouts and the decode-level selective score are built by Figure 3. Event extraction from
565   the frozen decoder dump is unchanged from the frozen asset; only the aggregation is restated here.
