      1   I NTRODUCTION
046
047
      In risk-gated deployment (selective prediction; Chow 1970), a decoder is asked to emit its best de-
048   code together with a conﬁdence-derived score, and the system abstains on the highest-risk fraction
049   to cover a target error — self-consistency and numerical conﬁdence calibration are standard ways
050   to build such a score Wang et al. (2023); Guo et al. (2017). In masked-diffusion language decod-
051   ing (LLaDA-style, block-wise parallel denoising), “which positions’ conﬁdence to aggregate into a
052   score” is not unique: one may take a position’s last revision conﬁdence (commit), its ﬁrst-exposure
053   conﬁdence (first), or the within-block mean (all); one may look only at early blocks (early
      window) or late blocks (late window); and one may aggregate intra-block or cross-block. This paper

056   bed decodes once per prompt, so no candidate pool exists; see Estimand, §2.2.)

      2.1     I DENTIFIABILITY OF THE READOUT CONSTRUCT
133
134   We study a concrete, measurable question in masked-diffusion language decoding: when conﬁdence
135   readouts are aggregated into a decode-level score used to select likely-correct decodes, do different
136   aggregation axes read one stable construct or different sources of measurement artifact? We compare
137   three axes: aggregation score (commit = within-block last-step conﬁdence; first = within-block
138   ﬁrst-step conﬁdence, desaturated; all = within-block mean), aggregation window (early = blocks
139   0–3 vs late = blocks 4–7), and aggregation scale (intra-block vs cross-block). All analysis is on a
140   single frozen-decode bed, zero GPU. We make no mechanism claim about the model’s internals; all
141   claims are restricted to the “readout → decision score” measurement layer.

      6    R ELATED W ORK
507
508
      This work sits at the intersection of (i) generative masked-diffusion language decoding (LLaDA-
509
      style block-level parallel noise→token denoising with sequential block progress Nie et al. (2025);
510
      block-progress simplex diffusion decoding Han et al. (2023); completeness-based early halting of
511   diffusion LM generation Lo Cicero Vaina et al. (2023); conﬁdence-thresholded parallel decoding
512   (Fast-dLLM) Wu et al. (2025)), (ii) using conﬁdence/score readouts for risk-gated selection and
513   self-assessment (calibration of modern NN conﬁdences Guo et al. (2017); semantic uncertainty in
514   natural-language generation Kuhn et al. (2023); self-consistency Wang et al. (2023) is a candidate-
515   pool practice — multiple decodes are scored and the best kept — which our k=1 bed does not instan-
516   tiate), and (iii) measurement-artifact and calibration discipline (separating “readout difference” from
517   “measurement-channel difference”). We do not claim masked diffusion’s internal representation
518   mechanism; we treat the decode event stream as a controlled conﬁdence-readout channel and study
519
      the construct consistency of aggregation axes on it. Our methodological contribution is a reusable
      discrimination chain (power-bound non-detection, desaturation-falsiﬁcation, BORDERLINE-MDE
520
      co-reporting, form d1/d2 grading) showing two gaps between “a readout discriminates” and “a read-
521
      out can be used for selection”—decision-readout and measurement-channel.
522
523   Our central negative ﬁnding—that intra-block conﬁdence aggregation is measurable, yet no intra-
524   block selection increment is resolvable at this bed’s decision-readout power (an underpowered non-
525
      detection, not a rejection)—connects directly to selective prediction and risk–coverage: statistical
      discrimination need not yield actionable selection utility at a user-set risk (Chow, 1970; Bartlett
526
      & Wegkamp, 2008; El-Yaniv & Wiener, 2010; Geifman & El-Yaniv, 2017). LLM uncertainty and
527
      selective-generation work (Kuhn et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2022), together with conﬁdence-based early
528   halting in masked-diffusion language models (Lo Cicero Vaina et al., 2023), frames the correspond-
529   ing decode-time utility question. Our judgment-hygiene chain is complementary: it proposes no new
530   selective classiﬁer, but isolates decision-readout and measurement-channel gaps between “a readout
531   discriminates” and “a readout can be used for selection.”
532
      Positioning against conﬁdence-gated in-decoding decoding. The closest prior art (Wu et al., 2025)
533
      treats in-decoding conﬁdence as an actionable, per-token signal (threshold-gated parallel unmask-
534   ing order), whereas our R3 test uses the decode- level aggregation of that same conﬁdence as a
535   risk-gating readout for selective prediction. The two are not in tension: a per-token unmasking de-
536   cision can be usable while the decode-level mean over a window fails as a selection readout at this
537   bed’s decision-readout power. Our contribution is the separation of these two granularities (and of
538   “discrimination” from “risk-gated selection utility”), not a claim that in-decoding conﬁdence is gen-
539   erally useless—indeed the conﬁdence-gated line reports it is useful per-token, which is compatible
      with our bounded, bed-speciﬁc decode-level null.

      We study single-decode (k=1) masked-diffusion decoding under the selective-prediction question:
543   can an in-decoding conﬁdence readout risk-gate (abstain on) a coverage-c fraction of decodes to
544   reduce expected error? Best-of-k candidate selection is not in scope — this bed decodes once
545   per prompt, so the question of picking among per-prompt candidate decodes never arises. Within
546   that scope, the headline, R3, is a power-bound non-detection: on this frozen masked-diffusion bed,
