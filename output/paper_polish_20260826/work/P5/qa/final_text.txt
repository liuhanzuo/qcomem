                                                  A BSTRACT
010
011
               Checkpoint policies are often evaluated under a fixed training distribution. We
012            instead impose a controlled temporal endpoint shock in synthetic sequence classi-
013            fication: labels remain clean until a predetermined subset is flipped during the final
014            quarter of training. A last-margin alarm compares the final calibration loss with
015            its running minimum, applies a threshold frozen on separate FIT seeds, and rolls
016            deployment back from last to best validation. On disjoint hold-out seeds, the
017            alarm detects all 64/64 shocked runs and flags none of the 64 healthy runs; roll-
018            back reduces mean outer loss by 0.3395 relative to last. However, every shocked
019            trajectory reaches its minimum immediately before the shock, so the gate, best-
020
               val, and early-stop return identical outcomes. The experiment therefore supports
               contamination detection and rollback relative to endpoint deployment, not discov-
021
               ery of a checkpoint unavailable to best-val. Healthy m ≡ 0 follows from strictly
022
               decreasing trajectories, leaving realistic deployment false alarms unmeasured.
023
024
025   1   I NTRODUCTION
026
027   A late event can invalidate an otherwise usable endpoint checkpoint. Static corruption does not
028   isolate this failure: when corruption is active from the first epoch, a poor final checkpoint does not
029   show that a previously clean candidate was later damaged. We construct the temporal case directly,
030   ending a clean prefix immediately before a final-quarter label shock. In every shocked hold-out run,
031   the checkpoint selected by best validation is recorded just before the shock begins (Figure 1).
032   We audit a simple deployment signal, the last margin m = L11 − min0≤t≤11 Lt , where Lt is
033   calibration loss after epoch t. The signal is read only at the end of the run. If m exceeds a thresh-
034   old calibrated without hold-out access, the gate raises a contamination flag and deploys the best-
035   validation checkpoint rather than last. Figure 1 shows actual healthy and shocked trajectories, not
036   a schematic.
037
      The experiment is pre-registered and deliberately narrow. Separate FIT seeds calibrate the threshold;
038   disjoint hold-out seeds settle detection and checkpoint outcomes (Tables 1 and 7). The endpoint
039   default is damaged, whereas the gate rolls each affected run back to the same pre-shock checkpoint
040   selected by best-val and early-stop (Tables 2 and 3). This distinction—measurable value versus
041   last, but no checkpoint advantage over existing selectors—sets the scope of the paper.
042
      Our contributions are:
043
044         1. a timing-controlled endpoint-shock protocol with a frozen FIT/hold-out split;
045
            2. a paired measurement of alarmed rollback versus last; and
046
047
            3. an explicit boundary: the gate does not outperform best-val or early-stop here, and the
               healthy carrier does not measure realistic false alarms.

      3.1   A TRUE TEMPORAL ENDPOINT SHOCK
088
      A run produces one calibration loss Lt and one model snapshot after each of E = 12 epochs. The
089
      shocked mode starts from the same clean training examples as the healthy mode. A predetermined
090   subset of 150 of the 600 training examples is designated as affected. Crucially, designation does not
091   change any label during epochs 0, . . . , 8. The command-line control --shock-time-frac=
092   0.25 sets
093
                                                        tshock = ⌈(1 − 0.25)E⌉ = 9.                                          (1)
094
095   During epochs 9, 10, 11 only, the affected examples use the deterministic target map y 7→ (y +
096   1) mod K; all other labels remain clean. Epoch shuffling changes batch order but not this onset.
097   The intervention is therefore a time-gated late-training event, rather than a claim that sample identity
098   encodes training time.
099   The observed timeline validates the intended mechanism. All 64 shocked trajectories attain their
100   minimum at epoch 8, immediately before tshock = 9, and all subsequent shocked points remain
101   above that minimum. Thus the clean candidate that later becomes best-val exists before the inter-
102   vention, while last is recorded after three affected epochs.

247   5.4     T HE ZERO FALSE - ALARM RATE IS A DESIGN EXCLUSION , NOT DEPLOYMENT EVIDENCE
248
249   FAR = 0 is a real observation on this carrier, but the carrier excludes non-zero healthy margins.
250   Every healthy trajectory is strictly decreasing at every adjacent transition, so its final point is nec-
251   essarily the minimum and m ≡ 0. Table 5 distinguishes this mechanism from whole-cell readout
252   collapse using the complete transition count and independent outer-loss and accuracy readouts.
253   The exact FAR bound therefore applies only to the registered monotone healthy population. A real
254   fine-tuning carrier with normal stochastic non-monotonicity is required to measure the deployment
255   false-alarm distribution; E1 does not supply that carrier.

260   5.5.1           T HE FLAG HAS MEASURABLE ENDPOINT VALUE , BUT DOES NOT DISCOVER A NEW

      6   D ISCUSSION
303
304   Because the affected labels activate only after a clean prefix, the experiment establishes the ordering
305   needed for an endpoint-shock audit: pre-shock minimum, shock onset, and damaged final deploy-
306   ment. The paired benefit and positive control in Table 3 quantify this same operational event from
307   opposite directions.
308
      The meaningful policy comparison is correspondingly narrow. The gate has measured value against
309
      last, but no checkpoint advantage over best-val or early-stop. SWA, EMA, soup, and rollback also
310   absorb much of the late shock (Table 2), so the experiment does not establish that an explicit alarm
311   is necessary when a pipeline already deploys a pre-shock selector.
312
313
      Table 6 makes the evidential boundary explicit. In particular, the healthy result is internally valid but
      externally narrow: strict monotonicity is verified as a real signal, yet it removes the variation needed
314
      for a realistic FAR test.

349   8    C ONCLUSION
350
351   Under a controlled final-quarter label shock, the FIT-frozen last-margin alarm detects all shocked
352   runs and rolls the damaged endpoint back to a pre-shock checkpoint. Its outer-loss benefit is mea-
353   sured relative to last; it offers no checkpoint advantage over best-val or early-stop, which return
354
      the same outcomes. Realistic false-alarm behavior and any advantage beyond existing selectors
      remain unmeasured.
