\begin{abstract}
Multiple-choice accuracy for a diffusion language model is not determined by
the model alone: the scorer chooses which option tokens are visible, when they
are revealed, and whether revealed ground-truth tokens are fed back into later
steps.  We audit that measurement construction on one bounded bed:
LLaDA-8B-Instruct with balanced four-choice GSM8K-MC.

On a confirmatory $n{=}400$ pool \sconf{} exceeds the non-injected
\mcu{} reference by $+13.0\pp$ at the per-candidate
$n_{{\rm mc},i}=2L_i$ total-NFE-fair equalization point ($b/c=109/57$,
exact $p=6.6\!\times\!10^{-5}$; four tested budgets, conservative read
$+9.0\pp$).  This \emph{refutes} the narrow account that the advantage is
an artifact of the reference receiving less compute, but establishes no
budget-independent law.  On a disjoint fresh pool a fixed left-to-right
order whose \emph{schedule selection} reads no model output still beats
\sconf{}; confidence ordering is therefore neither necessary nor uniquely
optimal on this answer format.  Rank-agreement and distractor-perturbation
diagnostics support only a bed-local, sign-consistent non-interchangeability
reading; we do not claim dose-response, cross-bed transfer, or isolated
causal attribution to feedback.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Masked diffusion language models assign a multiple-choice answer only after a
scoring protocol turns option-level denoising losses into a ranking.  That
protocol is part of the measurement instrument.  In particular, a scorer may
reveal candidate tokens sequentially and feed the known candidate token back
into the context, or it may score a randomly masked subset without such
feedback.  Even two feedback-based scorers can use different reveal schedules.
Treating their accuracies as if they were readings from the same ruler hides a
change in what was measured.

We report a deliberately narrow audit of that construction.  The evidence is
one LLaDA-8B-Instruct $\times$ GSM8K-MC four-option construction evaluated on
two historical balanced pilot pools, a confirmatory $400$-question pool for
the reference-arm contrast, and a disjoint fresh $819$-question pool for
reveal order.  The study makes three claims, each tied to its pool and
boundary (Table~\ref{tab:gt-result}, Table~\ref{tab:reveal-order-batches}).
\emph{First}, on the tested $n{=}400$ reference-budget curve, \sconf{}'s
advantage over the non-injected reference is positive and significant at every
tested budget and narrows as the reference is given more compute
(Table~\ref{tab:gt-result}, Figure~\ref{fig:decisive-paired}).  The registered
matched-$L$ point remains the primary endpoint and is measured on that bed; the
per-candidate total-NFE-fair equalization at $2L$ (the decisive read, both arms
$2L_i$ forwards) is measured on the same bed and, within this
$n{=}400$ endpoint change, reports the same headline $+13.0\pp$ gap (exact
$p=4.8\!\times\!10^{-5}$ and $6.6\!\times\!10^{-5}$); under the paper's
refusals this \emph{does not} license an estimand-external claim that endpoint
choice is irrelevant in general, only that on this tested bed the swap leaves
the headline unchanged.  The highest-budget read remains the conservative
one.  This \emph{refutes} a less-reference-compute-only account,
not a budget-independent one, and it is a complete protocol swap, not causal
isolation.  \emph{Second}, reveal \emph{order} on
injected prompts, measured against the \emph{tested persisted schedule}, has a
surviving effect on the disjoint fresh pool under its $k{=}1$ multiplicity
family; but a trivial fixed order whose \emph{schedule selection} reads no model
output still beats \sconf{} on that pool on average
(Table~\ref{tab:reveal-order-batches}) --- a margin that strengthens in the
answer-pool construction stratum and \emph{reverses} at answer position 0
(Table~\ref{tab:fresh-strata}).  Confidence ordering is therefore not
necessary, not uniquely optimal, and not exclusively causal; a non-random
structure---including answer format---can suffice.  \emph{Third}, protocols
are not interchangeable: changing the scorer changes per-question option
rankings and choice-set sensitivity on this bed.  The pairwise cross-protocol
Kendall $\tau$ sits only just above the random arm's \emph{own} self-$\tau$
floor, so it bounds protocol-specific disagreement rather than proving
distinctness; the deterministic \sconf{}--\finj{} rank agreement is separately
consistent with genuinely distinct protocols
(Table~\ref{tab:rank-diagnostics}).

The contribution is a protocol audit rather than a new scoring algorithm: a
paired, decision-record-level decomposition of comparisons that would otherwise collapse
model and scorer into one accuracy number, with the negative boundary stated
up front---neither the design nor its measurements support a dose-response
curve, a concavity claim, or transfer to another model, dataset, or judge.

\section{Related Work and Local Evidence Context}
\label{sec:related}

LLaDA introduced the 8B masked-diffusion model used here
\citep{nie2025llada}; iLLaDA later introduced confidence-based
multiple-choice scoring \citep{nie2026illada}.  That scorer reveals the most
confident candidate token and feeds the known token into later steps, so a
comparison with a Monte Carlo-style scorer replaces protocol components as
well as the accounting rule.  We audit that replacement, not the published
benchmark values.  GSM8K \citep{cobbe2021gsm8k} supplies only the controlled
four-choice bed; our claims do not transfer beyond it.

Scorer dependence is established more broadly: benchmarks vary with task and
prompt format \citep{brown2020fewshot,hendrycks2021mmlu,lin2022truthfulqa,chen2021codex};
frameworks distinguish raw from length-normalized scoring and expose prompt
sensitivity \citep{liang2023helm,biderman2024lessons,zhu2024promptrobust}; and
audits cover judge design, contamination, and aggregation
\citep{perez2022modelwritten,jain2024livecodebench,wang2023selfconsistency}.
Our contribution is the measured masked-diffusion protocol swing on a stated
budget curve.

The closest mechanism literatures are mask-out objectives
\citep{nie2025llada,nie2026illada,sahoo2024mdlm}, multiple-choice
normalization and position sensitivity \citep{holtzman2021surface,zheng2024selection},
and confidence-guided diffusion decoding \citep{nie2025llada}.  Our controls
extend these concerns to token injection: a fixed left-to-right schedule can
beat confidence ordering on this answer format, but no cited work or result
here establishes a transferable injected-feedback effect.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Bed and pairing.}
Each item pairs one GSM8K question with four candidate answers scored by
LLaDA-8B-Instruct \citep{nie2025llada} with frozen weights.  Choice sets were
frozen but differ by pool, and the fresh pool is \emph{internally}
heterogeneous by construction: the historical, $n=400$, and first 336 fresh
items use deterministic near-miss distractors; the remaining 483 fresh items
use three distinct values drawn from a frozen GSM8K answer pool.  Every arm
reuses the recorded choice set.  Historical and $n=400$ pools are exactly
answer-position balanced; the fresh pool is approximate.  Fresh-pool contrasts
therefore mix protocol effects with construction stratum, and post-hoc position
standardization leaves the fresh--pilot difference, so we report them as
strata-confounded.  Inference uses BF16, no temperature,
and no generation.  Confidence and fixed schedules are deterministic; the
seeded-random result is one persisted schedule.  Because its runner used an
unpinned Python string hash, replay is anchored to stored per-candidate orders,
not the nominal seed alone.  Appendix~\ref{app:repro} lists identifiers,
prompts, orders, and seeds.  Historical pools use 12 or 16 items per answer
position ($n=48/64$); the $n=400$ and fresh $n=819$ pools are non-overlapping.
Within each pool, arms score the same items, so contrasts are paired by
question.  Top-1 exact correctness is the sole endpoint; no partial credit is
assigned.

\paragraph{Five arms.}
For candidate tokens $y_{1:L_i}$, an injected arm chooses a permutation of
positions, reads cross-entropy before each reveal, inserts the known candidate
token at that position, and repeats until the candidate is visible.  It sums
the per-step cross-entropies and selects the option with minimum score.
\sconf{} chooses the next minimum-cross-entropy position; \rinj{} uses a
deterministically seeded random order; \ainj{} chooses the maximum-cross-entropy
position; and \finj{} uses fixed left-to-right order.  The fifth arm, \mcu{},
draws a masked subset of size $k \sim \mathrm{U}\{1,\dots,L\}$ at each of
$n_{\rm mc}$ draws (LLaDA upper-bound estimator), reads the partial-mask
cross-entropy at the masked positions, and rescales per option by the standard
factor $L_i/k$, so each draw is an unbiased length-corrected estimate of the
full mask-out score and the arm returns their mean; ties break by first
index.  The $n_{\rm mc}{=}1$ single draw is this paper's minimum-budget arm;
it differs operationally from the LLaDA/iLLaDA default confidence scorer,
whose reveal targets the model's own argmax token rather than the injected
ground-truth candidate.  Thus ``protocol family''
denotes the full tuple of feedback rule, reveal schedule, and scoring dose---not
the model name alone.\footnote{The anonymous artifact describes the exact
sampler, prompt, mask construction, random-seed policy, and first-index tie
rule.}  Table~\ref{tab:arms} summarizes these protocol components.

\paragraph{Statistics and derived diagnostics.}
For paired binary correctness indicators, $b$ and $c$ count discordant directions,
$m=b+c$, and $\widehat\Delta=(b-c)/n$.  We report the exact two-sided McNemar
test \citep{mcnemar1947} and the locked 95\% interval.  Paired intervals default
to the registered standard error $\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}=\sqrt{m}/n$; each
question-bootstrap or paired-bootstrap interval is named as such in its own
caption.  An
alternative mean-corrected form moves the reported registered-SE bounds
negligibly on the $n=400$ pool and is convention-sensitive only on the small
pilot pools, changing no verdict anywhere---Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:order-six-ci} prints both conventions side by side.  The
pre-registered interpretation gate requires $m\geq25$ before a paired contrast
is promoted.  For protocol agreement, Kendall
$\tau$ \citep{kendall1938} is recomputed within each item from the two
four-option score vectors after dropping tied pairs, then averaged across
items.  Choice-set sensitivity is an offline deterministic overlay: each of
the three distractors is shrunk by 25\%, and a flip is counted if the selected
option changes (with perturbation counts stated per pool in the appendix).
These are descriptive protocol diagnostics, not hypothesis tests.

\paragraph{Load-bearing estimand.}
The primary announcement is the paired mean accuracy gap
$\Delta=\EE_i[\II(A(q_i){=}y_i){-}\II(B(q_i){=}y_i)]=\Pr(A(q){=}y){-}\Pr(B(q){=}y)$
under nominal draw-count matching: $q_i$ is a fixed item tuple (question,
designated answer, deterministic four-option pool from the same scorer), the
expectation is over the $n$ items of a named pool, and the budget unit is the
nominal per-candidate scoring draw (each arm spends $L_i$ forwards per
candidate; nominal, not total NFE, per Table~\ref{tab:arms}).  Estimation is
the paired $\widehat\Delta=(b-c)/n$ with the exact two-sided McNemar test;
$\Delta$ is a protocol gap, not a mechanism attribution.  Registration kept
the nominal matched-$L$ point ($n_{\rm mc}{=}L$, draw-count only) primary; that
primary point and the per-candidate total-NFE-fair equalization at $2L$ are
both measured on the $n{=}400$ bed, and the reported curve still treats the
$+9.0\pp$ high-budget endpoint as its conservative read
(Table~\ref{tab:gt-result}).

\paragraph{Multiplicity.}
The confirmatory five-arm analysis uses all ten pairwise tests as one Holm
family \citep{holm1979} at $\alpha=0.05$; the smallest-step threshold is $0.005$.  The fresh
fixed-order adjudication has one preregistered load-bearing pair
(\sconf{}--\finj{}, $k=1$).  As a conservative sensitivity, we also name a
five-test reporting family: \sconf{} versus \rinj{}, \ainj{}, \finj{},
single-draw \mcu{}, and draw-count-matched \mcu{}.  Under Holm at
$\alpha=0.05$, the fixed-order result remains significant even under the
worst-case factor-five adjustment ($p=0.0194$).  This named five-test set is a
robustness disclosure, not a retroactive change to the registered $k=1$
endpoint.



\paragraph{Registration and principal-contrast selection.}
The initial registration fixes the bed, five arms, $m{\geq}25$ promotion gate,
and exact paired testing.  Draw-count matching was selected only after the
small-pool scan, so every small-sample row is historical/exploratory.  A
separate confirmatory registration fixed the $n=400$ draw-count-matched
contrast, the ten-test Holm family, and the power stop before data.  Its
planning MDE and its magnitude gate are distinct, unchanged quantities: the
planning MDE sized the $n=400$ pool, while the same $4\pp$ promotion bar --- a
\emph{point-estimate} gate, not an inferential lower-bound gate, so the
high-budget read's lower bound of $3.46\pp$ neither clears $4\pp$ nor needs to
--- applies to the matched-$L$, total-NFE-fair $2L$, and high-budget reads on
that bed, all three of which clear it (Table~\ref{tab:gt-result}).  A
separately pre-registered exploratory $25\pp$ tier applies only to the
historical small rows, not as a stricter second bar for any $n=400$ read; this
unified rule \emph{refutes} the earlier asymmetric thresholding.  The corrected
high-budget $n=400$ run and the anonymous decision chronology are in Appendix
Tables~\ref{tab:estimator-scan} and~\ref{tab:revision-chain}.
\section{The Reference Remains Lower Across the Tested Budget Curve on This Bed}
\label{sec:result1}

The answer-level budget curve joins independently registered $n=400$ runs, with
$n_{\rm mc}{=}128$ as the conservative high-budget main read.  Its decisive row
is the total-NFE-fair $2L$ point, where both arms spend $2L_i$ forwards per
candidate (Table~\ref{tab:gt-result}).  The pre-registered nominal matched-$L$
endpoint instead set $n_{{\rm mc},i}=L_i$, matching only the injected arm's
$L_i$ scoring steps and not its schedule-selection passes, token-forwards, or
total NFE; that draw-count-matched point is separately measured on this bed
(paired $b/c=106/54$), and the $2L/2L$ row returns the same $+13.0\pp$ gap from
a numerically distinct paired record ($b/c=109/57$), so the two endpoints differ
in discordance pattern but not in headline effect.  The candidate-length
distribution is in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:candidate-lengths}.  At
the headline operating point the paired result survives the ten-test Holm
family; Table~\ref{tab:gt-result} and Figure~\ref{fig:decisive-paired} report
the arm accuracies, discordances, estimate, interval, and exact test.

\paragraph{Same-bed likelihood readout.}
For each deterministic injected policy, summed candidate cross-entropy is the
exact negative log-likelihood under that sequential unmasking policy.  Since
selection is its $\arg\min$, score and likelihood rankings coincide \emph{by
construction}; this identity is not an additional comparison.
Table~\ref{tab:samebed-likelihood} shows both ground-truth signal and a policy
shift on the same questions: true-answer readout falls from $1.88$ to $1.21$
nats/token under \sconf{} versus fixed order.  Thus the gap is localized to a
protocol-dependent likelihood readout, without a cross-bed claim.  These are
teacher-forced option likelihoods, not generation; \mcu{} remains a separate
mask-out family whose ELBO-style estimand is not point-comparable to sequential
reveal.

\section{Scoring Protocols Are Not Interchangeable on This Bed}
\label{sec:result2}

The score vectors produced by \sconf{} and \finj{}---two deterministic
protocols, so no schedule-seed noise enters---disagree clearly in rank
($\tau{\approx}0.48$; Table~\ref{tab:rank-diagnostics}), consistent with
genuinely distinct protocols.  Between \sconf{} and the seeded-random \rinj{},
the item-level $\tau$ means sit only $0.01$--$0.10$ above the random arm's
\emph{own} self-$\tau$ floor (Figure~\ref{fig:tau}); because that floor's
dispersion exceeds the gap, we read that value as an \emph{upper bound} on
protocol-specific rank disagreement rather than as evidence of distinctness,
and the deterministic pair carries the load-bearing reading.  Replacing the scoring protocol is accordingly
not a harmless implementation detail, even among protocols that use
ground-truth feedback.

Choice-set perturbation provides a second, operational reading.
Figure~\ref{fig:flips} plots the exact-count rates for both protocols at
$n=64$; the historical $n=48$ panel is sign-consistent and omitted for space.
The between-protocol gap persists in sign across the two samples, although it
narrows in the larger sample.  The 25\% overlay is a design-chosen proportional
shrink of each protocol's own distractor scores, not a scale-invariant or
scale-fair one, so we compare only persistence and sign, never cross-scorer
magnitudes, and no significance or power claim attaches to these deterministic
perturbations.  Together with rank disagreement, they support only a
sign-consistent, bed-local non-interchangeability reading.

\paragraph{Persisted-schedule sensitivity.}  The single pinned seeded-random
schedule calls for a comparability audit against other schedule realizations.
Two \emph{independent events} observed on the same $n{=}819$ pool sharpen the
reading without further decoding.  First, the registered between-pool
heterogeneity check (pilot $n{=}400$ vs.\ fresh $n{=}819$, same \sconf{}
vs.\ \rinj{} contrast) returns $\delta_{\rm diff}=8.83\pp$ with $z=2.41$,
two-sided $p=0.016$; magnitude shifts of at least $\sim8\pp$ therefore arise
on this bed from pool composition alone, with the schedule \emph{pinned}.  The
post-data choice to break the tie by elevation of the fresh endpoint is
disclosed in Table~\ref{tab:endpoint-tree}, and the fresh row carries the
``post-selection secondary'' role in
Table~\ref{tab:reveal-order-batches}.  Second, the order-trace match share
between the persisted schedule and the fixed left-to-right order is
$2.1\%$ ($17/819$; Table~\ref{tab:reveal-order-batches} caption): the
persisted schedule is a single, non-representative schedule realization, not
an average over the seeded-random family.  Under the registered heterogeneity
observation, cross-realization magnitude shifts of magnitude at least
$8\pp$ are already plausible on this bed; the ``post-selection'' label and
the bed-local non-claim that follow are calibrated to that scale.  Multi-seed
replays with pinned, replayable schedule seeds are the follow-up correction
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}).  We do not re-run any scoring pass here; both
events are recorded properties of the existing $n{=}819$ archive.

\paragraph{Pool heterogeneity is disclosed, not merged.}
Figure~\ref{fig:forest} and Table~\ref{tab:reveal-order-batches} retain every
pool.  The fresh comparison is significant under the registered one-sided
$\alpha=.05$ rule, but because it was elevated only after pool heterogeneity was

are positive, and charging two-pool selection still gives a fresh one-sided

multiplicity accounts (Table~\ref{tab:reveal-order-batches}, note b).  This
supports a structured-order boundary, not equivalence or a uniquely
confidence-driven mechanism.

\paragraph{Boundary: what ``reveal order'' attributes here, and what it does not.}
The \sconf{}--\rinj{} ablation covers one persisted random schedule.  A
fixed order that selects its schedule without model output beats \sconf{} on
the fresh pool \emph{on average}; within that pool the margin is
construction-dependent in strength and reverses at answer position 0
(Table~\ref{tab:fresh-strata}).  The pooled-fresh sign repeats at $n{=}400$ but
reverses on historical $n{=}64$ (Figure~\ref{fig:accuracy}).  Because no fixed-order cross-pool test supports
merging and Table~\ref{tab:endpoint-tree}'s heterogeneity concerns \rinj{}, the
former cross-pool mechanism reading is \emph{refuted}.  Confidence is neither
necessary, uniquely optimal, nor exclusively causal; all six $n{=}400$ order
contrasts remain Holm-nonsignificant (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:order-six-ci}).
The cleanest same-bed zero-effect row is \sconf{} versus \ainj{}
($+2.00\pp$, exact $p{=}0.573$; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:order-six-ci}): reveal
direction alone carries neither the reference gap nor the schedule result.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Single bed and non-factorial mechanism.}
One checkpoint and one four-choice GSM8K bed support no cross-model, task, or
judge prediction.  The \sconf{}--\mcu{} replacement changes feedback, path,
and passes jointly, so refuting the narrow compute-artifact account does not
factorize it: the reference spends less at one draw but far more at the
high-budget read, and the gap survives both there and at measured exact $2L$
(Table~\ref{tab:gt-result}).

\paragraph{Headline robustness gates, reliability, and seed variation.}
The frozen rerun, hostile re-adjudication boundary, three-bucket rule, and
leave-one-out influence are consolidated in Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:robustness-gates}: the rerun stays inside its abort tolerance,
leave-one-out is a nondegenerate PASS, and the adverse-change counts delimit
where significance and direction would fail.  None of these supplies the
missing multi-seed evidence, and the non-scale-invariant shrink supports only
sign and persistence: multi-seed accuracy and $\tau$ require new decoding, and
the 48-item self-$\tau$ is only a noise floor.

\paragraph{Excluded claims and external validity.}
The rank-half design supports neither dose response nor concavity, and no
cross-bed prediction band is used.  Under its pre-registered joint
zero-and-relevance gate, the five-choice probe remains \emph{UNDETERMINED}:
neither supporting evidence nor evidence of absence, and its significant point
estimate is not upgraded to external-validity evidence anywhere in this paper
(Appendix Table~\ref{tab:5option}).  Length-normalized likelihood scorers
(acc\_norm/NLL) were also not measured; this is a scope limit, not an
equivalence or gap.  Table~\ref{tab:samebed-likelihood} localizes only the
injected-policy readout.

\paragraph{Efficiency and matched-$L$ boundaries.}
The scan and forward ledger (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:compute-ledger}) cover
answer scoring, not generation.  The tested
curve, including exact $2L$, refutes the narrow artifact but leaves off-curve
budget independence open (Table~\ref{tab:gt-result}).  Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:nfe-sensitivity} is instead a non-accuracy length identity and
no substitute for an accuracy measurement: its nominal all-candidate
substitution is a lower audit counterfactual, not input.  The three promoted
$n{=}400$ reads all clear the shared $4\pp$ bar, but the historical high-budget
$n=64$ row remains underpowered (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:compute-contrast}).

\section{Conclusion}
On this bed, confidence injection beats the tested non-injected reference,
while fixed order wins on average but reverses at position 0
(Table~\ref{tab:fresh-strata}).  Exact $2L$ refutes only a
less-reference-compute account; off-curve, multi-seed, and generalization
claims remain unresolved (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).
