\begin{abstract}
A score recorded when a masked-diffusion decoder commits a token and the
correctness of its final answer are measurements at different levels.  We audit the
evidence and estimand behind their cross-level mean difference in two source-reported
records.  One record gives a natural-strata difference of $+0.2227$; another, on a
different reported $400$-item pool, gives a paired $+37$-token,
fixed-$\mathrm{NFE}{=}64$ filler contrast of $-0.0075$.  These point values concern
different samples and estimands, and no joint comparison is defined or tested.  Their
juxtaposition therefore has no shared inferential interpretation.  We independently check only
the algebraic estimand definitions and arithmetic printed from the transcribed values.
The empirical measurements themselves are source-reported and cannot be independently
verified because the current package lacks the item records, exact intervention,
decode traces, and analysis implementation.  The paired estimand is consequently a
descriptive finite-pool quantity conditional on one recorded decode realization, not
expected decoder behavior.  The commit score is also not verified as a probability of
final-answer correctness.  No experiment was run as part of this audit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Prompt length can covary with task content, formatting, and difficulty.  A natural
length association therefore does not identify the effect of a prompt modification.
This distinction is especially important for masked-diffusion decoding, where
prompt construction, the unmasking trajectory, and a fixed inference budget can interact.
We examine one source-described GSM8K \citep{cobbe2021gsm8k} /
LLaDA-8B-Instruct \citep{nie2025large} record that places two analyses side by side:
an independent natural-strata analysis and a paired fixed-filler analysis.

The central quantity is a \emph{cross-level mean score--outcome difference}: the mean
of a decoder's recorded token-commit score minus mean final-answer correctness.  The
source artifacts call this quantity SCE; we retain that string only as an internal
artifact alias.  We do not call the score confidence or an answer probability because
the package contains neither the token-to-answer aggregation nor construct-validation
evidence.  The quantity remains arithmetically well defined as a directional summary,
but its zero point is not a calibration certificate
\citep{ao2023miscalibration,seligmann2023beyond,cruz2024riskscores}.

This paper is an evidence and estimand audit.  It asks what the source-described paired
summary estimates, which statements can be checked from the current package, and which
empirical conclusions remain unsupported.  The natural-strata and fixed-filler point
values are catalogued as distinct source records; neither is used to decide what the
other means.  The paper contributes:

\begin{enumerate}
\item a finite-pool estimand conditional on the recorded single-decode realization;
\item a separation between independently checkable definitions and printed arithmetic,
on the one hand, and empirical measurements that can only be transcribed, on the other;
and
\item a package inventory that makes the unavailable treatment, sample, score, decode,
and implementation evidence explicit.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Measurement status.}
Throughout, \srcv{} means \emph{source-reported and not independently verifiable from
the current package}.  Every empirical numerical value, count, and dose in the
manuscript is \srcv{}.  This global label also applies when space prevents repetition
in individual cells.  Evidence IDs A32-E1 and A32-E2 are opaque references to missing

\paragraph{What is missing.}
The authoring package does not contain the literal filler string, insertion location,
tokenizer-level dose check, immutable $400$-item manifest or selection rule, overlap
map with the natural-strata sample, exact prompt serialization, arm execution order,
token-to-answer score construction, or gate implementation.  It also lacks per-item
records, decode traces, preregistration, analysis code, model/tokenizer revision,
environment lock, and a claim-to-file crosswalk.  Consequently, the treatment,
sampling frame, score construct, and empirical measurements cannot be independently
reconstructed.  No interval, probability-calibration, causal-effect, or
decoder-expectation claim is retained.

\section{Finite-pool recorded-realization estimand}
\label{sec:estimand}

Let $\mathcal P$ denote the source-described finite paired pool and
$a\in\{S,L\}$ the Short and Long arms.  Let $\omega$ denote the complete recorded
single-decode realization, including every stochastic choice used to produce the stored
arm outputs.  For item $i$, let $s_{ia\omega}$ be the source-recorded commit score and
$y_{ia\omega}\in\{0,1\}$ final-answer correctness under the source's matcher.  Define
the arm summary
\[
 \crossdiff_{\mathcal P,\omega}(a)
 = \frac{1}{|\mathcal P|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal P}
   \left(s_{ia\omega}-y_{ia\omega}\right)
\]
and the paired filler contrast
\[
 \Delta_{\mathcal P,\omega}
 = \crossdiff_{\mathcal P,\omega}(L)-\crossdiff_{\mathcal P,\omega}(S)
 = \frac{1}{|\mathcal P|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal P}
 \left[(s_{iL\omega}-y_{iL\omega})-(s_{iS\omega}-y_{iS\omega})\right].
 \label{eq:recorded-estimand}
\]
This is a descriptive contrast for the named finite pool and stored decode realization.
It is not an expectation over a problem population, decoder randomness, filler strings,
or prompt constructions.  A decoder-expectation estimand would require deterministic
replay evidence or multiple paired decode realizations; neither is available.

Short and Long are labels attached to the source summaries.  Without the literal
prompts, assignment record, pairing rows, and execution trace, this audit cannot verify
arm construction, intervention fidelity, or execution order.

The independent natural-strata analysis instead compares high- and low-length groups
formed from a different source-reported record.  Natural prompt content and difficulty
co-vary with those groups.  Because the package lacks item identities and an overlap
map, no common-sample contrast, covariance-aware difference, prespecified comparison
functional, or joint test can be formed.

The source separately reports the paired-filler point $-0.0075$ and the natural-strata
point $+0.2227$.  We do not compute a cross-record difference, ratio, or ordering from
them.  They concern different item sets and estimands, and the package
provides neither a common-sample functional nor a joint analysis.  Their juxtaposition
is an index of two source records, not an empirical comparison.

The source-displayed score arm means have three decimal places, whereas the separately
reported score contrast has four.  Without unrounded records, subtraction of the
printed means need not recover that contrast.  This is unresolved rounding opacity,
not evidence of a numerical inconsistency.  The current package supports only an audit
of the formal target and evidence boundary; it cannot establish intervention fidelity
or explain the natural length association.

\section{Closest neighboring work and boundary}
\label{sec:related}

Prior work establishes that prompt choices can change calibration-related behavior, but
the verified neighboring studies do not test this exact operation.  Xia et al.
\citep{xia2025influences} study calibration across four prompt styles; their analysis
does not isolate prompt length, content-free filler, masked-diffusion commit scores, or
a same-item $+37$-token intervention.  Zhao et al. \citep{zhao2024far} study six
prompting strategies and propose a confidence-calibration prompting method; the
prompting strategy and elicited confidence change, rather than the source-described
fixed filler under fixed diffusion compute.  Kamoda et al.
\citep{kamoda2023tta} show that prompt variations affect factual-probing outputs and
evaluate prompt augmentation as a calibration intervention; they use prompt variants
and ensembling for factual probing, not the present fixed-NFE masked-diffusion filler
estimand.

For measurement context, signed mean score--label summaries and their cancellation
limits predate this study \citep{ao2023miscalibration,seligmann2023beyond,cruz2024riskscores}.
The present contribution boundary is therefore an evidence and estimand audit of one
source-described record, not a new metric, a general theory of prompt length, or a
verified answer-calibration result.  A broader prompt-length-specific and
masked-diffusion uncertainty search remains incomplete.

\section{Limitations, availability, and disclosure}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Scientific limits.}
The record covers one reported model--task setting, one fixed filler description, one
dose, one inference budget, and one stored decode per item--arm.  The exact sample and
intervention are missing.  The observational and paired analyses use different targets;
the score construct is not verified as an answer probability; and decoder-run
variation is unavailable.  These limits preclude causal, decoder-average, cross-model,
cross-task, or transport conclusions.

\paragraph{What the current package contains.}
The mutable authoring directory contains \texttt{main.tex}, \texttt{refs.bib}, the ICLR
style files, and compiled manuscript and auxiliary files.  It contains no figure asset,
and this manuscript includes no figure.  The directory does not contain experimental
records A32-E1 or A32-E2, literal prompts, item manifests, preregistration, protocol or
analysis code, model/tokenizer revisions, decode configuration, an environment lock, or
an evidence-ID crosswalk.  No empirical value was rerun or independently recomputed.

\paragraph{AI assistance disclosure.}
AI tools assisted with language editing, \LaTeX{} restructuring, and isolated internal
pre-submission checks.  The authors remain responsible for every claim and evidence
boundary.  AI assistance did not supply experimental records, generate new
measurements, verify the source-reported values, or create scientific figures.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This audit formalizes a descriptive finite-pool, recorded-single-decode estimand for the
source-reported filler point $-0.0075$.  A separate natural-strata record reports
$+0.2227$.  The samples and estimands differ, and no joint analysis is available, so
the two point values support no cross-record inference.  The estimand definitions and
printed decomposition arithmetic can be checked independently; the empirical inputs
can only be transcribed.  Missing records, intervention and sample construction, score
semantics, and decoder-replay evidence prevent broader interpretation.
